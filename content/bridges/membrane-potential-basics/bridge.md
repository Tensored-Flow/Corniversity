## Why the inside of a neuron is negative

A neuron is bounded by a lipid membrane that ions cannot cross on their own. Two facts set up everything else.

1. **Concentration gradients.** Ion pumps (chiefly the Na⁺/K⁺-ATPase, which moves three Na⁺ out and two K⁺ in for each ATP) keep potassium concentrated inside the cell and sodium concentrated outside. Typical mammalian values are about 140 mM K⁺ inside against 5 mM outside, and about 12 mM Na⁺ inside against 145 mM outside. These are representative figures, not constants.
2. **Selective permeability.** At rest the membrane is far more permeable to K⁺ than to Na⁺, because of potassium "leak" channels that are open most of the time.

Potassium therefore diffuses out, down its concentration gradient. Each K⁺ that leaves carries a positive charge with it, so the inside becomes negative relative to the outside, and that voltage pulls K⁺ back in. Flow stops when the electrical pull balances the chemical push. The voltage at which this happens for a single ion is its **equilibrium (Nernst) potential**:

$$E_{\text{ion}} = \frac{RT}{zF}\ln\frac{[\text{ion}]_{\text{out}}}{[\text{ion}]_{\text{in}}}.$$

At body temperature $RT/F \approx 26.7$ mV, so for a monovalent ion $E \approx 61.5\text{ mV}\times\log_{10}([\text{out}]/[\text{in}])$.

**Worked example.** With the figures above,
$E_K \approx 61.5 \times \log_{10}(5/140) \approx -89$ mV and $E_{Na} \approx 61.5 \times \log_{10}(145/12) \approx +67$ mV.

The real **resting potential**, about −65 to −70 mV, lies between these, much closer to $E_K$, because the membrane is mostly permeable to K⁺ with a small Na⁺ leak. (The Goldman–Hodgkin–Katz equation makes the weighting by permeabilities precise.) Remarkably few ions need to move to set this up: the membrane is so thin that a tiny separation of charge produces tens of millivolts.

## The membrane as a circuit

That last remark is the key to the model. Electrically, the membrane behaves like:

- a **capacitor** $C$ (the insulating lipid bilayer separating two conductive solutions; about 1 µF per cm² of membrane), in parallel with
- a **leak conductance** $g_L = 1/R$ (the open leak channels), in series with
- a **battery** $E_L$, the voltage at which the net leak current is zero.

Current conservation then says that any injected current $I$ either charges the capacitor or leaks out:

$$C\frac{dV}{dt} = -g_L\,(V - E_L) + I.$$

Multiply through by $R = 1/g_L$ and write $\tau_m = RC$:

$$\tau_m\frac{dV}{dt} = -(V - E_L) + RI.$$

This is exactly the leaky integrate-and-fire equation of NEU-220 unit 1. Every symbol now has a physical meaning:

| Symbol | What it is in the cell |
|---|---|
| $V$ | the membrane potential, inside minus outside |
| $E_L$ | the leak reversal potential, set by the ion gradients and leak permeabilities (close to, but not equal to, $E_K$) |
| $R$ | the membrane resistance: how hard it is for current to leak out |
| $C$ | the membrane capacitance: how much charge it takes to change $V$ |
| $\tau_m = RC$ | the membrane time constant: how quickly $V$ forgets its past |

**Worked example.** A specific membrane resistance of 20 kΩ·cm² and specific capacitance of 1 µF/cm² give $\tau_m = 20$ ms, independent of the cell's area (both $R$ and $C$ scale with area, in opposite directions).

## What integrate-and-fire keeps, and what it throws away

A real spike is generated by **voltage-gated** sodium and potassium channels: past a threshold, Na⁺ channels open, Na⁺ rushes in and drives $V$ towards $E_{Na}$, then Na⁺ channels inactivate and K⁺ channels open, bringing $V$ back down. The integrate-and-fire model keeps only the passive part described above, the capacitor charging through a leak, and replaces the whole spike with a rule: when $V$ reaches $V_{th}$, record a spike and reset $V$. That is why the unit calls the spike "a bookkeeping event": the model says when a spike happens, not how.

So when the unit asks what the model omits, the biology gives concrete answers: the voltage-gated currents that shape the spike, the dependence of the threshold on recent history, and the fact that $g_L$ and $E_L$ are themselves summaries of many channel types.
