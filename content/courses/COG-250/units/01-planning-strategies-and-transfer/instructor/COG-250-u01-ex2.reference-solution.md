## Reference reasoning (instructor only)

Values come from the planning laboratory at its default settings and seed 1. The settings are $\alpha=0.5$, $\varepsilon=0.1$, 60 training episodes, 12 test episodes and a step cap of 300. The values were recomputed with `packages/core/src/labs/planning.ts` on 2026-10-31. The planner is deterministic. The cached policy's numbers change with the seed but keep their pattern. Every value below is printed in the laboratory's table of episodes.

| | Block | Revaluation | Shortcut |
|---|---|---|---|
| Shortest path before → after | 8 → 16 | 8 → 8 (to the new goal) | 16 → 8 |
| Planner, first move after the change | right | **left** | left |
| Cached policy, first move | right | **right** | left |
| Planner, moves in test episodes 1, 2, 3, … | **25** (1 bump), then 16 throughout | 8 throughout | 16 throughout |
| Cached policy, moves (bumps) in test episodes 1–3 | **187** (93), 45 (15), 38 (14) | **245** (101), 79 (31), 92 (38) | 18 (0), 20 (2), 71 (29) |
| Mean gap, cached minus planner, first $k$ episodes ($k=1,2,3,12$) | 162, 95.5, 71, 42.5 | 237, 154, 130.7, 57.8 | 2, 3, 20.3, 7.8 |

**Which observations discriminate.**

- **Revaluation.** The first move discriminates, left against right. That holds only when the cached policy's training has converged: at $\alpha=0.1$ with 60 training episodes, its values at the start are nearly tied and its first move is close to random.
- **Block.** The first encounter discriminates: one bump and immediate replanning, against dozens of bumps while the values relearn. So does the route on the next episode: 16 moves against 45 here.
- **Shortcut.** No clean observation discriminates.
  - The planner never takes the 8-move route, because it cannot learn that a wall has gone.
  - The cached policy reaches the opening only by exploration. Once through, the cells it has never visited still have their starting values of 0, which draw its greedy moves in.
  - At seed 1 its third episode, 71 moves with 29 bumps, runs up the right-hand corridor, through the opened door and into (2,8). 23 of its 29 bumps are at the door or next to it, 15 of them in (2,8).
  - So the gap here does come from how the cached policy meets the opening, but through exploration and untried values, not planning. Within 12 episodes it never takes the shortest route.

**How the averaged gap changes.**

- **Block and revaluation.** The cached policy relearns from each episode, so its excess over the planner falls. A mean over twelve episodes is about a quarter of the first-episode gap: 42.5 against 162 for the block, 57.8 against 237 for revaluation.
- **Shortcut.** At seed 1 the gap rises from 2 to 20.3 over three episodes before falling to 7.8, because one exploratory episode dominates.

Either way, averaging mixes the discriminating first episode with later ones.

**A discriminating variant of the shortcut condition.** Announce the opening to the planner so that it updates its map, as revaluation announces the goal. The planner would then take the 8-move route at once, while the cached policy, with no experience of the opening, would not. This is the logic of revaluation applied to a change of transitions.

An alternative is to give the cached policy an exploration bonus for long-untried moves, Dyna-Q+'s idea. That tests a third strategy rather than separating these two; a learner who notices the difference deserves credit.
