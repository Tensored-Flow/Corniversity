## Reference reasoning (instructor only)

Cells are written (row, column), counting from 0 at the top left. `S` is (4,4), `G` is (0,8), and the door is (2,7).

**1. Shortest paths.**

| Layout | Route | Moves |
|---|---|---|
| (a) Base | right to (4,7), up through the door to (0,7), right to (0,8): $3+4+1$ | 8 |
| (b) After the block | left to (4,0), up to (0,0), right to (0,8): $4+4+8$ | 16 |
| (c) Shortcut, before | the door is a wall and (1,8) is a wall, so the right corridor is a dead end; the left route | 16 |
| (d) Shortcut, after | the door opens and the right route is available again | 8 |
| (e) Revaluation, new goal (0,0) | left 4, up 4; the right route would be $3+4+7=14$ | 8 |

**2. The planner after the block.** Its map still shows the door open, so it plans the short route. It moves right three times and up once, reaching (3,7) after 4 moves. Its fifth move, up into the door, bumps, and it adds (2,7) to its map. From (3,7) it replans:

- down to (4,7): move 6;
- left seven times to (4,0): move 13;
- up four times to (0,0): move 17;
- right eight times to (0,8): move 25.

The episode takes **25 moves, one of them a bump**. On the next episode its map is right, so it takes the long route at once: 16 moves, no bumps. The laboratory reproduces this at every seed, because the planner is deterministic.

**3. The cached policy at (3,7).** While up is the greedy move, each bump leaves the agent at (3,7), so the target is $-1+\max_{a'}Q((3,7),a')=-1+Q(\text{up})$. The update is then

$$Q(\text{up})\leftarrow Q(\text{up})+\alpha\big(-1+Q(\text{up})-Q(\text{up})\big)=Q(\text{up})-\alpha.$$

Each bump lowers $Q(\text{up})$ by exactly $\alpha$, so after $k$ bumps $Q(\text{up})=-4-k\alpha$.

- **$\alpha=0.5$.** After 4 bumps $Q(\text{up})=-6=Q(\text{down})$. Ties go to up, so the agent bumps a fifth time and $Q(\text{up})=-6.5<-6$. It first chooses down **after 5 bumps**.
- **$\alpha=0.1$.** $Q(\text{up})=-6$ after 20 bumps, which is again a tie that up wins. A 21st bump gives $-6.1$, so it switches **after 21 bumps**.

In general the count is the smallest $k$ with $-4-k\alpha<-6$, that is $k>2/\alpha$.

**4. Revaluation.** The planner's first move is **left**, towards the announced goal at (0,0). The cached policy's greedy first move is still **right**, because its values were learned towards the old goal and nothing it has experienced has changed them.

With $\varepsilon=0.1$, the cached policy explores on its first move with probability $0.1$, choosing each of the four moves with probability $\frac14$. So

$$P(\text{left})=0.1\times\tfrac14=0.025,\qquad P(\text{right})=0.9+0.025=0.925.$$

A single first move therefore discriminates well but not perfectly. Over several episodes or several agents the evidence accumulates. It is still the first decision in each episode, before new experience, that carries it.
