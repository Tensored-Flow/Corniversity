## Grading notes (instructor only)

The constructed report in the prompt makes three mistakes on purpose. A complete answer finds all three.

1. **An average over 20 episodes after the closure.** It dilutes the first-encounter difference and mixes it with relearning, so a model-free learner that relearns quickly could match it.
2. **"Fewer moves than a Q-learning model fitted to their training data".** The comparison is held out in time, which is right, but it is not a test of strategy.
   - The training episodes come from an unchanging maze, so they barely constrain how fast a model-free learner relearns after a change. A Q-learner with another learning rate or exploration rate could match the participants.
   - No planner's predictions were set beside it.
   - One fitted Q-learner does not stand for all model-free learners.
3. **"Therefore the participants planned".** This moves from a result to a mechanism without an observation the alternative could not produce.

Do not mark an answer down for reading the comparison as held out in time, because it is. An answer that calls it in-sample has misread the report. Say so in feedback, and credit only the parts of its argument that survive.

**What the answer should propose.** Any of these:

- the first move after an announced revaluation, given converged training;
- the bumps on the first encounter with the block, and the route on the next episode;
- a two-step design in the manner of Daw et al. (2011), where the stay-or-switch decision after a rare rewarded transition separates the strategies on every trial.

**Contract.** If the learner proposes a predictive comparison, such as fitting both strategies to participants and comparing held-out choices, the ten-point contract must come before any claim. It must name the unseen-person or later-episode split and the baseline (a biased random mover, or the shortest-path length). A proposal without the contract caps that criterion at partially met.

**The invented report.** It is labelled as constructed for the exercise. An answer that treats it as a real study, or invents a citation for it, has the first substantive gap to raise in feedback.
