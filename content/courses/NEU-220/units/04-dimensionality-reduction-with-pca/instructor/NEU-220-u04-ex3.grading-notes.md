## Grading notes (instructor only)

This is an explanation exercise; grade the correctness of the method claims and the honesty about data origin.

**Expected content.**
- Case 1 (synthetic Gaussian, Tutorials 1–2): PCA recovers exactly the covariance that was built in; linearity and Gaussianity hold by construction; it checks the method and is not a finding about neurons. t-SNE on a single Gaussian cloud produces structure-free blobs whose apparent clusters, if any, are artefacts of perplexity.
- Case 2 (MNIST, Tutorials 3–4): images with known labels, not neural data; the scree plot describes pixel-space spread; reconstructions sharpen with $K$; two-dimensional PCA overlaps classes while t-SNE separates them by preserving local neighbourhoods at the chosen perplexity; inter-island distances and island sizes are not meaningful and change with perplexity and seed.
- Case 3 (hypothetical recording): Poisson-like variance makes high-rate neurons dominate an unscaled covariance; non-stationarity and trial-to-trial variability mix with stimulus variance; single-trial versus trial-averaged analysis must be chosen and stated; the task variable may sit in a low-variance component; naming structure "neural" requires stating preprocessing (centring, scaling, averaging, binning) and ideally a target-variable check. The automatic checks require the t-SNE caveat and the MNIST-is-images point.
- Explicit statement that only case 3 is neural and that it is hypothetical here.

**Feedback.** Under 150 words. The common gap is treating MNIST separation as evidence about neural representations; the second is reading t-SNE distances. Quote the sentence.

**Confidence.** High when the three cases are distinguished and both caveats appear; route to `needs_review` if the learner cites specific published population analyses to argue a point, which may be correct but is beyond the unit's evidence.
