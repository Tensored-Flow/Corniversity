## Reference reasoning (instructor only)

**1. Variance of a projection.** The columns of $\mathbf{X}$ have mean zero, so the projections $\mathbf{X}\mathbf{w}$ have mean $\mathbf{1}^\top\mathbf{X}\mathbf{w}/N = 0$ and sample variance $\|\mathbf{X}\mathbf{w}\|^2/N = \mathbf{w}^\top(\mathbf{X}^\top\mathbf{X}/N)\mathbf{w} = \mathbf{w}^\top\boldsymbol\Sigma\mathbf{w}$.

**2. Maximisation.** $\boldsymbol\Sigma$ is symmetric and positive semidefinite ($\mathbf{w}^\top\boldsymbol\Sigma\mathbf{w} = \|\mathbf{X}\mathbf{w}\|^2/N \ge 0$). Lagrangian route: $L = \mathbf{w}^\top\boldsymbol\Sigma\mathbf{w} - \lambda(\mathbf{w}^\top\mathbf{w} - 1)$, $\nabla_{\mathbf{w}}L = 2\boldsymbol\Sigma\mathbf{w} - 2\lambda\mathbf{w} = 0$, so stationary points are unit eigenvectors and the objective there is $\mathbf{w}^\top\boldsymbol\Sigma\mathbf{w} = \lambda$; the maximum over the compact unit sphere exists and is attained at a stationary point, hence at the eigenvector of the largest eigenvalue $\lambda_1$, with maximal variance $\lambda_1$. Rayleigh route: by the spectral theorem (symmetry) there is an orthonormal eigenbasis $\{\mathbf{w}_k\}$ with $\boldsymbol\Sigma\mathbf{w}_k = \lambda_k\mathbf{w}_k$, $\lambda_1 \ge \dots \ge \lambda_D \ge 0$ (PSD); writing $\mathbf{w} = \sum c_k\mathbf{w}_k$ with $\sum c_k^2 = 1$ gives $\mathbf{w}^\top\boldsymbol\Sigma\mathbf{w} = \sum\lambda_kc_k^2 \le \lambda_1$, with equality iff $\mathbf{w}$ lies in the $\lambda_1$ eigenspace.

**3. Uncorrelated scores.** $\text{Cov}(\mathbf{X}\mathbf{w}_i, \mathbf{X}\mathbf{w}_j) = (\mathbf{X}\mathbf{w}_i)^\top(\mathbf{X}\mathbf{w}_j)/N = \mathbf{w}_i^\top\boldsymbol\Sigma\mathbf{w}_j = \lambda_j\mathbf{w}_i^\top\mathbf{w}_j = 0$, since eigenvectors of a symmetric matrix for distinct eigenvalues are orthogonal ($\lambda_i\mathbf{w}_i^\top\mathbf{w}_j = \mathbf{w}_i^\top\boldsymbol\Sigma\mathbf{w}_j = \lambda_j\mathbf{w}_i^\top\mathbf{w}_j$ forces $\mathbf{w}_i^\top\mathbf{w}_j = 0$). For the second component, maximise $\mathbf{w}^\top\boldsymbol\Sigma\mathbf{w}$ over unit vectors orthogonal to $\mathbf{w}_1$; in the eigenbasis this is $\sum_{k \ge 2}\lambda_kc_k^2 \le \lambda_2$, attained at $\mathbf{w}_2$.

**4. Computation.** $\det(\boldsymbol\Sigma - \lambda I) = (3 - \lambda)^2 - 1 = 0 \Rightarrow \lambda = 4, 2$. For $\lambda = 4$: $(3 - 4)w_1 + w_2 = 0 \Rightarrow \mathbf{w}_1 = (1, 1)/\sqrt2$. For $\lambda = 2$: $\mathbf{w}_2 = (1, -1)/\sqrt2$. Total variance $\text{tr}\,\boldsymbol\Sigma = 6$; PC1 explains $4/6 = 2/3 \approx 66.7\%$.

**5. Scaling and centring.** If variable $d$ is multiplied by $c$, row and column $d$ of $\boldsymbol\Sigma$ scale by $c$ and the diagonal entry by $c^2$; eigenvalues and eigenvectors change (the $2 \times 2$ example with the first variable doubled has $\boldsymbol\Sigma = \begin{pmatrix}12 & 2\\ 2 & 3\end{pmatrix}$ and a first component tilted towards the first axis). PCA is not scale-invariant; standardising is a choice. Without centring, $\mathbf{w}^\top\mathbf{X}^\top\mathbf{X}\mathbf{w}/N$ is the mean of squared projections (a second moment), not a variance, and its maximiser points towards the data mean.

**Alternative valid approaches.** SVD of $\mathbf{X}$ ($\mathbf{X} = \mathbf{U}\mathbf{S}\mathbf{V}^\top$, components are the right singular vectors, eigenvalues $s_k^2/N$); the Courant–Fischer characterisation. Accept 66.7% or 2/3.

**Common gaps.** Asserting "eigenvectors maximise variance" without the constraint or the spectral theorem; forgetting PSD when claiming eigenvalues are nonnegative; computing the share as $4/(4 + 2 + 3 + 3)$ or similar; claiming PCA is scale-free.
