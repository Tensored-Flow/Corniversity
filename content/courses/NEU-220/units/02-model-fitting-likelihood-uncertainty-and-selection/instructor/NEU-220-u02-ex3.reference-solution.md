## Reference reasoning (instructor only)

The laboratory's numbers vary with its random seed, so this reference gives the derivations, the expected qualitative results and the structure of a good report; the grading notes say how to check the learner's reported numbers.

**1. Likelihood and baseline.** For counts $n_i$ in windows of length $T$ at orientations $\theta_i$ with rate function $\lambda(\cdot)$, independence and the Poisson pmf give
$$\ell = \sum_i\big[n_i\ln(\lambda(\theta_i)T) - \lambda(\theta_i)T - \ln n_i!\big].$$
Constant rate $\lambda = b$: $\ell(b) = \ln(bT)\sum n_i - NbT - \sum\ln n_i!$; $d\ell/db = \sum n_i/b - NT = 0 \Rightarrow \hat b = \bar n/T$; $d^2\ell/db^2 = -\sum n_i/b^2 < 0$, a maximum. The baseline is the hypothesis that the stimulus variable does not affect the rate; the tuning model has four parameters, so beating a one-parameter baseline *out of sample* is the minimum evidence that those parameters carry information rather than fit noise.

**2. Fit and comparison.** The tuning model is fitted by numerical maximisation of $\ell$ on the training trials (with $\kappa \ge 0$, $A \ge 0$, $b > 0$). Mean held-out log-likelihood per trial, $\ell_{\text{test}}/N_{\text{test}}$, for both models; the difference is the evidence. With the laboratory's default generating parameters the tuning model should win clearly (a margin of order $0.5$–$1$ nats per trial) and the margin should be stable across bootstrap splits. Training log-likelihood is insufficient because the four-parameter model can only improve it; it would "win" even on data generated by the baseline.

**3. Parameters and uncertainty.** $\theta_{\text{pref}}$: the orientation of peak rate. $\kappa$: concentration, larger means narrower tuning; $\kappa = 0$ is flat (and then $\theta_{\text{pref}}$ is unidentifiable, which a wide bootstrap interval for $\theta_{\text{pref}}$ reveals). $A$: modulation depth, peak rate minus baseline scales with $A(e^{\kappa} - e^{-\kappa})$ in this parametrisation. $b$: baseline rate, the floor. Bootstrap by resampling trials within each orientation (stratified), refitting. With eight orientations at $22.5^\circ$ spacing, $\kappa$ is typically the least certain because the peak is sampled by at most two or three points; $\theta_{\text{pref}}$ is uncertain when the true peak falls between sampled orientations.

**4. Failure case.** The second dataset is generated with two preferred orientations $90^\circ$ apart (or with overdispersed counts; the laboratory says which it used). For bimodality: the unimodal fit either sits on one peak with a modest $\kappa$ or spreads between them with $\kappa \approx 0$; the held-out margin over the baseline shrinks; residuals (observed minus predicted mean count) plotted against orientation show a positive lobe at the second peak. The unrepresentable feature is a second mode: the von Mises form has one peak per period. For overdispersion: the mean curve may be fitted well but the Poisson likelihood overstates the evidence and bootstrap intervals are too narrow; the diagnostic is the variance-to-mean ratio of counts within each orientation exceeding 1.

**5. Scope (model answer, 95 words).** The exercise shows how maximum-likelihood tuning-curve fitting, baseline comparison, bootstrap uncertainty and failure diagnosis behave on synthetic data whose generating process is known. It shows nothing about real cortical neurons: no recording was used, and the generating model was chosen to be fittable. On recorded data the Poisson assumption (variance equal to mean) would have to be checked first, since cortical counts are often overdispersed; stationarity across trials, independence of trials and the single-peak assumption would also need checking before this model's parameters could be read as properties of a neuron.

**Alternative valid approaches.** Gaussian tuning curve instead of von Mises if the learner justifies it for a periodic variable (it is not periodic; note the issue). Deviance instead of log-likelihood difference. Cross-validation instead of a single held-out split (preferable; welcome it).
