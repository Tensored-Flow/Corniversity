## Grading notes (instructor only)

This is the course's laboratory report (assessment a2) and the brief's computational-neuroscience acceptance test: a model-fitting activity on explicitly labelled synthetic data with assumptions examined, a baseline comparison, parameter interpretation and a failure case.

- **Automatic checks** require the word synthetic (or simulated/generated) wherever data are mentioned, an out-of-sample term, a baseline term, and the absence of recording language. They are advisory. A report that says "synthetic" once at the top and then writes "the neuron prefers 45°" ten times passes the check and fails the intent; read for that.
- **Numbers.** The laboratory's values depend on its seed. Check internal consistency rather than matching a key: the baseline $\hat b$ must equal the training mean count divided by $T$; held-out log-likelihood per trial must be negative and the tuning model's must exceed the baseline's on the default dataset; bootstrap intervals must contain the point estimates; the failure-case margin must be smaller than the default-case margin. If the learner pastes numbers that violate these, quote the inconsistency as the first substantive gap.
- **Non-negotiables for acceptance:** Poisson log-likelihood correct; $\hat b = \bar n/T$ derived; an out-of-sample comparison against the baseline with the margin reported; at least two bootstrap intervals; the failure dataset run with the diagnostic named; the scope paragraph with an explicit "shows nothing about biological neurons" statement and one assumption to check.
- **Honesty.** Any sentence that attributes a property to a real neuron, animal or brain area is the first substantive gap, however good the rest. The tutor persona's habits apply: synthetic labelled every time; out-of-sample check for every claim; data leakage and seed policy checked before believing a number (ask whether the held-out trials were touched during fitting).
- **Alternate approaches:** cross-validation instead of a single split is better and should be welcomed; a Gaussian tuning curve is acceptable if the learner notes it is not periodic; deviance or AIC as the comparison statistic is fine if the held-out principle is kept.
- **Confidence:** high when all five sections are present and internally consistent; medium when the failure case is described without a named diagnostic; route to `needs_review` if the learner reports numbers the laboratory could not have produced (for example a positive log-likelihood per trial) and no explanation.
- Keep feedback structured by the five sections; under 250 words.
