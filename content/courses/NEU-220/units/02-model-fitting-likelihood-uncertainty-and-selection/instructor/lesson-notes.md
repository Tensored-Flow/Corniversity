## Lesson notes (instructor only)

**Source mapping (all opened 2026-10-30).** Neuromatch W1D2 chapter page (Intro, Tutorials 1–6, Outro, further reading, day summary). Tutorial 1: $y_i = \theta x_i + \epsilon_i$, MSE, least-squares optimum $\hat\theta = \mathbf{x}^\top\mathbf{y}/\mathbf{x}^\top\mathbf{x}$, synthetic data with true slope 1.2 and 30 samples, bonus derivation. Tutorial 2: Gaussian noise, likelihood and log-likelihood, MLE equal to the MSE solution, simulated data. Tutorial 3: bootstrap resampling and confidence intervals, simulated data with slope 1.2. Tutorial 4: design matrix, OLS estimator $(\mathbf{X}^\top\mathbf{X})^{-1}\mathbf{X}^\top\mathbf{y}$, polynomial design matrices, fit quality, simulated data. Tutorial 5: train versus test data, bias–variance trade-off, bonus proof of the decomposition. Tutorial 6: k-fold cross-validation, bonus AIC. All pages carry the CC BY 4.0 footer.

**Teaching intent.** For a mathematically strong learner the estimators are routine; the discipline to teach is (a) least squares as a special case of maximum likelihood, so the noise model is a choice, (b) held-out comparison as a rule, (c) honest labelling of synthetic data. The laboratory (labId neural-tuning-fit, implemented by the application) is the brief's acceptance test for this course; it uses Corniversity's own synthetic data and never any Neuromatch dataset.

**Re-pacing.** W1D2 is the heaviest Neuromatch day (six tutorials). Here it is a full teaching week at the top of the hour budget, with the report allowed to finish in the next week's first evening; the unit says so.

**Tutor behaviour (dev-anantharaman).** Ask why before how well; ask for the out-of-sample check on every claim; ask whether held-out data were touched during fitting; label synthetic data every time; do not trust a number from code the learner has not read. If the learner reports a tuning curve as a property of "the neuron", ask which neuron; the answer is "a synthetic one", and the sentence is rewritten.
