## Grading notes (instructor only)

This is an explanation exercise; grade the ideas and the honesty of the claims, not the prose.

**Expected content.**
- Encoding versus decoding as directions of prediction (stimulus → response; response → variable), with Tutorial 1 (RGC temporal filter, Linear-Gaussian then Poisson GLM) and Tutorial 2 (logistic decoding of Steinmetz choices) as the examples. Both datasets are recordings with their own terms; the learner should name them as recorded, not as synthetic.
- The biphasic filter is evidence of a statistical dependence of this cell's firing on stimulus history in this recording, under the model's assumptions (exponential of a linear combination; conditional independence of bins; stationarity). Not evidence of: the biophysical mechanism; other cells or cell types; the cell's own dynamics (refractoriness, adaptation) that the model omits; anything causal.
- Neither fit is causal: several causal structures (cause, consequence, common input, relayed copy) produce the same dependence; only intervention distinguishes them. The automatic checks require causal words to be qualified and flag "shows that the neurons cause".
- L1: a zero weight reflects the sparsity prior, not absence of information; a nonzero weight may be a representative of a correlated group chosen almost arbitrarily.

**Feedback.** Under 150 words. The most useful comment is usually on part 2: learners tend to list what the filter *is* and omit the assumptions under which it is evidence; ask for them.

**Confidence.** High when all four parts are present and no causal sentence survives unqualified; route to `needs_review` if the learner argues from the retinal literature (e.g. known RGC circuitry) that the filter does reflect mechanism, since that is a claim beyond the unit's evidence that may nonetheless be defensible.
