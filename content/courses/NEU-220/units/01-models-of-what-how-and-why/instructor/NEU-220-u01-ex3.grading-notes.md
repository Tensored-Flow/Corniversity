## Grading notes (instructor only)

- Automatic checks: `general-argument` flags lists of particular cases; `inequality-named` looks for KL, Gibbs, Jensen or Lagrange language; `ln2-gap` looks for 0.307 (nats). A learner working in bits will fail the last check with a correct answer (0.443 bits); read the units before trusting it.
- The proof is the main assessed object (45% of the rubric). Insist on the equality case and on the observation that $-\int p\ln q$ depends on $p$ only through its mean; that step is where the exponential's affine log-density does the work, and it is the part worth a tutor comment if missing.
- Lagrange-multiplier proofs: accept only with a concavity or second-variation argument that the stationary point is the maximum. Otherwise mark the proof criterion partially met and say exactly what is missing.
- The interpretation paragraph is graded on four items: the claim, its constraint, refuting features from recorded data, and a constraint revision. A learner who writes "neurons maximise information so ISIs are exponential" without the constraint has missed the point of the unit; quote the sentence as the first substantive gap.
- Keep the Steinmetz data labelled as recorded and the toy distributions as constructed; correct any sentence that blurs them.
- Confidence: high when the proof is complete and the interpretation names a constraint revision; route to `needs_review` if the learner argues from information-theoretic results beyond the unit (channel capacity, rate–distortion), which may be correct but is outside the evidence this unit collects.
