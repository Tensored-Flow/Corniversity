## Reference reasoning (instructor only)

**1. Pure Poisson process.** Let $S(\tau) = P(\text{no spike in } (0, \tau])$ after a spike at $0$. Independence of disjoint intervals gives $S(\tau + dt) = S(\tau)(1 - r\,dt)$, so $S' = -rS$ and $S(\tau) = e^{-r\tau}$. The ISI density is $p(\tau) = -S'(\tau) = re^{-r\tau}$ on $[0, \infty)$. Moments: $\langle\tau\rangle = 1/r$, $\langle\tau^2\rangle = 2/r^2$, variance $1/r^2$, SD $1/r$, so $\text{CV} = 1$ for every rate. (The count in a window of length $T$ is Poisson with mean $rT$; not required.)

**2. Absolute refractory period.** The interval is $\tau = t_{ref} + \tau'$ with $\tau'$ exponential of rate $r$. Density: $p(\tau) = re^{-r(\tau - t_{ref})}$ for $\tau \ge t_{ref}$, and $0$ for $\tau < t_{ref}$. Mean $t_{ref} + 1/r$; the shift leaves the SD at $1/r$; $\text{CV} = (1/r)/(t_{ref} + 1/r) = 1/(1 + rt_{ref})$, which is strictly below $1$ whenever $rt_{ref} > 0$ and tends to $0$ as the neuron becomes a regular clock ($rt_{ref} \to \infty$).

**3. Numbers.** $1/r = 50$ ms; mean ISI $= 5 + 50 = 55$ ms; $\text{CV} = 1/(1 + 20\times0.005) = 1/1.1 = 0.909$.

**4. Refutation.** Features a recorded histogram can show that the pure model cannot produce: a gap (near-zero density) at intervals below a few ms; a mode at a nonzero interval; a CV markedly different from 1; a second mode (bursting). The refractory model repairs the gap but still predicts independent intervals and a monotone density beyond $t_{ref}$; serial correlations between successive intervals, bursts, and rate changes within a trial (non-stationarity) are beyond it. A good exponential fit shows that the histogram has that shape; it does not establish the independence of spikes (the defining property of a Poisson process), nor any mechanism, because other processes (for example rate-modulated or renewal processes with suitable kernels) can produce near-exponential histograms. Tutorial 1 presents this as fitting "by hand" for exactly this reason.

**Alternative valid approaches.** Deriving the density from the Poisson count distribution ($P(\text{no spike in } \tau) = e^{-r\tau}$ directly); using the memoryless property. For part 2, convolution with a delta at $t_{ref}$. Accept $0.91$ for the CV.

**Common gaps.** Quoting the exponential without deriving it; claiming the refractory period increases variability (it increases regularity); computing the CV with the shifted mean but the shifted SD (there is no shifted SD); listing refuting features that the model does produce (e.g. "irregular spiking").
