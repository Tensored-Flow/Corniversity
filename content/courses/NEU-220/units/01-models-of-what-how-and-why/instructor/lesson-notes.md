## Lesson notes (instructor only)

**Source mapping (all opened 2026-10-30).** Neuromatch W1D1 chapter page (Intro, Tutorials 1–4, Outro, further reading, day summary). Tutorial 1 ('What' models): Steinmetz et al. (2019) subset, Neuropixels in mice; spike counts, rasters, ISI distributions; candidate functional forms exponential, inverse, linear fitted "by hand". Tutorial 2 ('How' models): linear integrate-and-fire $dV_m = \alpha I$, leaky version $dV_m = -\beta V_m + \alpha I$, Poisson input spikes, inhibition; bonus section with the full LIF $C_m\,dV_m/dt = -(V_m - V_{rest})/R_m + I$. Tutorial 3 ('Why' models): entropy $H_b(X) = -\sum p\log_b p$, pmf from histogram counts, deterministic/uniform/exponential comparison, Steinmetz ISI entropy; acknowledgement of the data. Tutorial 4: discussion prompts. Prerequisites page: Python, linear algebra, statistics, calculus expectations and refresher links. Licence footer (CC BY 4.0 content, BSD-3 code) on every page.

**Teaching intent.** Install the what/how/why vocabulary and the four-line model critique (kind, assumptions, prediction, refuting observation) before any fitting is done. The learner's mathematics makes the derivations easy; the discipline to teach is labelling data origin and constraints. All three exercises are original: the tutorials fit ISI forms by hand and simulate the LIF; the exercises derive the Poisson ISI law with a refractory period, solve the LIF exactly, and prove the maximum-entropy theorem the tutorial only illustrates.

**Re-pacing.** Neuromatch allots one intensive day (two 90-minute tutorial blocks plus discussion) to W1D1. Here it is one teaching week: notebooks on two evenings, the lesson and derivations on two more. Say this plainly if the learner asks why a "day" takes a week.

**Tutor behaviour (dev-anantharaman).** Ask why the model behaves as it does before asking how well it fits; ask for a refuting observation for every model; check which data were recorded and which simulated in every statement; treat a passing notebook as the start of understanding. Never describe experiments on animals; the Steinmetz data are referred to as published recordings with their own terms.
