## Outline: Structure of finite groups and the Sylow theorems

This unit is **outlined, not yet prepared**. It will be written from chapters 13 and 15 of Judson's Abstract Algebra: Theory and Applications (HTML edition, GFDL), with lectures 22–23 of the MIT RES.18-011 student notes (CC BY-NC-SA, unchecked by MIT faculty) as an optional supplement, citing each statement by its number so that every claim can be reopened at its section page.

**Scope.** Finitely generated groups (Proposition 13.1.3); the fundamental theorem of finite abelian groups, every finite abelian group being a direct product of cyclic groups of prime-power order (Theorem 13.1.4), through the lemmas on elements of order $p$, $p$-groups, the splitting into Sylow components and the splitting off of a cyclic factor of maximal order (Lemmas 13.1.6–13.1.9); the finitely generated case (Theorem 13.1.10); solvable groups and composition series (Section 13.2). Cauchy's theorem (Theorem 15.1.1, generalising unit 1's involution exercise), $p$-groups (Corollary 15.1.2), the First, Second and Third Sylow Theorems (Theorems 15.1.4, 15.1.7, 15.1.8) with the lemmas on normalisers and conjugates (Lemmas 15.1.5–15.1.6); applications (Section 15.2): groups of order $pq$, non-simplicity of groups of orders such as $15$, $30$ and $56$, and a survey of the finite simple groups. The final synthesis project classifies the groups of order at most $15$ using these tools.

**What preparation will add.** A full lesson with the Sylow proofs organised around group actions from unit 9, worked applications, original exercises with hint ladders and rubrics, instructor reference reasoning, an exit check and common misconceptions. Until then the reading list above is accurate and the tutor can discuss the material, but there is no assessed work for this unit.
