## Grading notes (instructor only)

Expected content. $U(8)=\{1,3,5,7\}$; $3^2=9\equiv1$, $5^2=25\equiv1$, $7^2=49\equiv1\pmod 8$, so every element has order $1$ or $2$. A cyclic group of order $4$ has a generator, an element of order $4$; $U(8)$ has none, so it is not cyclic. Link to unit 1 exercise 1: $U(8)$ is a group in which every element squares to the identity, hence abelian by that exercise (and in fact it has the subgroup pattern of $\mathbb Z_2\times\mathbb Z_2$ from Example 3.3.5: three subgroups of order $2$). $U(5)=\{1,2,3,4\}$: $2,4,8\equiv3,16\equiv1$, so $2$ generates; $3$ also does. $U(10)=\{1,3,7,9\}$: $3,9,27\equiv7,21\equiv1$, so $3$ generates; $7$ also does.

- The automatic check flags "U(8) is cyclic" as a conclusion; it does not flag "suppose U(8) is cyclic". Read the argument regardless.
- Accept "U(8) has three elements of order 2 but a cyclic group of order 4 has exactly one" as the named property (it is Exercise 1's $\varphi(2)=1$).
- Do not accept "U(8) is not cyclic because 8 is not prime" (U(9) is cyclic and 9 is not prime; U(8) is the counterexample precisely because the criterion is subtler). Do not accept an appeal to the classification of which $U(n)$ are cyclic unless it is labelled as a cited result not proved in the course.
- Keep feedback under 120 words.
