## Reference reasoning (instructor only)

**1. Bézout in a group.** Let $d=\gcd(m,n)$. By Theorem 2.2.2 there are integers $r,s$ (one of them typically negative) with $d=mr+ns$. By the exponent laws, $a^d=a^{mr+ns}=(a^m)^r(a^n)^s=e^re^s=e$. (Negative exponents are legitimate because $a^{-1}$ exists; this is where the group structure is used.)

Now let $a$ have order $k$ (least positive exponent with $a^k=e$). If $k\mid j$ then $a^j=(a^k)^{j/k}=e$. Conversely if $a^j=e$ with $j\neq0$, then also $a^{|j|}=e$ and by the first part $a^{\gcd(|j|,k)}=e$; since $0<\gcd(|j|,k)\le k$, minimality of $k$ forces $\gcd(|j|,k)=k$, so $k\mid j$. (For $j=0$ the claim is trivial.) This recovers Proposition 4.1.12 without the division algorithm; the two proofs are of course equivalent.

**2. Intersection of roots of unity.** Let $d=\gcd(m,n)$. If $z^d=1$ then $z^m=(z^d)^{m/d}=1$ and $z^n=1$, so $\mu_d\subset\mu_m\cap\mu_n$. Conversely if $z^m=z^n=1$ then, applying part 1 in the group $\mathbb C^*$ to the element $z$, $z^d=1$, so $\mu_m\cap\mu_n\subset\mu_d$. Hence $\mu_m\cap\mu_n=\mu_{\gcd(m,n)}$.

$\gcd(12,8)=4$, so $\mu_{12}\cap\mu_8=\mu_4=\{\operatorname{cis}(2\pi k/4):k=0,1,2,3\}=\{1,i,-1,-i\}$.

**3. Primitive roots.** By Theorem 4.2.10, $\mu_n=\langle\omega\rangle$ is cyclic of order $n$ with $\omega$ of order $n$. By Theorem 4.1.13, $\omega^k$ has order $n/\gcd(k,n)$. An element of a group of order $n$ generates it iff its order is $n$ (then its $n$ distinct powers exhaust the group), so $\omega^k$ is primitive iff $n/\gcd(k,n)=n$ iff $\gcd(k,n)=1$. For $n=12$ the $k\in\{1,\dots,12\}$ coprime to $12$ are $1,5,7,11$, so the primitive $12$th roots of unity are $\omega,\omega^5,\omega^7,\omega^{11}$ with $\omega=\operatorname{cis}(\pi/6)=\frac{\sqrt3}2+\frac i2$; there are $\varphi(12)=4$ of them.

**Alternative valid approaches.** Part 2 may be argued through the subgroup lattice of the cyclic group $\mu_{mn}$ (both $\mu_m$ and $\mu_n$ are subgroups of it, and the intersection of $\langle a^{n}\rangle$ and $\langle a^{m}\rangle$ in a cyclic group of order $mn$ is $\langle a^{\operatorname{lcm}(m,n)}\rangle$, of order $mn/\operatorname{lcm}(m,n)=\gcd(m,n)$); accept if every step is justified. Part 3 may be done by the direct criterion: $\omega^k$ is primitive iff $(\omega^k)^j\neq1$ for $0<j<n$ iff $n\nmid kj$ for $0<j<n$ iff $\gcd(k,n)=1$.

**Common gaps.** Using $a^{mn}=e$ (true but useless) instead of the gcd; forgetting the easy inclusion $\mu_d\subset\mu_m\cap\mu_n$; confusing $\gcd$ with $\operatorname{lcm}$ and answering $\mu_{24}$; listing $\omega^k$ for $k$ coprime to $12$ but including $k=0$ or omitting $11$.
