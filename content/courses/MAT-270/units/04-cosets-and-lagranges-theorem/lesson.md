## Outline: Cosets and Lagrange's theorem

This unit is **outlined, not yet prepared**. It will be written from chapter 6 of Judson's Abstract Algebra: Theory and Applications (HTML edition, GFDL), citing each statement by its number so that every claim can be reopened at its section page.

**Scope.** Left and right cosets $gH$ and $Hg$, the equivalence relation they come from, and the partition of $G$ into cosets of equal size (Proposition 6.2.1); the index $[G:H]$; Lagrange's theorem $|G|=[G:H]\,|H|$ (Theorem 6.2.2) and its corollaries: the order of every element divides $|G|$ (Corollary 6.2.3), a group of prime order is cyclic and generated by any non-identity element (Corollary 6.2.4), and $[G:K]=[G:H][H:K]$ (Corollary 6.2.5); the failure of the converse, $A_4$ having no subgroup of order $6$ (Proposition 6.2.7), and conjugate cycles (Theorem 6.2.8); Fermat's little theorem and Euler's theorem as Lagrange applied to $U(n)$ (Section 6.3). Exercise 1 of unit 2 will be revisited as the cyclic case of Lagrange.

**What preparation will add.** A full lesson with coset computations in $S_3$, $\mathbb Z$ and $D_4$, the distinction between left and right cosets made visible (preparing unit 6), original exercises with hint ladders and rubrics, instructor reference reasoning, an exit check and common misconceptions. Until then the reading list above is accurate and the tutor can discuss the material, but there is no assessed work for this unit.
