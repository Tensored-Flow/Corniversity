## Outline: Hierarchical models and learning to learn

This unit is **outlined, not yet prepared**. It will be written from OCW 9.66J lecture 11's hierarchical-priors slides, lectures 19–21 (Intuitive Biology and the Role of Theories; Learning Domain Structures 1–2; confirmed to exist, not yet text-extracted) and the web book's *Hierarchical models* chapter (reference-only).

**Scope.** Unit 2 ended with a coin whose fairness was itself a hypothesis. This unit makes the move general: a hierarchical model places priors on priors, so that experience with many coins, many categories or many words teaches *overhypotheses* about what new ones are like. The blessing of abstraction: the higher level can be learned faster than the lower levels it constrains, because every observation bears on it. Cognitive cases: the shape bias in word learning, learning that bags of marbles are uniform in colour, one-shot learning of visual categories, and the lectures' intuitive biology and learning of domain structures (trees, taxonomies) as the kind of abstract knowledge a theory supplies.

**What preparation will add.** A full lesson with a two-level Beta–Binomial model derived and its "learning to learn" behaviour computed, original exercises (the posterior over an overhypothesis after observing several homogeneous groups; a prediction for a new group from one example; an argument for when abstraction is *not* a blessing), instructor reference reasoning, an exit check and the unit's common misconceptions ("hierarchical models need more data than flat ones"). Until then the reading above is accurate and the tutor can discuss the material, but there is no assessed work for this unit.
