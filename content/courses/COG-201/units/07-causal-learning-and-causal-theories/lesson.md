## Outline: Causal learning and causal theories

This unit is **outlined, not yet prepared**. It will be written from OCW 9.66J lectures 22–24 (Causal Learning; Causal Theories 1–2; confirmed to exist, not yet text-extracted) and from the web book's dependence chapters and its "Estimating Causal Power" example (reference-only).

**Scope.** Causal learning as two inference problems: *structure* (which variables cause which) and *strength* (how strongly). The noisy-OR parameterisation and causal power as a parameter of a generative model; learning strength as Bayesian parameter estimation (the web book's causal-power example continues unit 2's Beta–Bernoulli machinery); learning structure as model selection over graphs (unit 5's razor); blocking and backward blocking as consequences of explaining away in a learner who maintains uncertainty over structure; the blicket detector as the experimental paradigm; and causal *theories*, abstract knowledge about what kinds of things can cause what, as hierarchical priors over causal models (unit 6). Intervention versus observation is made precise and used to say what an experiment can establish that passive data cannot.

**What preparation will add.** A full lesson with the noisy-OR likelihood and a structure-versus-strength computation written out, original exercises (a backward-blocking calculation; a structure posterior over three candidate graphs; an intervention design that distinguishes two causal hypotheses), instructor reference reasoning, an exit check and the unit's common misconceptions ("correlation plus a plausible story is causal evidence"). Until then the reading above is accurate and the tutor can discuss the material, but there is no assessed work for this unit.
