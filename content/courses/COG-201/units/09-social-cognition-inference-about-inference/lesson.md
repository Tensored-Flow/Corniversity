## Outline: Social cognition: inference about inference

This unit is **outlined, not yet prepared**. It will be written from the web book's *Social cognition* chapter (reference-only; read on the book's site). The 2004 OCW slide decks do not treat this topic, so the unit is the one place where the web book is the sole spine; the current MIT course lists theory of mind among its applications, which is the reason for including it.

**Scope.** Model an agent as a planner who chooses actions to achieve goals given beliefs; then invert that generative model to infer goals, preferences, knowledge and beliefs from observed actions. The chapter's progression from inferring goals to inferring what others know and believe is nested inference (a model of an agent who models the world), and its communication section makes a listener who reasons about a speaker who reasons about the listener, from which scalar implicature falls out. The unit treats these as computational-level models and asks, for each, what behavioural evidence exists and what it shows.

**What preparation will add.** A full lesson with a small inverse-planning model worked out exactly (a two-goal, two-action grid), original exercises (a goal posterior from a short action sequence; a joint inference about knowledge and goal; a one-level pragmatic listener computed by hand), instructor reference reasoning, an exit check and the unit's common misconceptions ("a model that infers beliefs shows that people represent beliefs"). Until then the reading above is accurate and the tutor can discuss the material, but there is no assessed work for this unit.
