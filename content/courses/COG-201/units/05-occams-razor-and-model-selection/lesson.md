## Outline: Occam's razor and model selection

This unit is **outlined, not yet prepared**. It will be written from OCW 9.66J lectures 17 and 18 (Controlling Complexity and Occam's Razor 1–2; their slide decks were confirmed to exist but have not yet been text-extracted, so page-level locators will be added at preparation), lecture 13's "Bayesian Occam's Razor" slide, and the web book's *Occam's Razor* chapter (reference-only).

**Scope.** The unit generalises unit 2's size principle. A more flexible hypothesis spreads its predictive probability over more possible data sets, so by the law of conservation of belief it assigns less to any particular one: the marginal likelihood $P(D\mid M)=\int P(D\mid\theta,M)P(\theta\mid M)\,d\theta$ automatically trades fit against complexity, and the Bayes factor between two models is the ratio of their marginal likelihoods. Worked cases from the book: fair versus unfair coin (a model with a free weight parameter against a fixed one), the effect of unused parameters, and polynomial curve fitting, which answers lecture 1's "Plato meets Matlab" question quantitatively. The unit also treats the rectangle game as the continuous analogue of the number game and reads several cognitive phenomena as model selection, always labelling what is model behaviour and what is evidence.
