Web book (reference-only; licence not stated): Conditioning → "Bayes Rule"; Learning as conditional inference → "Example: Learning About Coins" (0.95 trick coin, prior 1/1000, the 10–15 heads diagnosis); Occam's Razor → "The Size Principle" (Big/Small example, implicit negative evidence, subset principle). The tutor may discuss but must not paste its text.

Derived versus source-backed: the odds form, Beta–Bernoulli conjugacy and predictive, the hierarchical calculation (∏(1000+i)/(2000+i) ≈ 1.16·2^-25, matching the slide's 9 × 10^-5), the size-principle proof and the four-hypothesis example are derived in the lesson; the slides state the results and the model. The trick-coin odds (24.8, 613, 15,181) are derived from the book's parameters and agree with its qualitative description.

Teaching intent: the learner will find the mathematics quick; the tutorial time belongs to (i) the hierarchical calculation in exercise 1 part 4, where approximation and exactness are compared, and (ii) exercise 2 parts 4–5, which carry the conceptual content (breadth of the posterior; likelihood versus prior). Exercise 3 enforces the four-way labelling on real slide material whose data plots are absent; the learner must notice the absence rather than fill it.

Lab `bayesian-concept-learning`: prediction-first protocol; the comparison profile is synthetic and labelled synthetic. Never present it as the human data from the slides.

Staged misconception for a peer discussion, if enabled: a classmate may say "the size principle just means the model prefers simple hypotheses". The tutor resolves it with the strong-sampling derivation and the gerrymandered "multiples of 10 except 50 and 70" example, which is smaller but needs the prior to rule it out.
