## Reference reasoning (instructor only)

**1. Seven heads.** Likelihood ratio $\frac{P(D\mid\text{trick})}{P(D\mid\text{fair})}=\big(\tfrac{0.95}{0.5}\big)^7=1.9^7=89.387$. Prior odds $P(\text{trick})/P(\text{fair})=\tfrac{1/1000}{999/1000}=1/999$. Posterior odds $=89.387/999=0.08948$. Hence $P(\text{trick}\mid D)=\frac{0.08948}{1.08948}=0.0821$. Seven heads in a row raise the probability of a trick coin from $0.1\%$ to about $8\%$: notable, not decisive.

**2. Threshold.** Trick becomes more probable when posterior odds exceed $1$: $1.9^n>999\iff n>\ln999/\ln1.9=6.9068/0.6419=10.76$, so $n=11$. Check: $1.9^{10}=613.1<999$ (odds $0.614$, fair still favoured) and $1.9^{11}=1164.9>999$ (odds $1.166$). This matches the web book's observation that after ten heads the two are "roughly comparable" and that the evidence tips soon after.

**3. Beta–Bernoulli.** Prior $\mathrm{Beta}(2,2)$, data $N_H=3$, $N_T=7$: posterior $\mathrm{Beta}(5,9)$. Predictive $P(\text{H next}\mid D)=\mathbb E[\theta\mid D]=5/14=0.3571$. Mode $(5-1)/(5+9-2)=4/12=0.3333$. Variance $\frac{5\cdot9}{14^2\cdot15}=\frac{45}{2940}=0.01531$, standard deviation $0.1237$. The maximum-likelihood estimate is $3/10=0.3$; the predictive $0.357$ lies above it because the prior's two fictitious heads and two fictitious tails pull the estimate towards $\tfrac12$, by an amount that shrinks as data accumulate (with $300$ heads and $700$ tails the predictive would be $302/1004=0.3008$).

**4. Hierarchical.** $P(D\mid\text{unfair})=\int_0^1\theta^{25}\cdot1\,d\theta=\tfrac1{26}=0.03846$. For the fair branch, $\mathrm{Beta}(1000,1000)$ has mean $\tfrac12$ and standard deviation $\sqrt{\tfrac{1000\cdot1000}{2000^2\cdot2001}}\approx0.011$, so $\theta^{25}$ is close to $2^{-25}=2.98\times10^{-8}$ throughout the bulk of the prior and $P(D\mid\text{fair})\approx2^{-25}$. Then the Bayes factor for fairness is $\approx 2^{-25}/(1/26)=26\cdot2^{-25}=7.75\times10^{-7}$ and, with prior odds $0.99/0.01=99$, the posterior odds are $\approx7.7\times10^{-5}$. The exact value is $P(D\mid\text{fair})=\frac{B(1025,1000)}{B(1000,1000)}=\prod_{i=0}^{24}\frac{1000+i}{2000+i}$; each factor $\frac{1000+i}{2000+i}=\tfrac12\cdot\frac{1+i/1000}{1+i/2000}$ exceeds $\tfrac12$, and the product exceeds $2^{-25}$ by $\exp\big(\sum_i[\ln(1+i/1000)-\ln(1+i/2000)]\big)\approx\exp(0.15)\approx1.16$, giving $P(D\mid\text{fair})\approx3.46\times10^{-8}$, Bayes factor $9.0\times10^{-7}$ and posterior odds $8.9\times10^{-5}$, the slide's $9\times10^{-5}$. The point-mass approximation is therefore *below* the exact value: because $\theta^{25}$ is convex, spread in the prior around $\tfrac12$ raises the expectation (Jensen).

**Alternative valid approaches.** Working in log-odds throughout; computing part 3's standard deviation from the Beta variance formula or numerically; approximating the fair-branch integral with a second-order (delta-method) correction $2^{-25}(1+\binom{25}{2}\cdot4\sigma^2)\approx2^{-25}(1+300\cdot4\cdot1.25\times10^{-4})\approx1.15\cdot2^{-25}$, which agrees with the exact product to the stated precision.

**Common gaps.** Dropping the prior in part 1 (gives $0.989$); solving $1.9^n>1000$ and reporting $n=11$ with a wrong check; posterior $\mathrm{Beta}(3,7)$ (forgetting the prior) or $\mathrm{Beta}(4,8)$ (treating the prior as one fictitious observation each); mode and mean swapped; part 4 stated with the approximation but no direction for the error.
