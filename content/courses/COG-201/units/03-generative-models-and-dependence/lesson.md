## Outline: Generative models, graphical models and dependence

This unit is **outlined, not yet prepared**. It will be written from OCW 9.66J lectures 9 and 10 (Fall 2004, CC BY-NC-SA 4.0; note that the lecture 10 file, listed as "Simple Bayesian Learning 1", contains the Bayes-net material) and from the web book's chapters *Generative models*, *Causal and statistical dependence* and *Conditional dependence* (reference-only; read on the book's site).

**Scope.** A generative model as a description of how observations are produced, which can be run forward (simulation, prediction) and inverted (conditioning); the directed graphical model as a factorisation $P(x_1,\dots,x_n)=\prod_i P(x_i\mid \mathrm{pa}(x_i))$ and the conditional independences it encodes (lecture 10's global and local semantics); causal versus merely statistical dependence and why intervention, not observation, distinguishes them; screening off; explaining away as the signature of a common-effect structure, computed exactly on the lecture's logical-OR example and the web book's medical-diagnosis example; the blicket-detector studies as the cognitive case; and sampling as a general way of answering questions of a model, with the lecture's "sampling possible worlds" as the bridge to unit 8.

**What preparation will add.** A full lesson with the factorisation theorem and the explaining-away calculation written out, original exercises (proving a conditional independence from a factorisation; an explaining-away computation with the dependence on the functional form; an intervention-versus-observation contrast on a three-variable model), instructor reference reasoning, an exit check and the unit's common misconceptions ("an arrow in a Bayes net is always a causal claim"). Until then the reading above is accurate and the tutor can discuss the material, but there is no assessed work for this unit.
