## Reference reasoning (instructor only)

**1. Labels.**

- S1, *measurement*: it reports who (twenty participants), what they were shown (the example 60), what they did (rated other numbers) and what was recorded (ratings falling off gradually). It says nothing about any model.
- S2, *model behaviour*: it states what the Bayesian model with hypothesis averaging outputs for the same example. It is a fact about the model regardless of any data.
- S3, *experimental evidence*: it asserts a correspondence between S1 and S2, the model's profile matching the average ratings.
- S4, *causal (mechanistic) claim*: it asserts what people compute, a claim about the process inside the participants.

**2. Level and licence.** The correspondence in S3 bears on Marr's computational level: it shows that the problem the participants solve, and the quality of their solution, are well described by Bayesian generalisation over a hypothesis space of number concepts. The inference from S3 to S4 is not licensed. A level-1 fit constrains the input–output problem and its ideal solution; S4 is a claim about representation and algorithm (level 2) or implementation (level 3). Several different algorithms, including ones that never represent a posterior, can produce the same profile.

**3. Further evidence and an alternative model.** Evidence that bears on S4 would be process-level: response-time patterns predicted by a sampling or enumeration process, trial-by-trial learning trajectories, effects of interventions that should matter only to a posterior-computing process (manipulating the prior through instruction or context and predicting the quantitative shift), individual-difference signatures, or neural data. An alternative model that also yields a graded profile for the example 60 is a similarity-based or exemplar model that rates numbers by numerical or featural distance from the example; a plain distance heuristic ("closer numbers are more likely") also produces S2's qualitative shape. Distinguishing them requires cases where the models disagree, for instance example sets that make the Bayesian model switch to a rule-like profile.

**Grading note.** This exercise is the department convention in miniature; the four labels and the identification of the unlicensed step are the substance. Accept a learner who argues that S3 is evidence *for* S2 being a good level-1 account and nothing more. Do not accept "S4 is correct because the model fits"; the deterministic check flags the explicit form of this.
