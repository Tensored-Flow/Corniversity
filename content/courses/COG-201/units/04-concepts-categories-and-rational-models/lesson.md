## Outline: Concepts, categories and rational models of categorisation

This unit is **outlined, not yet prepared**. It will be written from OCW 9.66J lecture 12 (Probabilistic Models for Concept Learning and Categorization 1), lectures 14–16 (unsupervised and semi-supervised learning; exemplar models and neural networks) and the web book's *Mixture models* chapter (reference-only).

**Scope.** Lecture 12 defines a category as a set of objects treated equivalently for some purpose and a concept as its mental representation, and lists what concepts are for: classification, prediction, inductive generalisation, explanation and reference. The unit follows its path from the classical view (concepts as rules) through the statistical view (concepts as representations for classifying) to two-class classification with parametric class models fitted by maximum likelihood, and the prototype effects of Posner and Keele (an unseen prototype classified as well as memorised variants) as the phenomenon such models explain. It then takes the lecture's own list of limitations seriously (overlapping concepts, learning from a few positive examples, non-Gaussian categories) as the motivation for the number-game model of unit 2, and widens the frame with exemplar models and neural networks as non-parametric alternatives, unsupervised and semi-supervised learning, and the web book's mixture models with an unknown number of components.

**What preparation will add.** A full lesson, original exercises (the Bayes-optimal decision boundary for two Gaussian classes; a prototype-versus-exemplar prediction that the two models disagree on; an EM or Gibbs sketch for a two-component mixture on labelled synthetic data), instructor reference reasoning, an exit check and the unit's common misconceptions ("prototypicality is degree of membership"). Until then the reading above is accurate and the tutor can discuss the material, but there is no assessed work for this unit.
