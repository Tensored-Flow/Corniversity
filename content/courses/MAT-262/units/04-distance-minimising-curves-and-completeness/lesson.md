## Outline: distance, minimising curves and completeness

This unit is **outlined, not yet prepared**. It will be written from Gudmundsson's Proposition 5.10 and chapter 7 (Theorem 7.23, Definition 7.24, Proposition 7.33, Theorem 7.34; version 1.438, reference-only) and from Sternberg §6.5–6.6 (CC BY-NC-SA 2.0), with Lee chapter 6 as the commercial reference.

**Scope.** The Riemannian distance $d(p,q)=\inf L(\gamma)$ and the proof, deferred by the notes, that it is a metric inducing the manifold topology; Gauss's lemma on the orthogonality of radial geodesics to geodesic spheres; the local minimising property of geodesics and the converse that minimising curves are geodesics; totally geodesic submanifolds and the characterisation by vanishing second fundamental form; geodesic completeness, the Hopf–Rinow theorem (metric completeness, geodesic completeness and the Heine–Borel property are equivalent, and any of them gives minimising geodesics between any two points), of which Theorem 7.34 is the consequence recorded in the notes; symmetric spaces as complete homogeneous examples. The Hopf–Rinow theorem itself is not proved in the primary notes; the prepared lesson will prove it and label the proof as Corniversity's.

**What preparation will add.** A full lesson with definitions, examples and non-examples, the main theorems with their hypotheses in view, original exercises with hint ladders and rubrics, instructor reference reasoning for the assessor, an exit check and the unit's common misconceptions. Until then the reading list above is accurate and the tutor can discuss the material, but there is no assessed work for this unit.
