## Grading notes (instructor only)

This is an explanation exercise; grade the ideas, not the prose. Expected content, with the signature convention $(-,+,+,+)$ named (accept $(+,-,-,-)$ if named consistently).

- Survives verbatim: the components $g_{ij}=g(\partial_i,\partial_j)$ (now a symmetric invertible matrix with one negative eigenvalue), the transformation law $\tilde G=J^tGJ$, the law $\det\tilde G=(\det J)^2\det G$ (so $\det g<0$ in Lorentzian signature, but non-degeneracy is chart-independent), and the inverse $g^{ij}$. Reason: these use only bilinearity, symmetry and invertibility.
- Fails or changes: (i) arc length: $g(\dot\gamma,\dot\gamma)$ can be negative or zero, so $\sqrt{g(\dot\gamma,\dot\gamma)}$ is undefined; one uses $\int\sqrt{|g(\dot\gamma,\dot\gamma)|}$ and restricts to timelike or spacelike curves (proper time for timelike curves). (ii) The distance function of Proposition 5.10: the infimum of lengths over all curves is $0$ for any two points that can be joined through null directions, so no metric-space structure arises; the Lorentzian analogue (the time separation function) satisfies a reverse triangle inequality (Sternberg §7.1.6). (iii) Existence via partitions of unity (Theorem 5.16): a convex combination of non-degenerate indefinite forms can be degenerate, e.g. $\tfrac12\mathrm{diag}(-1,1)+\tfrac12\mathrm{diag}(1,-1)=0$; indeed not every manifold admits a Lorentzian metric (a compact manifold does only if its Euler characteristic vanishes), a fact the learner may state without proof.
- Separation: the two theories share the connection and curvature formalism (unit 2 onward) but differ in hypotheses and conclusions (distance, completeness, comparison theorems); keeping them in separate units prevents silent convention switches, which the tutor persona treats as an error.
- A learner who says "everything carries over" has the key misconception; point to the convex-combination example. Keep feedback under 120 words.
