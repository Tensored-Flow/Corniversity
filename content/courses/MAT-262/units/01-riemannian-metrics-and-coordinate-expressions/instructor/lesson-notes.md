## Lesson notes (instructor only)

Source mapping, Gudmundsson v1.438 (printed pages): Definition 5.5, Example 5.6, Definition 5.7 (p. 63); Examples 5.8–5.9, Proposition 5.10 (p. 64); Definition 5.11, Example 5.12, Definition 5.14 (p. 65); Fact 5.15, Theorem 5.16 (p. 66); Definitions 5.17–5.20, Example 5.21 (p. 67); Remark 5.30 (pp. 70–71), Example 5.32 (p. 71); Exercise 5.2 (p. 74). The notes use $m$ for the dimension and $\langle,\rangle$ for the Euclidean metric; the lesson uses $n$ and the same bracket. The Gram criterion and the graph example are Corniversity additions, not in the notes.

Teaching intent: the unit installs the persona's habit "a chart is a tool with a domain". Keep the tutorial on exercise 1 until the learner has written the chart domain before any computation and has separated the coordinate statement from the geometric one at the poles. Once that habit is visible, move to exercise 2 and the density question.

Riemannian versus Lorentzian: the lesson names the Lorentzian case only to exclude it; exercise 3 is the place to discuss it, with Sternberg §7.2 as the open reading and Lee's "Pseudo-Riemannian Metrics" section (chapter 2, p. 40 per the table of contents) as the reference if the book is available. Do not let the signature discussion enter exercises 1 and 2.

The owner's named textbook (Lee, 2nd ed.) is commercial and only its front matter is public; its chapter 2 is listed as optional preparation and must not be presented as required reading.

Staged misconception for a peer discussion, if peers are enabled: a classmate may claim that $g_{\varphi\varphi}\to0$ "shows the sphere is pinched at the poles"; resolve with the Gram criterion and the graph chart.
