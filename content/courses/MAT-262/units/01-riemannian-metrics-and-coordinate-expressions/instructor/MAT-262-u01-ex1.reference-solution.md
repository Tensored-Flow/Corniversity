## Reference reasoning (instructor only)

**Conventions.** Signature $(+,+)$; $x^1=\theta$, $x^2=\varphi$; $g_{ij}=g(\partial_i,\partial_j)$ with lower indices; $\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle$ the Euclidean inner product of $\mathbb{R}^3$. $S^2_R=f^{-1}(R^2)$ for $f(p)=|p|^2$, and $R^2$ is a regular value ($df_p=2\langle p,\cdot\rangle\neq0$ on the sphere), so $S^2_R$ is a smooth surface and $T_pS^2_R=p^\perp$.

**1. $F$ is a parametrisation.** The components of $F$ are smooth. *Injective:* if $F(\theta,\varphi)=F(\theta',\varphi')$ then $R\cos\theta=R\cos\theta'$ forces $\theta=\theta'$ because $\cos$ is injective on $(0,\pi)$; then $\sin\theta>0$ and $(\cos\varphi,\sin\varphi)=(\cos\varphi',\sin\varphi')$ forces $\varphi=\varphi'$ in $(0,2\pi)$. *Image:* a point $(x,y,z)\in S^2_R$ is hit iff $z\in(-R,R)$ (so $\theta=\arccos(z/R)\in(0,\pi)$) and $(x,y)/(R\sin\theta)$ is a unit vector different from $(1,0)$; the excluded points are therefore exactly $\{(x,y,z)\in S^2_R:y=0,\ x\ge0\}$, the closed half-meridian through both poles (the poles themselves have $x=y=0$ and lie in it). So $W=S^2_R\setminus\{y=0,x\ge0\}$, the complement in $S^2_R$ of a closed set, hence open. *Immersion:*
$$\partial_\theta F=R(\cos\theta\cos\varphi,\ \cos\theta\sin\varphi,\ -\sin\theta),\qquad \partial_\varphi F=R(-\sin\theta\sin\varphi,\ \sin\theta\cos\varphi,\ 0),$$
and $\partial_\theta F\times\partial_\varphi F=R^2\sin\theta\,(\sin\theta\cos\varphi,\sin\theta\sin\varphi,\cos\theta)=R\sin\theta\,F(\theta,\varphi)$, of length $R^2\sin\theta>0$ on $(0,\pi)$. So the partials are independent and $dF$ has rank $2$ everywhere on the domain. *Chart:* $F$ is a smooth injective immersion from the 2-manifold $(0,\pi)\times(0,2\pi)$ into the 2-manifold $S^2_R$ (it lands in $S^2_R$ since $|F|=R$); by the inverse function theorem it is a local diffeomorphism onto open subsets of $S^2_R$, hence an open map, hence a diffeomorphism onto its open image $W$. Its inverse $F^{-1}:W\to(0,\pi)\times(0,2\pi)$ is a chart of $S^2_R$. (Explicit alternative: $\theta=\arccos(z/R)$ and $\varphi$ the unique angle in $(0,2\pi)$ with $(\cos\varphi,\sin\varphi)=(x,y)/\sqrt{x^2+y^2}$; both are continuous on $W$ since $\arccos$ is continuous on $(-1,1)$ and the angle function is continuous on the plane minus the non-negative $x$-axis.)

**2. Coefficients.** From the partials,
$$g_{\theta\theta}=\langle\partial_\theta F,\partial_\theta F\rangle=R^2(\cos^2\theta\cos^2\varphi+\cos^2\theta\sin^2\varphi+\sin^2\theta)=R^2,$$
$$g_{\theta\varphi}=R^2(-\cos\theta\sin\theta\cos\varphi\sin\varphi+\cos\theta\sin\theta\sin\varphi\cos\varphi+0)=0,\qquad g_{\varphi\varphi}=R^2\sin^2\theta(\sin^2\varphi+\cos^2\varphi)=R^2\sin^2\theta.$$
Hence $g=R^2\,d\theta^2+R^2\sin^2\theta\,d\varphi^2$, matrix $(g_{ij})=\mathrm{diag}(R^2,R^2\sin^2\theta)$, positive definite on $W$ because $R^2>0$ and $\sin^2\theta>0$ for $\theta\in(0,\pi)$. Consistency: $\det(g_{ij})=R^4\sin^2\theta=|\partial_\theta F\times\partial_\varphi F|^2$, as the Gram criterion predicts.

**3. The poles.** At $\theta=0$ or $\pi$, $\sin\theta=0$, so $\partial_\varphi F=0$: $dF$ has rank $1$ and the extension is not an immersion. It is also not injective: $F(0,\varphi)=(0,0,R)$ for every $\varphi$ (the whole segment $\{0\}\times(0,2\pi)$ collapses to the north pole), and likewise at $\theta=\pi$. Correspondingly $\det(g_{ij})=R^4\sin^2\theta\to0$. None of this concerns the sphere, which is a smooth surface at the poles (regular value argument above), nor the metric $g$, which is the restriction of a positive definite inner product to $T_pS^2_R$ and hence positive definite at every point of $S^2_R$, poles included. To see it in coordinates, use the graph chart $G(x,y)=(x,y,\sqrt{R^2-x^2-y^2})$ on the disc $x^2+y^2<R^2$, which covers the north pole. With $h=\sqrt{R^2-x^2-y^2}$, $\partial_xG=(1,0,-x/h)$ and $\partial_yG=(0,1,-y/h)$, so
$$g_{xx}=1+\frac{x^2}{R^2-x^2-y^2},\qquad g_{xy}=\frac{xy}{R^2-x^2-y^2},\qquad g_{yy}=1+\frac{y^2}{R^2-x^2-y^2},$$
smooth on the disc, and at the pole $(x,y)=(0,0)$ the matrix is the identity: positive definite. The degeneration in part 2 is a *coordinate singularity*: by the Gram criterion $\det(g_{ij})\to0$ exactly where the parametrisation stops being an immersion, and no further.

**4. Lengths.** The parallel $\gamma(\varphi)=F(\theta_0,\varphi)$, $\varphi\in(0,2\pi)$, has $\dot\gamma=\partial_\varphi$, so $|\dot\gamma|=\sqrt{g_{\varphi\varphi}}=R\sin\theta_0$ and $L=\int_0^{2\pi}R\sin\theta_0\,d\varphi=2\pi R\sin\theta_0$: the circumference of the Euclidean circle of radius $R\sin\theta_0$ in the plane $z=R\cos\theta_0$. The meridian arc $\gamma(\theta)=F(\theta,\varphi_0)$, $\theta\in[\theta_1,\theta_2]$, has $|\dot\gamma|=\sqrt{g_{\theta\theta}}=R$ and $L=R(\theta_2-\theta_1)$: an arc of a great circle of radius $R$ subtending angle $\theta_2-\theta_1$. (The omitted single point $\varphi=0$ of the parallel does not change the length.)

**Alternative valid approaches (accept with correct domains).** Longitude in $(-\pi,\pi)$, or colatitude measured from the south pole, or latitude $\lambda=\pi/2-\theta$ giving $g=R^2(d\lambda^2+\cos^2\lambda\,d\varphi^2)$ on $(-\pi/2,\pi/2)\times(0,2\pi)$. Stereographic coordinates $w\in\mathbb{R}^2$ from the north pole onto the plane $z=0$: $g=\dfrac{4R^4}{(R^2+|w|^2)^2}(dw_1^2+dw_2^2)$ on $S^2_R$ minus the north pole, with the coordinate singularity at the *north pole only* (the chart covers the south pole and in fact all of $S^2_R\setminus\{N\}$); the learner must then discuss the pole they excluded. Cylindrical coordinates $(z,\varphi)$ on $|z|<R$: $g=\dfrac{R^2}{R^2-z^2}dz^2+(R^2-z^2)d\varphi^2$, degenerate in the other direction at $z=\pm R$ ($g_{zz}\to\infty$, $g_{\varphi\varphi}\to0$); accept with the correct analysis. Any of these is a full answer if the chart domain is stated, the coefficients are derived from the ambient inner product, the pole discussion is carried out for the chart actually used, and a second chart is produced at the excluded points.

**Common gaps.** Dropping the factor $R^2$; writing the domain as $[0,\pi]\times[0,2\pi]$; "the metric is singular at the poles" without the qualifier "in these coordinates"; identifying the collapse of the $\varphi$-circle with non-smoothness of the sphere; omitting the verification that the second chart's Gram matrix is positive definite at the pole; checking the lengths without the comparison to Euclidean circles.
