## Grading notes (instructor only)

- Automatic checks: `metric-coefficients` detects $R^2\sin^2\theta$ (or $(R\sin\theta)^2$, or the factored form); `no-geometric-singularity-claim` flags "the sphere is singular at the poles" and similar; `chart-domain-stated` fires only when the learner calls $(\theta,\varphi)$ a chart without a domain restriction. All advisory; read the argument. A learner using latitude or stereographic coordinates will legitimately fail `metric-coefficients`; mark the derivation on its merits and note the alternate approach.
- Conventions must be stated (signature, index placement, which coordinate is which). Missing conventions are a precise comment and a "partially met" on `conventions-and-chart`, not a failure of the mathematics.
- First substantive gap, most often: part 3 says the metric is singular at the poles without distinguishing coordinates from geometry, or produces no second chart. Quote the sentence and ask for the Gram criterion.
- Accept the inverse-function-theorem argument for the chart or the explicit inverse; either is complete. "The inverse is continuous because $F$ is continuous and bijective" is not an argument.
- The image $W$ must exclude the whole closed half-meridian, including both poles; a learner who excludes only the poles has an incorrect domain for $\varphi\in(0,2\pi)$ (mark `conventions-and-chart` partially met with the correction).
- Confidence: high when coefficients, the rank drop at the poles and a positive definite second chart appear; medium when the second chart is named but not computed; route to `needs_review` if the learner invokes Lorentzian signature or physics conventions (the exercise is purely Riemannian).
- Do not penalise $\phi$ versus $\varphi$, $x^1,x^2$ versus $u,v$, or $ds^2$ notation.
