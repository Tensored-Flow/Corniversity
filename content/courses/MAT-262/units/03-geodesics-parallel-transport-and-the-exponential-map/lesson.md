## Outline: geodesics, parallel transport and the exponential map

This unit is **outlined, not yet prepared**. It will be written from Gudmundsson's chapter 7 (version 1.438, reference-only), keeping the notes' statement numbering (Definitions 7.1, 7.4, 7.21; Proposition 7.2; Theorems 7.5, 7.9, 7.23) so that every claim can be cited to an exact page, with Sternberg §3.3–3.4 and chapter 6 (CC BY-NC-SA 2.0) as the open-licensed alternative and Lee chapters 4–5 as the commercial reference.

**Scope.** Vector fields along a curve and the induced covariant derivative $D/dt$, characterised by linearity, the Leibniz rule and agreement with $\nabla_{\dot\gamma}$; parallel fields and parallel transport as a linear isometry between tangent spaces (Theorem 7.5); geodesics as curves with $\nabla_{\dot\gamma}\dot\gamma=0$, their local existence and uniqueness (Theorem 7.9), the coordinate form $\ddot\gamma^k+\Gamma^k_{ij}\dot\gamma^i\dot\gamma^j=0$ and constancy of speed; great circles on spheres and one-parameter subgroups of Lie groups with bi-invariant metrics; the exponential map, normal neighbourhoods and the first variation of energy; geodesics as local minimisers of length (Theorem 7.23), continued in unit 4 with the distance function.

**What preparation will add.** A full lesson with definitions, examples and non-examples, the main theorems with their hypotheses in view, original exercises with hint ladders and rubrics (including the geodesics of the sphere of radius $R$ in the chart of unit 1), instructor reference reasoning for the assessor, an exit check and the unit's common misconceptions. Until then the reading list above is accurate and the tutor can discuss the material, but there is no assessed work for this unit.
