## Reference reasoning (instructor only)

Throughout, $\top$ and $\perp$ denote the pointwise orthogonal projections $T_pN=T_pM\oplus N_pM\to T_pM$ and $\to N_pM$ for $p\in M$ (Definition 5.33); both are linear on each fibre and smooth (Proposition 6.17). Extensions exist locally (Fact 6.15) and the definitions are extension-independent (Remark 6.18), as the prompt allows us to assume.

**1. $\tilde\nabla$ is a connection.** Let $\tilde X,\tilde Y,\tilde Z$ be vector fields on $M$ with extensions $X,Y,Z$, and $f\in C^\infty(M)$ with an extension $F$ to a neighbourhood in $N$ (extend using a slice chart). *Linearity in $\tilde Y$:* $(\nabla_X(\lambda Y+\mu Z))^\top=\lambda(\nabla_XY)^\top+\mu(\nabla_XZ)^\top$ by linearity of $\nabla$ and of $\top$. *Leibniz:* $FY$ extends $f\tilde Y$, and $(\nabla_X(FY))^\top=(X(F)Y+F\nabla_XY)^\top=X(F)|_M\,Y^\top+F|_M(\nabla_XY)^\top=\tilde X(f)\,\tilde Y+f\,\tilde\nabla_{\tilde X}\tilde Y$, using $Y^\top=\tilde Y$ along $M$ (as $Y$ is tangent there) and $X(F)|_M=\tilde X(f)$ (as $X$ is tangent to $M$ along $M$ and $F|_M=f$). *$C^\infty(M)$-linearity in $\tilde X$:* $FX+GZ$ extends $f\tilde X+g\tilde Z$, and $(\nabla_{FX+GZ}Y)^\top=F(\nabla_XY)^\top+G(\nabla_ZY)^\top$ along $M$ by the ambient axiom and linearity of $\top$. The result is a smooth section of $TM$ by Proposition 6.17.

**2. Torsion-free.** *Claim: if $X,Y$ are tangent to $M$ along $M$, then $[X,Y]$ is tangent to $M$ along $M$ and $[X,Y]|_M=[\tilde X,\tilde Y]$.* Work in a slice chart $(x^1,\dots,x^n)$ of $N$ around $p\in M$ in which $M=\{x^{m+1}=\dots=x^n=0\}$. Tangency means $X^\alpha=Y^\alpha=0$ on $M$ for $\alpha>m$. Then $[X,Y]^\alpha=X^i\partial_iY^\alpha-Y^i\partial_iX^\alpha$. On $M$: for $i\le m$, $\partial_i$ is tangent to $M$ and $Y^\alpha\equiv0$ on $M$, so $\partial_iY^\alpha=0$ there; for $i>m$, $X^i=0$ on $M$. Each term vanishes on $M$, so $[X,Y]^\alpha=0$ on $M$: the bracket is tangent. For a function $f$ on $M$ with extension $F$, $[X,Y](F)|_M=X(Y(F))|_M-Y(X(F))|_M=\tilde X(\tilde Y f)-\tilde Y(\tilde Xf)=[\tilde X,\tilde Y]f$, using that $Y(F)|_M=\tilde Y f$ and then tangency of $X$ again. Hence $[X,Y]|_M=[\tilde X,\tilde Y]$.

Now $\tilde\nabla_{\tilde X}\tilde Y-\tilde\nabla_{\tilde Y}\tilde X=(\nabla_XY-\nabla_YX)^\top=([X,Y])^\top=[X,Y]|_M=[\tilde X,\tilde Y]$, since $\nabla$ is torsion-free and $[X,Y]$ is already tangent. So $\tilde\nabla$ is torsion-free.

**3. Metric.** For $\tilde X,\tilde Y,\tilde Z$ on $M$, $g(\tilde Y,\tilde Z)=h(Y,Z)|_M$ and $\tilde X\big(g(\tilde Y,\tilde Z)\big)=X\big(h(Y,Z)\big)|_M$ (tangency of $X$). By compatibility of $\nabla$ with $h$, this equals $h(\nabla_XY,Z)+h(Y,\nabla_XZ)$ along $M$. Since $Z$ is tangent along $M$, $h(\nabla_XY,Z)=h((\nabla_XY)^\top,Z)+h((\nabla_XY)^\perp,Z)=g(\tilde\nabla_{\tilde X}\tilde Y,\tilde Z)+0$, and similarly for the other term. Hence $\tilde X g(\tilde Y,\tilde Z)=g(\tilde\nabla_{\tilde X}\tilde Y,\tilde Z)+g(\tilde Y,\tilde\nabla_{\tilde X}\tilde Z)$.

**4. Conclusion.** By Theorem 6.9 the Levi-Civita connection of $(M,g)$ is the unique torsion-free metric connection on $TM$; $\tilde\nabla$ is one, so $\tilde\nabla$ is the Levi-Civita connection of $(M,g)$ (Theorem 6.20). Symmetry of $B$: $B(\tilde X,\tilde Y)-B(\tilde Y,\tilde X)=(\nabla_XY-\nabla_YX)^\perp=([X,Y])^\perp=0$ because $[X,Y]$ is tangent along $M$. ($C^\infty$-bilinearity of $B$, Proposition 6.21, follows from part 1: the Leibniz terms are tangent and are killed by $\perp$.)

**5. The sphere.** For $N=\mathbb{R}^3$, $h=\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle$ and $\nabla=\partial$ (Proposition 6.1 plus uniqueness). At $p\in S^2_R$ the normal space is $\mathbb{R}p$ with unit normal $p/R$, so $v^\perp=R^{-2}\langle v,p\rangle p$. Let $X,Y$ be tangent to $S^2_R$ (extended arbitrarily). Along the sphere $\langle Y,p\rangle=0$, where $p$ denotes the position vector field, whose directional derivative is $\partial_Xp=X$. Differentiating along the tangent field $X$:
$$0=X\langle Y,p\rangle=\langle\partial_XY,p\rangle+\langle Y,\partial_Xp\rangle=\langle\partial_XY,p\rangle+\langle Y,X\rangle,$$
so $\langle\partial_XY,p\rangle=-\langle X,Y\rangle$ on $S^2_R$. Therefore
$$B(X,Y)=(\partial_XY)^\perp=\frac{1}{R^2}\langle\partial_XY,p\rangle p=-\frac{1}{R^2}\langle X,Y\rangle p,\qquad \tilde\nabla_XY=\partial_XY-(\partial_XY)^\perp=\partial_XY+\frac{1}{R^2}\langle X,Y\rangle p .$$
Sanity checks: $B$ is symmetric and tensorial, as proved; for a unit-speed great circle $\gamma$ with $X=Y=\dot\gamma$, $\partial_{\dot\gamma}\dot\gamma=\ddot\gamma=-R^{-2}\gamma$ (circular motion of radius $R$ at unit speed), and the formula gives $\tilde\nabla_{\dot\gamma}\dot\gamma=-R^{-2}\gamma+R^{-2}\gamma=0$: great circles are geodesics, anticipating unit 3. In the spherical chart one can also check that $\tilde\nabla_{\partial_\varphi}\partial_\varphi=\partial_\varphi\partial_\varphi F+R^{-2}|\partial_\varphi F|^2F=-\sin\theta\cos\theta\,\partial_\theta F$, matching $\Gamma^\theta_{\varphi\varphi}$ from exercise 1.

**Alternative valid approaches.** Proving tangency of the bracket via flows (the flow of a tangent field preserves $M$), or via the characterisation of $T_pM$ as the kernel of the differentials of the defining functions. Direct verification that $(\partial_XY)^\top$ satisfies the Koszul formula for $g$ (longer but acceptable).

**Common gaps.** Omitting the proof that $[X,Y]$ is tangent; forgetting that $f$ must be extended before the Leibniz rule can be applied; writing the sphere formula with a minus sign (confusing $B$ with $-B$); normalising the normal vector incorrectly ($R^{-1}$ instead of $R^{-2}$).
