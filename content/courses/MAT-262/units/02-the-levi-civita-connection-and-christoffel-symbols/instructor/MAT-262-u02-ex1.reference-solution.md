## Reference reasoning (instructor only)

**Conventions.** $x^1=\theta$, $x^2=\varphi$; $\nabla_{\partial_i}\partial_j=\Gamma^k_{ij}\partial_k$ (direction in the first lower index); $g_{ij}=\mathrm{diag}(R^2,R^2\sin^2\theta)$ on $W$, where $0<\theta<\pi$.

**1. Inverse metric.** $(g^{ij})=\mathrm{diag}\big(R^{-2},(R^2\sin^2\theta)^{-1}\big)$.

**2. Symbols.** The only coefficient with a non-zero derivative is $g_{\varphi\varphi}$, and $\partial_\theta g_{\varphi\varphi}=2R^2\sin\theta\cos\theta$; $\partial_\varphi$ of every coefficient vanishes, as does $\partial_\theta g_{\theta\theta}=\partial_\theta g_{\theta\varphi}=0$. Since $(g^{kl})$ is diagonal, the sum over $l$ in $\Gamma^k_{ij}=\tfrac12g^{kl}(\partial_ig_{jl}+\partial_jg_{il}-\partial_lg_{ij})$ reduces to $l=k$:
$$\Gamma^\theta_{\varphi\varphi}=\tfrac12g^{\theta\theta}\big(\partial_\varphi g_{\varphi\theta}+\partial_\varphi g_{\varphi\theta}-\partial_\theta g_{\varphi\varphi}\big)=\tfrac12R^{-2}\big(-2R^2\sin\theta\cos\theta\big)=-\sin\theta\cos\theta,$$
$$\Gamma^\varphi_{\theta\varphi}=\Gamma^\varphi_{\varphi\theta}=\tfrac12g^{\varphi\varphi}\big(\partial_\theta g_{\varphi\varphi}+\partial_\varphi g_{\theta\varphi}-\partial_\varphi g_{\theta\varphi}\big)=\tfrac12\frac{2R^2\sin\theta\cos\theta}{R^2\sin^2\theta}=\cot\theta .$$
The remaining symbols vanish because each term in their formula is a derivative that is zero: $\Gamma^\theta_{\theta\theta}=\tfrac12g^{\theta\theta}\partial_\theta g_{\theta\theta}=0$; $\Gamma^\theta_{\theta\varphi}=\Gamma^\theta_{\varphi\theta}=\tfrac12g^{\theta\theta}(\partial_\theta g_{\varphi\theta}+\partial_\varphi g_{\theta\theta}-\partial_\theta g_{\theta\varphi})=0$; $\Gamma^\varphi_{\theta\theta}=\tfrac12g^{\varphi\varphi}(2\partial_\theta g_{\theta\varphi}-\partial_\varphi g_{\theta\theta})=0$; $\Gamma^\varphi_{\varphi\varphi}=\tfrac12g^{\varphi\varphi}\partial_\varphi g_{\varphi\varphi}=0$.

So $\nabla_{\partial_\theta}\partial_\theta=0$, $\nabla_{\partial_\theta}\partial_\varphi=\nabla_{\partial_\varphi}\partial_\theta=\cot\theta\,\partial_\varphi$, $\nabla_{\partial_\varphi}\partial_\varphi=-\sin\theta\cos\theta\,\partial_\theta$.

**3. Checks.** Symmetry $\Gamma^k_{ij}=\Gamma^k_{ji}$ holds in the table (the only mixed symbols are $\Gamma^\varphi_{\theta\varphi}=\Gamma^\varphi_{\varphi\theta}$ and $\Gamma^\theta_{\theta\varphi}=\Gamma^\theta_{\varphi\theta}=0$). Metric compatibility in the instance requested: $2g(\nabla_{\partial_\theta}\partial_\varphi,\partial_\varphi)=2\cot\theta\,g_{\varphi\varphi}=2\cot\theta\cdot R^2\sin^2\theta=2R^2\sin\theta\cos\theta=\partial_\theta g_{\varphi\varphi}$, as compatibility requires ($\partial_\theta g_{\varphi\varphi}=g(\nabla_{\partial_\theta}\partial_\varphi,\partial_\varphi)+g(\partial_\varphi,\nabla_{\partial_\theta}\partial_\varphi)$). A second instance: $\partial_\varphi g_{\theta\theta}=0$ and $2g(\nabla_{\partial_\varphi}\partial_\theta,\partial_\theta)=2\cot\theta\,g(\partial_\varphi,\partial_\theta)=0$.

**4. The blow-up of $\cot\theta$.** Christoffel symbols are not tensor components; they are the coefficients of $\nabla$ in the frame $\{\partial_\theta,\partial_\varphi\}$, and this frame degenerates at the poles ($\partial_\varphi\to0$), so unbounded coefficients say nothing about $\nabla$. Intrinsically, by Theorem 6.20 the Levi-Civita connection of $S^2_R\subseteq\mathbb{R}^3$ is $\tilde\nabla_XY=(\partial_XY)^\top$, the tangential projection of the Euclidean directional derivative; both $\partial$ and the orthogonal projection onto $T_pS^2_R=p^\perp$ (namely $v\mapsto v-R^{-2}\langle v,p\rangle p$) depend smoothly on $p$ over the whole sphere, poles included. So $\nabla$ is smooth everywhere, and the singular coefficient is a coordinate artefact exactly like $\Gamma^\theta_{r\theta}=1/r$ for polar coordinates on the plane. (A learner who instead computes the symbols in the graph chart at the pole and finds them smooth has given a correct but coordinate-based reason; the prompt asked for an intrinsic one.)

**5. Scaling.** Write the Koszul formula for $\tilde g=c\,g$: $2\tilde g(\tilde\nabla_XY,Z)=c\big(Xg(Y,Z)+Yg(Z,X)-Zg(X,Y)+g(Z,[X,Y])+g([Z,X],Y)+g([Z,Y],X)\big)=2c\,g(\nabla_XY,Z)=2\tilde g(\nabla_XY,Z)$ for all $Z$; non-degeneracy gives $\tilde\nabla=\nabla$. In coordinates, $\tilde g^{kl}=c^{-1}g^{kl}$ and $\partial_i\tilde g_{jl}=c\,\partial_ig_{jl}$, so the factors cancel in $\Gamma^k_{ij}$. This is why $R$ disappears from the table: the metric of $S^2_R$ in the chart is $R^2$ times that of the unit sphere.

**Common gaps.** A sign error in $\Gamma^\theta_{\varphi\varphi}$ (writing $+\sin\theta\cos\theta$); forgetting $g^{\varphi\varphi}$ carries $\sin^{-2}\theta$ and obtaining $\sin\theta\cos\theta$ for $\Gamma^\varphi_{\theta\varphi}$; asserting "all symbols vanish because the metric is diagonal"; treating the blow-up as a singularity of the connection; proving the scaling only in the example.
