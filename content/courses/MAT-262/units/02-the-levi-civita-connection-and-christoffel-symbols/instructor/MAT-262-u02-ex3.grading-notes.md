## Grading notes (instructor only)

This is an explanation exercise; grade the ideas, not the prose.

- Roles in uniqueness: metric compatibility is applied three times (to $(X,Y,Z)$, $(Y,Z,X)$, $(Z,X,Y)$); torsion-freeness converts $\nabla_YX$, $\nabla_ZX$, $\nabla_ZY$ into $\nabla_XY$, $\nabla_XZ$, $\nabla_YZ$ plus brackets, which is what makes the unknown terms cancel when the three identities are combined. Either description, or "the symmetric part comes from the metric condition and the antisymmetric part from torsion-freeness", is acceptable (Observation 6.5, Theorem 6.9).
- Why neither alone suffices: every connection on $\mathbb{R}^n$ is $\partial_XY+A(X,Y)$ with $A$ a $(1,2)$-tensor; it is torsion-free iff $A$ is symmetric and metric iff $\langle A(X,Y),Z\rangle$ is antisymmetric in $(Y,Z)$. Each condition leaves a large family.
- Metric with torsion: on $\mathbb{R}^3$, $\nabla_XY=\partial_XY+X\times Y$. Metric because $\langle X\times Y,Z\rangle+\langle Y,X\times Z\rangle=\det(X,Y,Z)+\det(Y,X,Z)=0$; torsion $T(X,Y)=X\times Y-Y\times X=2X\times Y\neq0$. (Any constant antisymmetric $A$ with $\langle A(X,Y),Z\rangle$ alternating works; in $\mathbb{R}^n$, $A(X,Y)=\omega(X,Y,\cdot)^\sharp$ for a 3-form $\omega$.)
- Torsion-free but not metric: $\nabla_XY=\partial_XY+X(f)Y+Y(f)X$ for a non-constant smooth $f$ (a projective change). Symmetric in $(X,Y)$, hence torsion-free; $g(\nabla_XY,Z)+g(Y,\nabla_XZ)-X\langle Y,Z\rangle=2X(f)\langle Y,Z\rangle+Y(f)\langle X,Z\rangle+Z(f)\langle X,Y\rangle$, non-zero in general (take $X=Y=Z$ with $X(f)\neq0$). Simpler alternatives: $\partial_XY+\langle X,Y\rangle v$ for a fixed non-zero vector $v$ (symmetric; $\langle A(X,Y),Z\rangle=\langle X,Y\rangle\langle v,Z\rangle$ is not antisymmetric in $(Y,Z)$).
- A learner who gives $\partial_XY+X(f)Y$ (not symmetric, not metric) has an example of neither; point this out precisely. Keep feedback under 120 words.
