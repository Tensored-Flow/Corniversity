## Outline: Riemannian submanifolds and the Gauss equation

This unit is **outlined, not yet prepared**. It will be written from Gudmundsson's Definitions 5.33 and 6.19, Theorem 6.20, Proposition 6.21, Example 6.23, Proposition 6.24, Definition 7.24 and Theorem 8.19 with its corollaries (version 1.438, reference-only), with Wendl §6.2 and Sternberg chapter 1 as open-licensed alternatives and Lee chapter 8 as the commercial reference.

**Scope.** The normal bundle of a submanifold and its metric connection; the second fundamental form $B$ as a symmetric tensor with values in the normal bundle, and for hypersurfaces the shape operator and principal curvatures; the Gauss equation $g(\tilde R(X,Y)Z,W)-h(R(X,Y)Z,W)=h(B(Y,Z),B(X,W))-h(B(X,Z),B(Y,W))$ (Theorem 8.19) and its consequence that the sectional curvature of a submanifold of Euclidean space is $h(B(X,X),B(Y,Y))-|B(X,Y)|^2$ for orthonormal $X,Y$; the theorema egregium (Gaussian curvature of a surface in $\mathbb{R}^3$ is intrinsic) as the two-dimensional case; the round sphere of radius $R$ as the running example, with $B(X,Y)=-R^{-2}\langle X,Y\rangle p$ from unit 2 giving $K=R^{-2}$; totally geodesic and minimal submanifolds.

**What preparation will add.** A full lesson with definitions, examples and non-examples, the main theorems with their hypotheses in view, original exercises with hint ladders and rubrics, instructor reference reasoning for the assessor, an exit check and the unit's common misconceptions. Until then the reading list above is accurate and the tutor can discuss the material, but there is no assessed work for this unit.
