## Outline: Rigid bodies I — the inertia tensor and Euler's equations

This unit is **outlined, not yet prepared**. It will be written from Chapter 2 (Rigid Body Dynamics) of the MIT 8.09 Fall 2014 notes (CC BY-NC-SA 4.0; chapter PDF located, page-level locators to be added when the unit is prepared) and Chapter 3 (The Motion of Rigid Bodies) of David Tong's *Classical Dynamics* notes (reference-only), with the angular-momentum passage of 8.09 Chapter 1, Section 1.3 (PDF page 13) as the starting point.

**Scope.** Kinematics of a rigid body: the body frame, angular velocity $\boldsymbol\omega$ and $\dot{\mathbf r} = \boldsymbol\omega\times\mathbf r$; kinetic energy $T = \tfrac12\boldsymbol\omega^T\mathsf I\boldsymbol\omega$ and the inertia tensor $\mathsf I$ as a symmetric positive-definite tensor (units $\mathrm{kg\,m^2}$); principal axes, the parallel-axis theorem, and the inertia tensors of standard bodies; angular momentum $\mathbf L = \mathsf I\boldsymbol\omega$ and why $\mathbf L$ and $\boldsymbol\omega$ are generally not parallel; Euler's equations in the body frame and the torque-free motion of symmetric and asymmetric tops, including the stability of rotation about the principal axes (the intermediate-axis instability). Limiting cases: a point mass, a thin rod, a sphere.

**What preparation will add.** A full lesson, original exercises (an inertia-tensor computation with a principal-axis check, a torque-free Euler-equation problem with a stability analysis, an explain-back), instructor reference reasoning, an exit check and the unit's common misconceptions. Until then the reading list is accurate and the tutor can discuss the material, but there is no assessed work for this unit.
