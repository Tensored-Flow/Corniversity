## Reference reasoning (instructor only)

**1. Lagrangian.** Cylindrical coordinates $(r,\theta,z)$ with $z$ vertical. The constraint $z = r^2/(2a)$ gives $\dot z = r\dot r/a$. Kinetic energy $T = \tfrac12 m(\dot r^2 + r^2\dot\theta^2 + \dot z^2) = \tfrac12 m\big[(1 + r^2/a^2)\dot r^2 + r^2\dot\theta^2\big]$; potential $V = mgz = mgr^2/(2a)$. So
$$L = \tfrac12 m\Big[\Big(1+\frac{r^2}{a^2}\Big)\dot r^2 + r^2\dot\theta^2\Big] - \frac{mg r^2}{2a}.$$
Units: $r^2/a^2$ is dimensionless; $m\dot r^2$ and $m r^2\dot\theta^2$ are $\mathrm{kg\,m^2\,s^{-2}} = \mathrm J$; $mgr^2/a$ is $\mathrm{kg\cdot m\,s^{-2}\cdot m^2/m} = \mathrm J$.

**2. Equations of motion.** $\theta$: $\partial L/\partial\theta = 0$, so $\frac{d}{dt}(m r^2\dot\theta) = 0$ and $\ell \equiv m r^2\dot\theta$ is constant — the vertical component of angular momentum, conserved because $\theta$ is absent from $L$ (rotational symmetry about the axis).

$r$: $\partial L/\partial\dot r = m(1+r^2/a^2)\dot r$, whose time derivative is $m(1+r^2/a^2)\ddot r + 2m r\dot r^2/a^2$; $\partial L/\partial r = m r\dot r^2/a^2 + m r\dot\theta^2 - mgr/a$. Hence
$$\Big(1+\frac{r^2}{a^2}\Big)\ddot r + \frac{r}{a^2}\dot r^2 - r\dot\theta^2 + \frac{g}{a}r = 0 .$$
Every term has units $\mathrm{m\,s^{-2}}$.

**3. Circular orbits.** With $\dot r = \ddot r = 0$: $-r_0\dot\theta^2 + (g/a)r_0 = 0$, so
$$\dot\theta^2 = \frac{g}{a}, \qquad \omega = \sqrt{g/a},\qquad T = 2\pi\sqrt{a/g},$$
*independent of $r_0$*: every circular orbit in the bowl has the same period, the period of a pendulum of length $a$. (The surprise is the isochronism; the reason is that $V = \tfrac12 m (g/a) r^2$ is exactly harmonic in $r$ while the kinetic correction $(r^2/a^2)\dot r^2$ vanishes on circles.) From $\ell = m r_0^2\omega$: $r_0^2 = \ell\sqrt{a/g}/m$, i.e.
$$r_0 = \Big(\frac{\ell^2 a}{m^2 g}\Big)^{1/4}.$$
Units: $\ell^2 a/(m^2 g)$ has units $(\mathrm{kg\,m^2\,s^{-1}})^2\,\mathrm m/(\mathrm{kg^2\,m\,s^{-2}}) = \mathrm m^4$, so $r_0$ is in metres.

**4. Limit $a\to\infty$.** The surface flattens to the plane $z = 0$; $\omega\to 0$ and, for fixed $\ell$, $r_0\to\infty$. On a flat frictionless plane there is no horizontal force, hence no centripetal force, hence no circular orbit at finite radius: the particle moves in a straight line. Consistent.

**Optional extension (not required for acceptance).** Small radial oscillations about a circular orbit at fixed $\ell$: substitute $\dot\theta = \ell/(m r^2)$, linearise $r = r_0 + \epsilon$ and drop $\dot r^2$ (second order). The restoring term is $\big[-3\ell^2/(m^2 r_0^4) - g/a\big]\epsilon = -4(g/a)\epsilon$ using $\ell^2/(m^2 r_0^4) = \omega^2 = g/a$, so
$$\ddot\epsilon = -\frac{4(g/a)}{1 + r_0^2/a^2}\,\epsilon,\qquad \omega_r = \frac{2\sqrt{g/a}}{\sqrt{1+r_0^2/a^2}}.$$
For $r_0\ll a$, $\omega_r \to 2\omega$: the radial oscillation runs at twice the orbital frequency, so nearly circular orbits close after one revolution, as they must for the isotropic two-dimensional harmonic oscillator that the bowl becomes near its bottom.

**Alternative valid approaches.** Working with $z$ as the coordinate instead of $r$ ($r^2 = 2az$) is fine; the circular-orbit condition then reads $\dot\theta^2 = g/a$ as well. Using energy conservation plus $\ell$ to get an effective potential $V_{\rm eff}(r) = \ell^2/(2mr^2) + mgr^2/(2a)$ and minimising it is an acceptable route to $r_0$ (it gives $r_0^4 = \ell^2 a/(m^2 g)$ directly).

**Common gaps.** Forgetting the $\dot z^2$ term in $T$ (then the $r$ equation lacks the $(1+r^2/a^2)$ and $\dot r^2$ terms; the circular-orbit result is unaffected, which hides the error — check for it explicitly); writing $d/dt$ of $m(1+r^2/a^2)\dot r$ without the $2mr\dot r^2/a^2$ term; stating the conserved quantity without naming the cyclic coordinate as the reason; no units.
