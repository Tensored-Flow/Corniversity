## Reference reasoning (instructor only)

**1. Constraint.** The contact point is instantaneously at rest, so the centre moves at $\dot x = a\dot\phi$: in differential form $dx - a\,d\phi = 0$ with $a_x = 1$, $a_\phi = -a$, $a_t = 0$. The coefficients are constant, so the integrability conditions hold trivially and the constraint integrates to $x - a\phi = x_0$: holonomic. (One could therefore eliminate $\phi$ and write $L = \tfrac12 m(1+\beta)\dot x^2 + mgx\sin\alpha$, which gives the acceleration but not the friction force — the point of keeping both coordinates.)

**2. Lagrangian and multiplier equations.** Taking $x$ downhill from the starting point and the potential $V = -mgx\sin\alpha$,
$$L = \tfrac12 m\dot x^2 + \tfrac12\beta m a^2\dot\phi^2 + mgx\sin\alpha .$$
The multiplier equations $\frac{d}{dt}\frac{\partial L}{\partial\dot q_j} - \frac{\partial L}{\partial q_j} = \lambda a_j$ read
$$x:\quad m\ddot x - mg\sin\alpha = \lambda,\qquad \phi:\quad \beta m a^2\ddot\phi = -\lambda a .$$

**3. Solution.** From the $\phi$ equation $\lambda = -\beta m a\ddot\phi = -\beta m\ddot x$ (using $\ddot\phi = \ddot x/a$). Substituting, $m\ddot x - mg\sin\alpha = -\beta m\ddot x$, so
$$\ddot x = \frac{g\sin\alpha}{1+\beta},\qquad \lambda = -\frac{\beta\,mg\sin\alpha}{1+\beta}.$$
The term $\lambda a_x = \lambda$ in the $x$ equation is the generalized constraint force along $x$: negative, i.e. *up* the slope, of magnitude $\beta mg\sin\alpha/(1+\beta)$. It is the static friction force at the contact point; $\lambda a_\phi = -\lambda a$ is its torque about the centre. Units: $\lambda$ is $\mathrm{kg\,m\,s^{-2}} = \mathrm N$, as a coefficient of a dimensionless $a_x$ must be. Static friction does no work: the contact point has zero velocity, and the total mechanical energy $\tfrac12 m(1+\beta)\dot x^2 - mgx\sin\alpha$ is conserved.

**4. Limits.** $\beta\to 0$: $\ddot x\to g\sin\alpha$ and $\lambda\to 0$ — a point mass (no rotational inertia) slides as if frictionless, since no torque is needed to spin it. Ordering: $\ddot x = g\sin\alpha/(1+\beta)$ is largest for the smallest $\beta$, so the solid sphere ($\beta = 2/5$, $\ddot x = \tfrac57 g\sin\alpha$) beats the disc ($\tfrac23 g\sin\alpha$), which beats the hoop ($\tfrac12 g\sin\alpha$). Independent of $m$ and $a$.

**5. No-slip condition.** The required friction $|\lambda| = \beta mg\sin\alpha/(1+\beta)$ must not exceed $\mu N = \mu mg\cos\alpha$:
$$\tan\alpha \le \mu\,\frac{1+\beta}{\beta}.$$
For the disc, $\tan\alpha\le 3\mu$; for the hoop, $\tan\alpha\le 2\mu$; for the sphere, $\tan\alpha\le \tfrac72\mu$. Beyond this angle the body slides as well as rolls and the constraint must be replaced by a kinetic-friction generalized force.

**Alternative valid approaches.** Newton's laws with torque about the contact point ($I_{\rm contact} = (1+\beta)ma^2$) give the same acceleration in one line; accept it as a check but the exercise asks for the multiplier route. Choosing $x$ positive uphill flips signs consistently. Writing the constraint as $\dot\phi - \dot x/a = 0$ rescales $\lambda$ by $-1/a$ (then $\lambda$ has units of torque); accept, provided the force is identified correctly.

**Common gaps.** Sign of $a_\phi$ inconsistent with the constraint as written; calling $\lambda$ the normal force (the normal force is perpendicular to the slope and does not enter these equations because the coordinate normal to the plane was eliminated); claiming friction dissipates energy (flagged by the automatic check); forgetting that the ordering is independent of mass and radius.
