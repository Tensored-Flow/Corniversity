## Reference reasoning (instructor only)

**1. Lagrangian and invariance.** $L = \tfrac12 m(\dot r^2 + r^2\dot\theta^2 + \dot z^2) - U(r,\theta - kz)$. Under $\theta\to\theta+\epsilon$, $z\to z+\epsilon/k$: $\theta - kz\to \theta + \epsilon - kz - \epsilon = \theta - kz$, and the velocities $\dot r,\dot\theta,\dot z$ are unchanged because $\epsilon$ is constant. Hence $\delta T = 0$ and $\delta U = 0$: $\delta L = 0$ exactly, not only to first order.

**2. Noether's theorem.** The generator is $K_r = 0$, $K_\theta = 1$, $K_z = 1/k$, so
$$Q = \sum_i p_iK_i = p_\theta + \frac{p_z}{k} = m r^2\dot\theta + \frac{m\dot z}{k}.$$
Any nonzero multiple is equivalent; $kQ = kp_\theta + p_z$ is the tidier form. Units of $Q$: $\mathrm{kg\,m^2\,s^{-1}}$ (angular momentum); $p_z/k$ has $\mathrm{kg\,m\,s^{-1}}/\mathrm{m^{-1}}$, consistent.

Direct verification. Writing $U_2$ for $\partial U/\partial(\text{second argument})$: $\dot p_\theta = \partial L/\partial\theta = -U_2$ and $\dot p_z = \partial L/\partial z = -U_2\cdot(-k) = kU_2$. Then $\dot Q = -U_2 + kU_2/k = 0$. $\checkmark$

**3. Separate momenta and the $k\to 0$ limit.** $\dot p_\theta = -U_2$ and $\dot p_z = kU_2$ vanish identically only if $U_2 \equiv 0$, i.e. $U$ depends on $r$ alone; then both $\theta$ and $z$ are cyclic and both momenta are conserved separately. For $U_2\neq 0$ only the combination survives: the symmetry group is one-dimensional (a helix), so there is one conserved charge. As $k\to 0$ the potential $U(r,\theta - kz)\to U(r,\theta)$ no longer depends on $z$, translation along $z$ becomes a symmetry, and $kQ = kp_\theta + p_z\to p_z$, which is indeed conserved in that limit. (As $k\to\infty$ with fixed helix shape the limit is not meaningful; do not expect one.)

**4. Hamiltonian.** $L$ has no explicit time dependence, so $dH/dt = -\partial L/\partial t = 0$: $H$ is conserved. $T$ is homogeneous of degree two in $(\dot r,\dot\theta,\dot z)$ (cylindrical coordinates are a time-independent transformation of Cartesian ones) and $U$ is velocity independent, so $H = T + V = E$. Both hypotheses are needed and both hold here.

**Alternative valid approaches.** A learner may find $Q$ by changing to adapted coordinates $\psi = \theta - kz$, $\zeta = z$ (then $\zeta$ is cyclic and $p_\zeta = \partial L/\partial\dot\zeta = m r^2\dot\theta\,k + m\dot z = kQ$); accept this as the cleanest route and ask for the Noether derivation as well, since the exercise asks for it. Also acceptable: Cartesian components with $L_z = m(x\dot y - y\dot x)$ for $p_\theta$.

**Common gaps.** Writing $K_z = k$ instead of $1/k$ (dimensional check catches it: $Q$ must have a single unit); forgetting the chain-rule factor $-k$ in $\dot p_z$; asserting $H = E$ without naming the hypotheses (flagged by the automatic check); claiming $p_\theta$ alone is conserved "because the potential is helically symmetric".
