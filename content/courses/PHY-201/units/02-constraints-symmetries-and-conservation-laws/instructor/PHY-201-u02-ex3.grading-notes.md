## Grading notes (instructor only)

Explanation exercise; grade the ideas and keep feedback under 120 words.

- Must contain: $H$ is conserved iff $L$ (equivalently $H$) has no explicit time dependence, $dH/dt = -\partial L/\partial t$; $H = T + V$ requires $T$ homogeneous of degree two in the velocities and $V$ velocity independent; these are different conditions, so each of "H conserved" and "E conserved" can hold without the other.
- Expected example: the spring on the cart. In the wall coordinate $x$, $H = E$ but $\partial L/\partial t\neq 0$: the agent that keeps the cart moving at constant speed does work on the mass–spring system, so $E$ changes. In the cart coordinate $x'$, $H'$ is conserved but equals the cart-frame energy minus the constant $\tfrac12 mv_0^2$, not $E$. Other correct examples: a bead on a rotating hoop (time-dependent coordinate transformation makes $H\neq E$, $H$ conserved, $E$ not — the motor does work); a charged particle in a static magnetic field ($H = E$ conserved; velocity-dependent potential but $\mathbf A\cdot\dot{\mathbf r}$ is linear so $H = T$).
- Common conceptual error to correct precisely: "the Hamiltonian is defined as the total energy". Reply with the definition $H = \sum p\dot q - L$ and the theorem's hypotheses.
- Do not require the explicit formula for $H'$.
