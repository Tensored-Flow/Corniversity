## Outline: Hamiltonian mechanics — phase space and Poisson brackets

This unit is **outlined, not yet prepared**. It will be written from the Hamiltonian passages of Chapter 1 of the MIT 8.09 Fall 2014 notes (Section 1.2, PDF pages 8–9, Eqs. 1.24–1.33: canonical momenta, the Legendre transformation, Hamilton's equations, when $H = E$; Section 1.5, PDF page 23, Eqs. 1.81–1.82: Hamilton's equations from a phase-space action), the opening of Chapter 4 (Canonical Transformations, Hamilton-Jacobi Equations, and Action-Angle Variables), and Chapter 4 (The Hamiltonian Formulation) of David Tong's *Classical Dynamics* notes (reference-only).

**Scope.** The Legendre transformation and why $H(q,p,t)$ rather than $H(q,\dot q,t)$; Hamilton's equations as $2N$ first-order equations and their derivation both from the Lagrangian and from the phase-space action $\int(p\dot q - H)\,dt$; phase space, flows and Liouville's theorem (conservation of phase-space volume, with the harmonic oscillator and the free particle as pictures); Poisson brackets, their algebra (antisymmetry, Leibniz rule, Jacobi identity), $\dot F = \{F,H\} + \partial_tF$, conserved quantities as functions that Poisson-commute with $H$, and the angular-momentum algebra $\{L_i,L_j\} = \epsilon_{ijk}L_k$; the correspondence with quantum mechanics stated as an outlook only.

**What preparation will add.** A full lesson, original exercises (a Legendre transformation for a Lagrangian with a velocity-dependent potential, a Poisson-bracket proof, an explain-back on Liouville's theorem), instructor reference reasoning, an exit check and the unit's common misconceptions. Until then the reading list is accurate and the tutor can discuss the material, but there is no assessed work for this unit.
