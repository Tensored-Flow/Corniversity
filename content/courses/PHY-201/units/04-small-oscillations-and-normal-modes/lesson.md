## Outline: Small oscillations and normal modes

This unit is **outlined, not yet prepared**. It will be written from Chapter 3 (Vibrations & Oscillations) of the MIT 8.09 Fall 2014 notes (CC BY-NC-SA 4.0; chapter PDF located, page-level locators to be added when the unit is prepared) and Section 2.6 (Small Oscillations and Stability) of David Tong's *Classical Dynamics* notes (reference-only), whose worked examples are the double pendulum and the linear triatomic molecule.

**Scope.** Equilibrium and its stability from the Hessian of $V$; expansion of $T$ and $V$ to second order and the Lagrangian $L = \tfrac12\dot{\boldsymbol\eta}^T\mathsf T\dot{\boldsymbol\eta} - \tfrac12\boldsymbol\eta^T\mathsf V\boldsymbol\eta$; the generalized eigenvalue problem $\mathsf V\mathbf a = \omega^2\mathsf T\mathbf a$, why its eigenvalues are real and why $\mathsf T$-orthogonality replaces ordinary orthogonality; normal coordinates and the decoupling of the motion; zero modes and their meaning; the double pendulum and a triatomic molecule as examples; damped and forced oscillations as a bridge to the chaos unit. Units and limiting cases (equal masses, weak coupling) checked throughout.

**What preparation will add.** A full lesson, original exercises (a normal-mode calculation with an explicit frequency check in a limiting case, a proof about the generalized eigenvalue problem, an explain-back), instructor reference reasoning, an exit check and the unit's common misconceptions. Until then the reading list is accurate and the tutor can discuss the material, but there is no assessed work for this unit.
