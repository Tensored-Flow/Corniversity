## Outline: Canonical transformations and generating functions

This unit is **outlined, not yet prepared**. It will be written from Chapter 4 (Canonical Transformations, Hamilton-Jacobi Equations, and Action-Angle Variables) of the MIT 8.09 Fall 2014 notes (CC BY-NC-SA 4.0; chapter PDF located, page-level locators to be added when the unit is prepared) and the canonical-transformation sections of Chapter 4 of David Tong's *Classical Dynamics* notes (reference-only).

**Scope.** Transformations $(q,p)\to(Q,P)$ that preserve the form of Hamilton's equations; the four types of generating function and the Legendre transformations relating them, with the identity transformation, point transformations (unit 1 revisited) and the exchange $Q = p$, $P = -q$ as examples; the symplectic condition $M^T\mathsf JM = \mathsf J$ for the Jacobian and the invariance of Poisson brackets, $\{Q_i,P_j\} = \delta_{ij}$, as the practical test; the harmonic oscillator solved by a canonical transformation to an angle variable; infinitesimal canonical transformations, conserved quantities as generators of symmetries (Noether in Hamiltonian form), and Liouville's theorem as a corollary of the symplectic condition.

**What preparation will add.** A full lesson, original exercises (verifying that a given transformation is canonical by two methods, constructing a generating function, an explain-back), instructor reference reasoning, an exit check and the unit's common misconceptions. Until then the reading list is accurate and the tutor can discuss the material, but there is no assessed work for this unit.
