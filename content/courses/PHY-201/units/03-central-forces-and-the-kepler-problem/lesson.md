## Outline: Central forces and the Kepler problem

This unit is **outlined, not yet prepared**. It will be written from Chapter 4 (Central Forces) of David Tong's *Dynamics and Relativity* notes and Section 2.5.4 (Two Body Problem) of his *Classical Dynamics* notes, both reference-only (free to read on the author's page, no licence stated, nothing copied), together with the scaling-symmetry discussion in Section 1.3 of the MIT 8.09 Chapter 1 notes (PDF page 14), which derives Kepler's third law $T^2\propto a^3$ from the homogeneity of the Coulomb-type potential.

**Scope.** Reduction of the two-body problem to one body of reduced mass $\mu = m_1m_2/(m_1+m_2)$ moving in the relative coordinate; conservation of angular momentum and the effective potential $V_{\rm eff}(r) = \ell^2/(2\mu r^2) + V(r)$; classification of orbits by energy; the orbit equation in $u = 1/r$ and the conic sections for $V = -k/r$; Kepler's three laws, with the third law also obtained from the scaling symmetry of unit 2; precession as the signature of a non-$1/r$ potential; scattering by a central force and the Rutherford cross-section. SI units throughout; the gravitational and Coulomb cases are kept in parallel.

**What preparation will add.** A full lesson carrying the derivations through, original exercises with hint ladders and rubrics (an effective-potential problem, an orbit-equation derivation and an explain-back), instructor reference reasoning, an exit check and the unit's common misconceptions. Until then the reading list is accurate and the tutor can discuss the material, but there is no assessed work for this unit.
