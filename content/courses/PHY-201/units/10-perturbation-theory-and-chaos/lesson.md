## Outline: Perturbation theory and the onset of chaos

This unit is **outlined, not yet prepared**. It will be written from Chapter 5 (Perturbation Theory) and Chapter 7 (Chaos and Non-Linear Dynamics) of the MIT 8.09 Fall 2014 notes (CC BY-NC-SA 4.0; chapter PDFs located, page-level locators to be added when the unit is prepared). The 8.09 readings page maps its chaos lectures to Strogatz, *Nonlinear Dynamics and Chaos*, a commercial book that Corniversity does not require; the notes are sufficient.

**Scope.** Canonical perturbation theory in action–angle variables: first-order shifts of frequencies, the problem of small denominators at resonance, and the regime in which the expansion is trustworthy; time-dependent perturbation theory with the driven oscillator as the example; adiabatic invariants revisited from the perturbative side; qualitative dynamics — fixed points, their linear stability and bifurcations; the damped driven pendulum as the route to chaos, with sensitive dependence on initial conditions and the Poincaré section as the diagnostic; the KAM picture of what survives when an integrable system is perturbed, stated without proof; the logistic map as a one-dimensional model of period doubling. Every approximation names what it neglects.

**What preparation will add.** A full lesson, original exercises (a first-order frequency shift for an anharmonic oscillator checked against an exact result, a fixed-point stability analysis, an explain-back on what "chaos" does and does not mean for a deterministic system), instructor reference reasoning, an exit check and the unit's common misconceptions. Until then the reading list is accurate and the tutor can discuss the material, but there is no assessed work for this unit.
