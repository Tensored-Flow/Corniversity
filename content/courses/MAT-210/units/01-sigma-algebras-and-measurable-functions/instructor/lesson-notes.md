## Lesson notes (instructor only)

Source mapping, 18.125 Fall 2003 notes (LaTeX by Ethan Brown from Jeff Viaclovsky's handwritten lectures; no printed page labels; PDF pages are index + 1). Lecture 1 (3 pages): σ-algebra axioms (σ1)–(σ3), contrast with a topology, definition of measurable function and Proposition 0.1 on page 1; Theorem 0.2, Examples (a)–(d), Proposition 0.3 on page 2; Borel sets, $F_\sigma$, $G_\delta$ on page 3. Lecture 2 (4 pages): Proposition 0.1 (a)–(c), lim sup on page 1; Proposition 0.2, Theorem 0.3, Corollary 0.4 on page 2; $f^\pm$, simple functions, Theorem 0.5, positive measure and the simple integral on page 3; integral of nonnegative functions on page 4.

The notes are terse and restart numbering in each lecture: always say "lecture 2, Proposition 0.1" in tutorials. The notes state the $(\alpha,\infty]$ criterion for $\alpha\in[-\infty,\infty]$; the lesson states it for real $\alpha$, which is equivalent and is what exercise 2 refines to rational $\alpha$.

Teaching intent: install the good-sets principle and the habit of asking "which union is countable here?". For an Imperial-trained learner the σ-algebra axioms are familiar; spend the tutorial time on exercise 2's sup argument, where the uncountability hypothesis must be used twice. If the learner finishes early, ask them whether the characterisation survives for $f:X\to[-\infty,\infty]$ (yes, with $c\in[-\infty,\infty]$, but the proof of nonemptiness of $S$ changes) and for $f:X\to\mathbb C$ (yes, componentwise).

Forward references used deliberately: the existence of a non-measurable set (lecture 9, unit 5) and of Lebesgue measurable non-Borel sets (lecture 8, unit 5). Label them as cited, not proved.
