## Grading notes (instructor only)

This is an explain-back; grade the ideas and the honesty of labelling, not the prose.

- Countable versus finite: the expected instance is Theorem 0.3 of lecture 2, $\{\sup_nf_n>\alpha\}=\bigcup_n\{f_n>\alpha\}$, or Corollary 0.4 (pointwise limits), or that $\sigma(\mathcal F)$ must contain countable unions of generators (e.g. a countable set in the countable–co-countable σ-algebra). Any correct instance from the unit counts. "Finite unions would not let us take limits" without an instance is partially met.
- Countable versus arbitrary: every subset of $\mathbb R$ is the union of its singletons, so closure under arbitrary unions plus singletons forces $\mathcal M=\mathcal P(\mathbb R)$; the forward reference (unit 5, lecture 9: a non-measurable set exists for any translation-invariant extension of length) is what makes this a problem. The learner must say that the non-existence result is cited, not proved here.
- A learner who instead argues via the uncountable supremum of indicators (misconception 4 of the unit) has given a correct alternative; accept.
- Do not penalise the absence of the phrase "σ-additivity"; the course has not yet defined a measure. Do flag any claim that finite additivity "extends automatically" to countable additivity.
- Keep feedback under 120 words.
