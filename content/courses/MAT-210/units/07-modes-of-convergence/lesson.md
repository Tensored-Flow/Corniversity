## Outline: Modes of convergence

This unit is **outlined, not yet prepared**. It will be written from lecture 13 of the MIT 18.125 Fall 2003 notes (CC BY-NC-SA 4.0), citing each statement by lecture, number and PDF page once the lecture PDF has been opened and its page indices verified.

**Scope.** Egoroff's theorem: on a space of finite measure, almost-everywhere convergence is uniform off a set of arbitrarily small measure, with the finiteness hypothesis shown necessary; convergence in measure and its basic properties; almost-everywhere convergence implies convergence in measure when the measure is finite; convergence in measure yields a subsequence converging almost everywhere; the dominated convergence theorem with convergence in measure in place of pointwise convergence. The standard diagram of implications between a.e., in-measure and $L^1$ convergence, with the typewriter sequence and moving bumps as counterexamples, will anchor the lesson.

**What preparation will add.** A full lesson with definitions, the implication diagram with proofs and counterexamples, original exercises with hint ladders and rubrics, instructor reference reasoning, an exit check and common misconceptions. Until then the reading list above is accurate and the tutor can discuss the material, but there is no assessed work for this unit.
