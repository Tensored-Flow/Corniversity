## Lesson notes (instructor only)

Source mapping, 18.125 Fall 2003 notes (PDF pages are index + 1; no printed labels). Lecture 2: positive measure and the simple integral on page 3, the supremum definition and $f^+-f^-$ on page 4. Lecture 3: Riemann material on page 1 (unit 3, not this unit); counting measure and Dirac mass, Theorem 0.2 and Properties (a)–(f) on pages 2–3. Lecture 4: Proposition 0.1 on page 1, Theorem 0.2 (MCT) on page 2, Theorem 0.3, Corollary 0.4 and Lemma 0.5 on page 3, Fatou's proof on page 4.

Two places where the lesson adds what the notes omit: the independence of the simple integral from the representation (the notes define it with disjoint $A_i$ and move on), and the counterexample for continuity from above (the notes state the finiteness hypothesis without an example). Theorem 0.2(1) in the notes is misprinted "$E\subset\mathbb R$"; the lesson states it correctly.

Teaching intent: the proof of the monotone convergence theorem is the one proof of the course the learner must be able to reproduce on demand, because dominated convergence, additivity, Fatou and the completeness of $L^p$ are all reductions to it. Run the tutorial on exercise 2 part 1 until the learner can say in one sentence why the subtraction needs finiteness. If they are fast, ask for the version of exercise 2 part 1 where $f_1$ may be infinite on a null set, and let them discover they need "almost everywhere" (unit 3).

Peer discussion seed (if peers are enabled): a classmate claims "the limit of the integrals is the integral of the limit whenever the limit exists". The tutor must resolve it with the moving bump and with the tail indicators, naming the hypotheses of MCT and Fatou.
