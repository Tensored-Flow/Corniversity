## Reference reasoning (instructor only)

All values come from the laboratory at its default settings and seed 42. They were recomputed with `packages/core/src/labs/choice-models.ts` on 2026-10-31 and are given as the laboratory prints them. A learner who changes the seed will see different numbers. Judge the reasoning, and check numbers only against the learner's own stated seed. Where a pattern holds only at this seed, the text says so.

**1. Informative case** (300 trials, arms $0.2$ and $0.8$).

Parameter recovery used 8 settings with 3 repetitions each:

| Measure | Value |
|---|---|
| Correlation between true and recovered $\alpha$ | 0.974 |
| Correlation between true and recovered $\beta$ | 0.828 |
| Mean absolute error in $\alpha$ | 0.051 |
| Mean absolute error in $\beta$ | 1.29 |

The worst setting is $\alpha=0.9$, $\beta=8$. There recovered $\beta$ overshoots by about $7.9$ on average, from fits of 12.8, 20.0 and 15.0. With a fast learner and a high inverse temperature, choices quickly become almost deterministic. The likelihood then barely changes as $\beta$ increases further, so the maximum drifts towards the grid's upper bound of 20.

Model recovery used 20 datasets per model:

| Generating model | Selected as bias | Selected as win-stay lose-shift | Selected as Rescorla-Wagner |
|---|---|---|---|
| Bias | 19 | 1 | 0 |
| Win-stay lose-shift | 0 | 20 | 0 |
| Rescorla-Wagner | 0 | 0 | 20 |

The matrix is diagonal-dominant. It licenses this statement: in this design and region, BIC selects the generating model almost always. It does not show that any real participant uses one of these models.

**2. Ambiguous case** (60 trials, arms $0.4$ and $0.6$, participants from the low-$\beta$ region).

Parameter recovery used 6 settings with 3 repetitions each:

| Measure | Value |
|---|---|
| Correlation between true and recovered $\alpha$ | 0.413 |
| Correlation between true and recovered $\beta$ | 0.225 |

The errors are large. For example, $\beta$ is overestimated by about $1.2$ at $\beta=0.5$ with $\alpha=0.2$ or $0.5$, though hardly at all with $\alpha=0.8$.

| Generating model | Selected as bias | Selected as win-stay lose-shift | Selected as Rescorla-Wagner |
|---|---|---|---|
| Bias | 16 | 4 | 0 |
| Win-stay lose-shift | 6 | 13 | 1 |
| Rescorla-Wagner | 11 | 7 | 2 |

The matrix is not diagonal-dominant, and it is the learning model that fails. Data from biased responding and from win-stay lose-shift are mostly recovered as themselves, 16 and 13 of 20. Rescorla-Wagner data are recovered as Rescorla-Wagner only twice.

Here is why:

- With $\beta$ at most $1.5$ and small differences between the values, softmax probabilities stay near $\frac12$.
- With $\varepsilon$ from $0.6$ to $0.95$, the win-stay lose-shift rule is followed with probability only $0.525$ to $0.70$.
- A Rescorla-Wagner learner from this region therefore makes choices close to those of a biased responder or a noisy rule-follower.
- The one-parameter models then usually win on the BIC penalty.

The participants' region drives this, not the short session or the close arms. Model recovery was run over seeds 1 to 5, with 20 datasets per model:

| Design and participant region | RW data recovered as RW |
|---|---|
| As built | 19 of 100 |
| Arms 0.2 and 0.8 | 13 of 100 |
| 300 trials | 42 of 100 |
| Participants from the informative region, 60 trials, arms 0.4 and 0.6 | 79 of 100 |

A learner who tests these with the laboratory's design controls should find the same pattern. The case licenses only this claim: this design and region cannot tell the learning model from the others. No participant's fitted $\alpha$ or $\beta$ should be interpreted.

**3. Held-out comparison** (8 synthetic Rescorla-Wagner participants drawn from each case's region). Values are mean log-likelihood per held-out trial; chance is $-0.693$.

| Case and split | Held-out trials | Bias | Win-stay lose-shift | Rescorla-Wagner |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Informative, later trials of the same participants | 720 | −0.260 | −0.501 | −0.188 |
| Informative, participants left out of the fit | 2400 | −0.336 | −0.498 | −0.227 |
| Ambiguous, later trials of the same participants | 144 | −0.728 | −0.669 | −0.674 |
| Ambiguous, participants left out of the fit | 480 | −0.693 | −0.696 | −0.699 |

In the informative case Rescorla-Wagner beats the baseline and chance on both splits. Leaving participants out costs it about 0.04 per trial, because one shared parameter set must serve people it has not seen. In the ambiguous case every model is at or near chance. At this seed Rescorla-Wagner is slightly behind the baseline for participants left out; across seeds the difference is a few hundredths of a nat per trial at most, in either direction. The small differences on later trials rest on 144 trials.

The contract for participants left out of the fit, as the laboratory states it:

1. **Observed:** choices and rewards of several synthetic participants, each generated with individual parameters.
2. **Prediction target:** every choice of the participant left out of the fit.
3. **History in training:** all trials of the other participants, with one shared parameter set per model.
4. **History at inference:** the held-out participant's own earlier trials, but none of their data in the fit.
5. **Person:** unseen.
6. **Task:** familiar.
7. **Later time periods:** no.
8. **Unavailable in real use:** none. The fit uses other participants' data and the prediction uses the held-out participant's own earlier trials, both available in real use.
9. **Simplest baseline:** biased random responding fitted on the other participants.
10. **Claims not tested:** another task, later sessions of the same people, and any population other than the simulated one.

The learner writes this contract before running the split, then compares. The points most often wrong are 4 (the held-out participant's own earlier trials are used) and 8 (nothing used here would be missing in real use).

**4. The leaking split.** In the informative case at seed 42, random trials give these values over 737 held-out trials:

| Model | Mean log-likelihood per trial |
|---|---|
| Rescorla-Wagner | −0.229 |
| Bias | −0.346 |
| Win-stay lose-shift | −0.518 |

Every model scores lower here than on later trials of the same participants. The leak is not visible in the numbers, and need not be. The contract is what shows it: trials after each held-out trial were used to fit the parameters that predict it, which no real forecast could do. Whatever the numbers are, they are not evidence about predicting future behaviour.
