## Reference reasoning (instructor only)

**1. Win-stay lose-shift estimate.** Trial 1 is a coin flip and contributes $\ln\frac12$ whatever $\varepsilon$ is. Each of the $n=200$ later trials follows the rule with probability $1-\varepsilon/2$ and breaks it with probability $\varepsilon/2$, so

$$\ell(\varepsilon)=\ln\tfrac12+c\ln\!\big(1-\tfrac{\varepsilon}{2}\big)+(n-c)\ln\tfrac{\varepsilon}{2}.$$

Differentiating,

$$\ell'(\varepsilon)=-\frac{c}{2-\varepsilon}+\frac{n-c}{\varepsilon}=0\iff (n-c)(2-\varepsilon)=c\,\varepsilon\iff \varepsilon=\frac{2(n-c)}{n}.$$

The second derivative is negative on $(0,2)$, so this is the maximum. It lies in $[0,1]$ exactly when $c\ge n/2$. If fewer than half the later trials follow the rule, $\ell$ increases on all of $[0,1]$ and the constrained estimate is $\hat\varepsilon=1$, meaning the participant follows the rule no more often than chance. Here $c=170\ge100$, so

$$\hat\varepsilon=\frac{2\cdot30}{200}=0.30.$$

Check: the predicted rule-following probability is $1-\hat\varepsilon/2=0.85=170/200$, the observed proportion.

**2. Rescorla-Wagner values.** Only arm 1 is chosen and updated:

- after trial 1, $Q_1=0.5+0.5(1-0.5)=0.75$;
- after trial 2, $Q_1=0.75+0.5(0-0.75)=0.375$;
- after trial 3, $Q_1=0.375+0.5(1-0.375)=0.6875$.

$Q_0$ is never chosen and stays at $0.5$.

**3. Choice probability.**

$$P(c_4=1)=\frac{1}{1+e^{-4(0.6875-0.5)}}=\frac{1}{1+e^{-0.75}}=\frac{1}{1+0.47237}=0.6792.$$

**4. BIC.** With $\ln200=5.29832$:

- Rescorla-Wagner: $\mathrm{BIC}=2(120.5)+2\ln200=241+10.597=251.60$;
- win-stay lose-shift: $\mathrm{BIC}=2(124.0)+1\cdot\ln200=248+5.298=253.30$.

BIC prefers Rescorla-Wagner, by $1.70$.

**5. What it licenses.** The difference is computed in sample, on the session the models were fitted to, and it is small. It says that, after the complexity penalty, Rescorla-Wagner predicts this session's choices slightly better than win-stay lose-shift. It does not say which process generated the choices. A stronger statement would need, first, model recovery in this design and parameter region: how often BIC selects each model when the generating model is known. It would also need validation by simulation, checking that the fitted model reproduces the model-independent measures, and ideally a held-out comparison with a stated contract.
