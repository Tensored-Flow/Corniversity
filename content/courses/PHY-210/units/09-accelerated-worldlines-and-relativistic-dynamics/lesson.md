## Outline: Accelerated worldlines and relativistic dynamics

This unit is **outlined, not yet prepared**. It will be written mainly from Section 7.4 of Tong's *Dynamics and Relativity* notes — 7.4.5 Newton's Laws of Motion and 7.4.6 Acceleration (reference-only; nothing copied) — with Sections 5.6–5.8 of Taylor and Wheeler's Chapter 5 (wristwatch time along a worldline, kinked worldline, stretch factor; CC BY 4.0, chapter PDF located) and the MIT 8.20 Week 3 video Lecture 9.4 Forces and Kinetic Energy as supplements.

**Scope.** Proper time along an arbitrary timelike worldline, $\tau = \int\sqrt{1 - v^2/c^2}\,dt$, and the four-velocity and four-acceleration as derivatives with respect to $\tau$; the relativistic form of Newton's second law $dP^\mu/d\tau = F^\mu$ and the relation of the four-force to the three-force, with the consequence that force and acceleration are not parallel in general; constant proper acceleration and the hyperbolic worldline $x^2 - c^2t^2 = c^4/\alpha^2$, its proper time and the Rindler horizon as a flat-spacetime phenomenon; the relativistic rocket and the twin paradox with a smooth turn-around (Chapter 5 revisited); work and kinetic energy, $dE/dt = \mathbf F\cdot\mathbf v$. Units are carried throughout and every result is checked in the limit $v\ll c$. Acceleration here is special relativity: inertial frames remain the background and no gravity enters.

**What preparation will add.** A full lesson, original exercises (the hyperbolic worldline derived from $A\cdot A = \alpha^2$ with its Newtonian limit, a relativistic rocket problem with proper time to a distant star, an explain-back on why acceleration poses no problem for special relativity), instructor reference reasoning, an exit check and the unit's common misconceptions. Until then the reading list is accurate and the tutor can discuss the material, but there is no assessed work for this unit.
