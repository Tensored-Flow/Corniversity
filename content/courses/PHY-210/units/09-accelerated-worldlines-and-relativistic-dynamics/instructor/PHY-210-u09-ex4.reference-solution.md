# When a four-force is not orthogonal to velocity — reference reasoning

Here $t=\tau$, $U=(c,\mathbf0)$, $A=0$, $P=(mc,\mathbf0)$ and $K=(\lambda c,\mathbf0)$. Thus $K\cdot U=-\lambda c^2$, whereas $mA=0$. Differentiating $mU$ gives $K=mA+\dot mU$; the constant-mass hypothesis failed. Incoming radiation supplies energy $\lambda c^2$ per second and zero net momentum in this rest frame. Including the radiation source and fields restores closed-system conservation. A rocket alone is likewise open, but its exhaust generally carries directional momentum; mass loss cannot be represented solely by substituting a changing mass into the constant-mass force equation.
