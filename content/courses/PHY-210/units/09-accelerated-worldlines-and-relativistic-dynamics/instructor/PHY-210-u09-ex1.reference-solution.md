# A braking force is not a constant coordinate deceleration — reference reasoning

Initially $\gamma_0=5/4$, $p_0=3mc/4$, $E_0=5mc^2/4$. Since $dp/dt=-F$, $t_s=p_0/F=3mc/(4F)$. The work identity gives $-Fx_s=mc^2-E_0$, hence $x_s=mc^2/(4F)$. Rapidity starts at $\theta_0=\operatorname{artanh}(3/5)=\log2$. In collinear motion $dp/dt=mc\,d\theta/d\tau=-F$, so $\tau_s=(mc/F)\log2$ and $|a_*|=F/m$. For general $v_0$, $t_s=m\gamma_0v_0/F$, $x_s=(\gamma_0-1)mc^2/F$, $\tau_s=(mc/F)\operatorname{artanh}(v_0/c)$. Their low-speed limits are $mv_0/F$, $mv_0^2/(2F)$ and $mv_0/F$. Coordinate acceleration is $-F/(m\gamma^3)$, not $-F/m$ except at low speed.

## Assessment note

Accept any correct alternative derivation with equivalent stated conventions. A numerical check supports a calculation; it does not prove a general claim. Diagnose the first substantive gap, and do not equate help-assisted work with independent demonstration.
