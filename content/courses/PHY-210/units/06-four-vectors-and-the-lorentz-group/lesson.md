## Outline: Four-vectors and the Lorentz group

This unit is **outlined, not yet prepared**. It will be written mainly from Sections 7.3.2 (The Lorentz Group) and 7.4 (Relativistic Kinematics, including 7.4.1 Proper Time and 7.4.7 Indices Up, Indices Down) of Tong's *Dynamics and Relativity* notes (reference-only; nothing copied), with Section 7.2 (Momenergy Arrow) of Taylor and Wheeler's Chapter 7 (CC BY 4.0; chapter PDF located) and the MIT 8.20 Week 3 videos (Lecture 8.1 Algebra of Lorentz Transformations, Lecture 8.2 Introduction to 4-Vector Notation, Lecture 8.3 Proper Velocity) as supplements.

**Scope.** The position four-vector $X^\mu = (ct,\mathbf x)$ and the Minkowski inner product $X\cdot Y = \eta_{\mu\nu}X^\mu Y^\nu$ with $\eta = \mathrm{diag}(-1,+1,+1,+1)$ (Tong uses the opposite sign; the dictionary is stated); the Lorentz group as the set of matrices with $\Lambda^T\eta\Lambda = \eta$, its dimension, its four components, boosts and rotations as generators, and why two boosts compose to a boost plus a rotation; four-vectors defined by their transformation law; the four-velocity $U^\mu = dX^\mu/d\tau = \gamma(c,\mathbf v)$ with $U\cdot U = -c^2$ (derivation and units), the four-acceleration and $A\cdot U = 0$; raising and lowering indices with $\eta$; the gradient as a covector. The treatment is that of a mathematician: invariants first, components second.

**What preparation will add.** A full lesson, original exercises (a proof that the Lorentz group has dimension six and four components, a four-velocity/four-acceleration calculation with the hyperbolic worldline, an explain-back on covariance versus invariance), instructor reference reasoning, an exit check and the unit's common misconceptions. Until then the reading list is accurate and the tutor can discuss the material, but there is no assessed work for this unit.
