# An accelerated body at constant speed — reference reasoning

$\gamma=5/4$,$\mathbf v=(-R\omega\sin\omega t,R\omega\cos\omega t,0)$ and $\mathbf a=-\omega^2\mathbf x$. Thus $U=\gamma(c,\mathbf v)$,$A=(0,\gamma^2\mathbf a)$ because $d/d\tau=\gamma d/dt$ and $\gamma$ is constant. The first norm is $\gamma^2(-c^2+v^2)=-c^2$, and $U\cdot A=\gamma^3\mathbf v\cdot\mathbf a=0$. Proper acceleration is $\gamma^2v^2/R=(25/16)(9/25)c^2/R=9c^2/(16R)$. The tangent $U$ remains timelike. A is its rate of change, not a displacement or velocity worldline, so its spacelike norm is compatible with subluminal motion.
