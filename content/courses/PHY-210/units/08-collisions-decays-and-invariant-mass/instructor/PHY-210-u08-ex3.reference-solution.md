# A photon loses energy but momentum still balances — reference reasoning

The Compton formula yields $E\prime=mc^2/[1+(1-1/2)]=2mc^2/3$ with $m=m_e$. The electron kinetic energy is $mc^2/3$ and total energy $4mc^2/3$. Incoming momentum is $(mc,0)$; outgoing photon momentum is $(mc/3,\sqrt3\,mc/3)$. Electron momentum is therefore $(2mc/3,-\sqrt3\,mc/3)$, with magnitude $\sqrt7\,mc/3$. The mass shell is $(4/3)^2-[(2/3)^2+(\sqrt3/3)^2]=1$. Speed is $\sqrt7\,c/4$ and direction is below $+x$ at angle $\arctan(\sqrt3/2)$. The electron already possessed rest energy before scattering; the photon energy loss changes its kinetic energy, while its invariant rest mass remains m.
