## Reference reasoning (instructor only)

**1. Derivation.** Linearity (with coincident origins): $ct' = A\,ct + Bx$, $x' = C\,ct + Dx$. The origin of $S'$ ($x' = 0$) moves as $x = vt$ in $S$, so $C\,ct + D\,vt = 0$ for all $t$: $C = -D\,v/c$, i.e. $x' = D(x - vt)$. Invariance for all $(t,x)$:
$$-(A\,ct + Bx)^2 + D^2(x - vt)^2 = -c^2t^2 + x^2 .$$
Coefficient of $x^2$: $-B^2 + D^2 = 1$. Coefficient of $t^2$: $-A^2c^2 + D^2v^2 = -c^2$. Coefficient of $tx$: $-2ABc - 2D^2v = 0$, so $AB = -D^2v/c$. From the first two, $D^2 = 1 + B^2$ and $A^2 = 1 + D^2v^2/c^2$. Substituting into $A^2B^2 = D^4v^2/c^2$: $(1 + D^2v^2/c^2)(D^2 - 1) = D^4v^2/c^2$, which simplifies to $D^2(1 - v^2/c^2) = 1$, so $D = \gamma$ (positive root: the identity at $v = 0$). Then $B^2 = \gamma^2 - 1 = \gamma^2v^2/c^2$ and $A^2 = 1 + \gamma^2v^2/c^2 = \gamma^2$; the sign of $B$ is fixed by $AB = -\gamma^2v/c$ with $A = \gamma > 0$: $B = -\gamma v/c$. Hence
$$t' = \gamma\Big(t - \frac{vx}{c^2}\Big),\qquad x' = \gamma(x - vt).$$
Units: $vx/c^2$ is $(\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}})(\mathrm m)/(\mathrm{m^2\,s^{-2}}) = \mathrm s$; both terms in the bracket are times. The book's route (Special Topic L.3–L.5) fixes $D$ and $A$ first from the clock at the rocket origin and then $B$ from the cross term; either order is fine.

**2. Hyperbolic form.** $\gamma^2 - (\gamma v/c)^2 = \gamma^2(1 - v^2/c^2) = 1$, and $\gamma \ge 1$, so $\gamma = \cosh\phi$, $\gamma v/c = \sinh\phi$ for a unique real $\phi$ with $\tanh\phi = v/c$. Then
$$\Lambda(\phi) = \begin{pmatrix}\cosh\phi & -\sinh\phi\\ -\sinh\phi & \cosh\phi\end{pmatrix},\qquad \Lambda^T\eta\Lambda = \begin{pmatrix} -\cosh^2\phi + \sinh^2\phi & \cosh\phi\sinh\phi - \sinh\phi\cosh\phi\\ \cdot & -\sinh^2\phi + \cosh^2\phi\end{pmatrix} = \eta .$$

**3. Composition.** With $c_i = \cosh\phi_i$, $s_i = \sinh\phi_i$: the $(1,1)$ entry of $\Lambda(\phi_1)\Lambda(\phi_2)$ is $c_1c_2 + s_1s_2 = \cosh(\phi_1+\phi_2)$ and the $(1,2)$ entry is $-c_1s_2 - s_1c_2 = -\sinh(\phi_1+\phi_2)$; the other entries follow by symmetry. (Equivalently $\Lambda(\phi) = e^{-\phi K}$ with $K = \begin{pmatrix}0&1\\1&0\end{pmatrix}$, and exponentials of multiples of one matrix multiply by adding exponents.) Velocities: $w/c = \tanh(\phi_1+\phi_2) = \dfrac{\tanh\phi_1 + \tanh\phi_2}{1 + \tanh\phi_1\tanh\phi_2}$, i.e. $w = \dfrac{u + v}{1 + uv/c^2}$. Since $\tanh$ maps $\mathbb R$ onto $(-1,1)$, $|w| < c$ for all finite rapidities, i.e. whenever $|u|,|v| < c$. Check: $u = v = 0.6c$ gives $w = 1.2c/1.36 = 0.882c$, not $1.2c$.

**4. Limits.** For $v\ll c$: $\gamma = 1 + v^2/2c^2 + \dots\to 1$ and $vx/c^2\to 0$ (relative to $t$ for $x\lesssim ct$), giving $t' = t$, $x' = x - vt$: the Galilean transformation; $w\approx u + v$ when $uv\ll c^2$. The neglected terms are $O(v^2/c^2)$ in $\gamma$ and $O(vx/c^2)$ in $t'$.

**Alternative valid approaches.** Diagonalising $\Lambda$ in light-cone coordinates $x^\pm = ct\pm x$, where a boost is $x^\pm\to e^{\mp\phi}x^\pm$, gives parts 2–3 in two lines; accept gladly. Deriving $D = \gamma$ from a clock at the origin (book's Step 1) rather than from the full coefficient comparison is acceptable if the cross-term condition is still used for $B$.

**Common gaps.** Taking the negative root for $D$; sign of $B$ unexplained; verifying $\Lambda^T\eta\Lambda = \eta$ only for the diagonal entries; "proving" composition by quoting that boosts form a group; forgetting the bound $|w|<c$.
