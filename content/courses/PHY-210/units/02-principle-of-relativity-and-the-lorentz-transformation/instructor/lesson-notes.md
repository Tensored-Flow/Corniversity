## Lesson notes (instructor only)

Source mapping, Taylor and Wheeler, *Spacetime Physics* 2nd ed. (CC BY 4.0 scans). Chapter 3 (printed = PDF index + 53): 3.1 Principle of Relativity pp. 53–55; 3.2 pp. 56–59; 3.3 pp. 60–61 (Sample Problem 3-1 on p. 62 lists what is and is not the same); 3.4 Relativity of Simultaneity pp. 62–63 (Figure 3-1 on p. 63); 3.5 Lorentz Contraction pp. 63–64 (measured, not seen: p. 64); 3.6 Invariance of Transverse Dimension pp. 65–67 (Figures 3-2, 3-3); 3.7 Invariance of the Interval Proved pp. 67–70 (Figures 3-4 to 3-6, Table 3-1 p. 70); 3.8 super-rocket pp. 71–72; 3.9 Summary pp. 73–75. Special Topic (printed = index + 95): L.1 p. 95; L.2 pp. 96–98; L.3 First Steps p. 99 (Eqs. L-1 to L-3); L.4 Form of the Lorentz Transformation p. 100 (L-4, L-5); L.5 Completing the Derivation p. 101 (L-6 to L-9); result (L-10a,b) and L.6 Inverse p. 102; L.7 Addition of Velocities pp. 103–110; L.8 Summary p. 111. The book sets $c = 1$ and writes $v_{\rm rel}$ for our $v/c$; its (L-10) is our inverse transformation. Tong, Dynamics and Relativity, 7.1 and 7.3 (reference-only) give the matrix form with $\eta = \mathrm{diag}(+1,-1,-1,-1)$. MIT 8.20 Week 2 (Lectures 4.4, 4.5, 5.2, 6.1) is optional.

Teaching intent: the learner will find the coefficient derivation routine; the physics to press is (i) exactly which hypotheses enter the light-clock proof, (ii) that length measurement is a simultaneity statement, and (iii) that velocity non-additivity is a theorem about $\tanh$, not a mystery. Do the pole-and-barn consistency check ($6.40 + 3.60 = 10.0$) live; it converts the "paradox" into arithmetic.

Watch for: $D$ negative root; "each sees the other's clocks slow — contradiction"; treating transverse invariance as obvious (the book's argument is worth a minute); mixing the book's $v_{\rm rel}$ ($=v/c$) with SI $v$.

Staged misconception for a peer discussion, if peers are enabled: a classmate may say "the pole really is 8 m while moving, because length contraction is physical". Resolve by asking what measurement defines "length" and in which frame the two end-measurements are simultaneous.
