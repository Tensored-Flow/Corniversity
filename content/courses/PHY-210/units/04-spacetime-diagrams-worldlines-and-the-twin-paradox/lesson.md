## Outline: Spacetime diagrams, worldlines and the twin paradox

This unit is **outlined, not yet prepared**. It will be written from Chapters 4 (Trip to Canopus) and 5 (Trekking through Spacetime) of Taylor and Wheeler's *Spacetime Physics* (2nd ed., CC BY 4.0; chapter PDFs located, page-level locators to be added when the unit is prepared), with Section 7.1.2 (Spacetime Diagrams) of Tong's *Dynamics and Relativity* (reference-only) and the MIT 8.20 Week 2 videos (Lecture 5.3 Spacetime Diagrams, Lecture 6.2 Twin Paradox) as supplements.

**Scope.** The spacetime map with time vertical and the light cone at $45^\circ$ when $c = 1$ (5.1–5.2); the invariant hyperbola $-c^2t^2 + x^2 = \text{const}$ as the locus of events at fixed interval from the origin and the calibration of a moving frame's axes (5.3); worldlines (5.4); "length" along a worldline as accumulated proper time, $\tau = \int\sqrt{1 - v(t)^2/c^2}\,dt$, and why a straight timelike worldline *maximises* proper time between two events — the reverse of the Euclidean triangle inequality (5.5–5.6); the kinked worldline and the twin paradox resolved both by the proper-time integral and by relativity of simultaneity at the turn-around (5.7; Chapter 4's trip to Canopus, 4.6 Twin Paradox, 4.9 Relativity of Simultaneity, 4.10 Experimental Evidence); the stretch factor $\gamma$ read off the diagram (5.8). Signature $(-,+,+,+)$ throughout; the book's "interval" is $\sqrt{-\Delta s^2}$.

**What preparation will add.** A full lesson, original exercises (a diagram-based analysis of a round trip with numbers, a proof that the straight worldline maximises proper time, an explain-back on what is asymmetric between the twins), instructor reference reasoning, an exit check and the unit's common misconceptions. Until then the reading list is accurate and the tutor can discuss the material, but there is no assessed work for this unit.
