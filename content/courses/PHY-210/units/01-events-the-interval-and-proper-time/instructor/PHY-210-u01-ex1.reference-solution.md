## Reference reasoning (instructor only)

Convention $(-,+,+,+)$: $\Delta s^2 = -c^2\Delta t^2 + \Delta x^2$.

**1.** $c\Delta t = 33.36\ \mathrm{ns}\times 0.299\,792\,458\ \mathrm{m\,ns^{-1}} = 10.001\ \mathrm m \approx 10.0\ \mathrm m$ (three figures). $\Delta s^2 = -(10.0)^2 + (6.00)^2 = -100 + 36.0 = -64.0\ \mathrm{m^2}$. Negative: timelike. (In the book's convention the interval is $+64\ \mathrm{m^2}$, "8 metres of timelike interval".)

**2.** A clock present at both sparks must cover $6.00\ \mathrm m$ in $10.0\ \mathrm m$ of light-travel time: $v/c = 6.00/10.0 = 0.600$. In its frame $\Delta x' = 0$, so $-c^2\tau^2 = \Delta s^2$ and $c\tau = \sqrt{64.0}\ \mathrm m = 8.00\ \mathrm m$, i.e. $\tau = 8.00/0.299\,792\,458 = 26.7\ \mathrm{ns}$. This is the proper time (wristwatch time) between the sparks. Cross-check with the lesson's formula: $\Delta t\sqrt{1 - 0.36} = 10.0\times0.800 = 8.00\ \mathrm m$. $\checkmark$

**3.** No. In a frame where the sparks are simultaneous, $\Delta t' = 0$ and $\Delta s^2 = \Delta x'^2 \ge 0$; but $\Delta s^2 = -64\ \mathrm{m^2} < 0$ in every frame. Timelike pairs have a frame-independent time order.

**4(a).** $\Delta x = 10.0\ \mathrm m$: $\Delta s^2 = 0$, lightlike. The co-moving speed would be $v = c$, which no clock attains; no proper time is defined (or it is zero along a light ray). No frame makes them simultaneous either ($\Delta s^2 = 0$ would require $\Delta x' = 0 = \Delta t'$, the same event). The sparks can be connected by a single light flash.

**4(b).** $\Delta x = 12.0\ \mathrm m$: $\Delta s^2 = -100 + 144 = +44\ \mathrm{m^2}$, spacelike. No co-moving frame ($v$ would be $1.2c$). A frame moving at $v = c^2\Delta t/\Delta x$, i.e. $v/c = 10.0/12.0 = 0.833$, sees them simultaneous; in it $\Delta x' = \sqrt{44}\ \mathrm m = 6.63\ \mathrm m$, the proper distance. (With the Lorentz transformation of unit 2: $\Delta t' = \gamma(\Delta t - v\Delta x/c^2) = 0$ gives the same $v$; $\Delta x' = \gamma(\Delta x - v\Delta t) = (12 - 10\cdot 10/12)/\sqrt{1 - 100/144} = (12 - 8.333)/0.5528 = 6.63\ \mathrm m$. $\checkmark$)

**Alternative valid approaches.** Working entirely in the book's convention is fine if stated. Using $\gamma = 1/\sqrt{1-v^2/c^2} = 1.25$ and $\tau = \Delta t/\gamma$ for part 2 is fine. For 4(b), a learner who has not met the Lorentz transformation may cite the lesson's formula $v = c^2\Delta t/\Delta x$ and then get $\Delta x'$ from invariance; both routes are acceptable.

**Common gaps.** Forgetting to convert nanoseconds to metres (then adding $\mathrm{ns^2}$ to $\mathrm m^2$); reporting $\tau$ in metres only when the question asks for nanoseconds too; concluding that a lightlike pair has a co-moving frame "moving at $c$"; in 4(b), giving $v = 1.2c$ as if it were a frame.
