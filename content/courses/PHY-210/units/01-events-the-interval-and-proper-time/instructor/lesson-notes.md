## Lesson notes (instructor only)

Source mapping, Taylor and Wheeler, *Spacetime Physics* 2nd ed. (CC BY 4.0 scans; printed page = PDF index + 1 in Chapter 1): Section 1.1 pp. 1–4; Section 1.2 pp. 5–8 with Table 1-3 (p. 6), Eqs. (1-4)–(1-5) (p. 6), Table 1-4 and Eq. (1-6) (p. 7), "Why the minus sign?" (p. 7); Sample Problem 1-1 pp. 8–9; Section 1.3 pp. 9–11 (proper time, proper clock, p. 10); Section 1.4 pp. 11–13 (timelike/spacelike p. 11, 1983 metre p. 12, Table 1-5 p. 13); Sample Problem 1-2 pp. 14–15; Section 1.5 pp. 15–18. The OCR layer has errors (e.g. "rhe", "srudent"); quote from the page image if quoting. Tong, Dynamics and Relativity, Section 7.3.1 (reference-only) gives the same material in the opposite sign convention with the rotation analogy; MIT 8.20 Week 1 videos (Lecture 2.1 Events, Lecture 3.3 Michelson-Morley) are optional.

Teaching intent: the learner's geometry background makes the invariant-metric idea immediate; the work is in (i) keeping conventions straight across three sources and (ii) the physics of proper time as a property of a pair of events. Insist on the convention line at the top of every submission. The spark-data check is worth doing live with a calculator; the discrepancy between $1.41$ and the book's $1.42$ years for the starship is a good moment to talk about carrying figures.

Watch for: inverted time-dilation factor; "proper time is the observer's time"; treating $\Delta s^2 < 0$ as an error; claiming a frame "moving at $c$" exists for lightlike pairs.

Staged misconception for a peer discussion, if peers are enabled: a classmate may insist that "John's clocks are right and Mary's clock is slow". Resolve by asking which two events each measurement refers to and which clocks are present at them.
