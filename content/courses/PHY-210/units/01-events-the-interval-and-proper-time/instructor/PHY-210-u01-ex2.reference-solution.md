## Reference reasoning (instructor only)

**1.** Laboratory: $\Delta x = v\Delta t$, so $\Delta s^2 = -c^2\Delta t^2 + v^2\Delta t^2 = -c^2\Delta t^2(1 - v^2/c^2)$. Clock frame: the clock is at rest, $\Delta x' = 0$, and its reading is the proper time, $\Delta s^2 = -c^2\tau^2$. Invariance: $c^2\tau^2 = c^2\Delta t^2(1-v^2/c^2)$, hence
$$\tau = \Delta t\sqrt{1 - v^2/c^2} = \Delta t/\gamma,\qquad \gamma \equiv (1-v^2/c^2)^{-1/2}.$$
Units: $v^2/c^2$ dimensionless; $\tau$, $\Delta t$ in seconds (or metres of light-travel time, consistently).

**2.** $(1-\epsilon)^{1/2} = 1 - \epsilon/2 - \epsilon^2/8 - \dots$ with $\epsilon = v^2/c^2$: $\tau \approx \Delta t(1 - v^2/2c^2)$, fractional deficit $v^2/2c^2$. Jet, $v = 250\ \mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}$: $v/c = 8.34\times10^{-7}$, $v^2/2c^2 = 3.5\times10^{-13}$ (about $30\ \mathrm{ns}$ per day). Satellite, $v = 7.7\ \mathrm{km\,s^{-1}}$: $v/c = 2.57\times10^{-5}$, $v^2/2c^2 = 3.3\times10^{-10}$ (about $28\ \mathrm{\mu s}$ per day; GPS clocks also carry a gravitational correction of the opposite sign that belongs to PHY-240, not to this course — unit 10 says so explicitly).

**3.** $\tau/\Delta t = \tfrac12 \iff 1 - v^2/c^2 = \tfrac14 \iff v^2/c^2 = \tfrac34$, so $v = \tfrac{\sqrt3}{2}c = 0.866c$ (equivalently $\gamma = 2$).

**4.** For $0\le v<c$, $0 < 1 - v^2/c^2 \le 1$, so $0 < \tau\le\Delta t$ with equality iff $v = 0$. For $v>c$ the radicand is negative: no real $\tau$. Physically, a worldline with $v>c$ would make $A$ and $B$ spacelike separated, and no clock can be present at both; the derivation's premise (a clock at both events) fails. Also acceptable: "the Lorentz factor would be imaginary, signalling that no inertial frame moves at $v \ge c$".

**Alternative valid approaches.** Starting from $\gamma$ as known and deriving backwards is not acceptable for part 1 (the point is to derive it from invariance), but using $\gamma$ to organise parts 2–4 is fine. Expanding $1/\gamma$ via the binomial series is equivalent.

**Common gaps.** Writing $\tau = \gamma\Delta t$ (inverted — catch it with the $v\to c$ limit, which must give $\tau\to0$); treating $v^2/2c^2$ as the correction to $\Delta t$ rather than to $\tau$ (same to this order, but say which); giving $0.866$ without the exact $\sqrt3/2$ or vice versa; arguing part 4 from "nothing goes faster than light" alone without connecting it to the radicand.
