# An inelastic collision in two frames — reference reasoning

Each incoming particle has $\gamma=5/3$. Total momentum in S is 0 and total energy is $10mc^2/3$, so $M=10m/3$. In S\prime the incoming velocities are $(4/5-3/5)c/(1-12/25)=5c/13$ and $(-4/5-3/5)c/(1+12/25)=-35c/37$. Transforming each momentum gives energies $13mc^2/12$ and $37mc^2/12$, and momenta $5mc/12$ and $-35mc/12$. Totals are $25mc^2/6$ and $-5mc/2$. Their invariant is $[(25/6)^2-(5/2)^2]m^2c^4=(100/9)m^2c^4$, so $M=10m/3$ again. The product moves at $-3c/5$ and has the same totals. Relative kinetic energy became internal energy; invariant system mass already included it before impact. If photons escape, total four-momentum of product plus photons remains conserved, but the product alone can lose invariant mass. One must specify the photons' energy and momentum to calculate that reduction.

## Assessment note

Accept any correct alternative derivation with equivalent stated conventions. A numerical check supports a calculation; it does not prove a general claim. Diagnose the first substantive gap, and do not equate help-assisted work with independent demonstration.
