## Outline: Momenergy — relativistic momentum and energy

This unit is **outlined, not yet prepared**. It will be written from Chapter 7 (Momenergy) of Taylor and Wheeler's *Spacetime Physics* (2nd ed., CC BY 4.0; chapter PDF located, page-level locators to be added when the unit is prepared), with Section 7.4 (Relativistic Kinematics, including 7.4.4 Massless Particles) of Tong's *Dynamics and Relativity* (reference-only) and the MIT 8.20 Week 3 videos (Lecture 9.1 Momentum Conservation, Lecture 9.2 Energy Conservation) as supplements.

**Scope.** Why Newtonian momentum $m\mathbf v$ cannot be conserved in all frames once velocities add relativistically; the four-momentum $P^\mu = mU^\mu = (E/c,\mathbf p)$ as the unique four-vector proportional to the four-velocity (the book's "momenergy arrow", 7.1–7.2); its components $E = \gamma mc^2$, $\mathbf p = \gamma m\mathbf v$ and its invariant magnitude $P\cdot P = -m^2c^2$, i.e. $E^2 = p^2c^2 + m^2c^4$ (7.3–7.5), with SI units checked and the Newtonian limit $E\approx mc^2 + \tfrac12mv^2$ derived with the neglected terms named; mass as the invariant, energy and momentum as the frame-dependent components (7.6, and the book's "Dialog: Use and Abuse of the Concept of Mass" in Chapter 8); massless particles with $E = pc$; conservation of momenergy as a four-vector statement. Signature $(-,+,+,+)$; the book keeps $c = 1$ and measures energy and momentum in mass units.

**What preparation will add.** A full lesson, original exercises (a derivation of $E^2 = p^2c^2 + m^2c^4$ from the invariant with the Newtonian expansion, a calculation of the kinetic energy and momentum of a fast particle in SI units with a check against $E = pc$ for $v\to c$, an explain-back on "relativistic mass"), instructor reference reasoning, an exit check and the unit's common misconceptions. Until then the reading list is accurate and the tutor can discuss the material, but there is no assessed work for this unit.
