# A variable metric coefficient in flat spacetime — reference reasoning

Put $b=\rho+\xi$, $q=a\eta/c$. Then $d(ct)=\sinh q\,d\xi+b(a/c)\cosh q\,d\eta$ and $dx=\cosh q\,d\xi+b(a/c)\sinh q\,d\eta$. Subtracting squares gives $d\xi^2-b^2(a/c)^2d\eta^2$, the claimed metric. Thus $d\tau_h/d\tau_0=1+ah/c^2$ for equal coordinate increments. The null propagation equation is stationary, giving equal coordinate separations of successive emissions and receptions, so $\nu_h/\nu_0=(1+ah/c^2)^{-1}$. The hyperbola radius b gives proper acceleration $c^2/b=a/(1+a\xi/c^2)$. This is an explicit coordinate transformation of flat spacetime, so curvature has not been established. Track the relative motion of a small initially comoving free-fall cloud while controlling rotation and nongravitational forces to test tidal deformation.

Accept any correct alternative derivation with equivalent stated conventions. A numerical check supports a calculation; it does not prove a general claim. Diagnose the first substantive gap, and do not equate help-assisted work with independent demonstration.
