# Audit a light-bending argument — reference reasoning

In the initial inertial frame, the ray crosses in $t\simeq L/c$ and the lift rises by $gt^2/2$, giving relative displacement $-gL^2/(2c^2)$. Require $gL/c^2\ll1$ and approximately constant acceleration. The ray is a straight null line in the flat inertial description, so coordinate bending does not establish curvature. A stellar trajectory crosses changing gravitational conditions; the local expression supplies neither a global metric nor the complete angle. The equivalence principle relates the local effect to a small supported gravitational laboratory with negligible tides. A stellar prediction requires a specified gravitational geometry and null-trajectory calculation.

## Assessment note

Accept any correct alternative derivation with equivalent stated conventions. A numerical check supports a calculation; it does not prove a general claim. Diagnose the first substantive gap, and do not equate help-assisted work with independent demonstration.
