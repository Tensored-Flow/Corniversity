## Outline: Causal structure — timelike, spacelike and lightlike

This unit is **outlined, not yet prepared**. It will be written from Chapter 6 (Regions of Spacetime) of Taylor and Wheeler's *Spacetime Physics* (2nd ed., CC BY 4.0; chapter PDF located, page-level locators to be added when the unit is prepared), with Section 7.2.2 (Causality) and Section 7.3.1 (The Invariant Interval) of Tong's *Dynamics and Relativity* (reference-only) and the MIT 8.20 Week 2 videos (Lecture 5.4 Regions in Spacetime Diagrams, Lecture 5.5 Causality) as supplements; the timelike/spacelike discussion of Section 1.4 (unit 1) is the starting point.

**Scope.** Light speed as the limit on causal influence (6.1); the three relations between events — timelike, spacelike, lightlike — as the frame-independent partition of spacetime by the sign of $\Delta s^2$ (6.2), with proofs that the time order of a timelike pair is the same in every frame and that for a spacelike pair there are frames with either order and one with simultaneity (the Lorentz transformation of unit 2 applied); the light cone as the boundary between the absolute future, the absolute past and the "elsewhere" of an event (6.3); why a faster-than-light signal would allow a message into one's own past, made quantitative with two boosts; proper time versus proper distance as the invariant attached to each class. Signature $(-,+,+,+)$ with the dictionary to the book's and Tong's conventions.

**What preparation will add.** A full lesson, original exercises (a proof of the invariance of time order for timelike pairs, a two-frame construction showing a superluminal signal reversing cause and effect, an explain-back on the three regions), instructor reference reasoning, an exit check and the unit's common misconceptions. Until then the reading list is accurate and the tutor can discuss the material, but there is no assessed work for this unit.
