# A clock offset masquerading as a speed anomaly — reference reasoning

The reflection coordinate time is $(2+8)/2=5.0\,\mu\mathrm s$, consistent with $L=c(8-2)/2=900\,\mathrm m$. B is ahead by $0.4\,\mu\mathrm s$. Its particle detection therefore occurs at $15.0\,\mu\mathrm s$, giving $v=900/5=180\,\mathrm m/\mu\mathrm s=0.6c$. Ignoring the offset gives $900/5.4=166.666\ldots\,\mathrm m/\mu\mathrm s=5c/9$. The equal out-and-back time convention assumes stationary separation, negligible reflection delay and vacuum light propagation in the inertial frame; it is not an independent one-way measurement.

## Assessment note

Accept any correct alternative derivation with equivalent stated conventions. A numerical check supports a calculation; it does not prove a general claim. Diagnose the first substantive gap, and do not equate help-assisted work with independent demonstration.
