# How local is a free-float laboratory? — reference reasoning

For initially zero relative velocity, $L(t)-L(0)\simeq\kappa L(0)t^2/2$. The tolerance gives $t\lesssim\sqrt{2\times10^{-5}/(6\times10^{-6})}=1.8257\,\mathrm s$. Here $\kappa t^2\simeq10^{-5}\ll1$, so neglecting the higher terms of $L_0\cosh(\sqrt\kappa t)$ is consistent. Halving $L$ increases the time bound by $\sqrt2$, assuming the same $\kappa$ and absolute displacement tolerance. A single free particle can define the central freely falling worldline; the relative motion of separated particles exposes the spatial variation that limits one common inertial approximation. Other forces and apparatus drift must be excluded before attributing measured relative acceleration to tides.
