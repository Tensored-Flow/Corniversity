## Outline: Free-float frames and the lattice of clocks

This unit is **outlined, not yet prepared**. It will be written from Chapter 2 (Floating Free) of Taylor and Wheeler's *Spacetime Physics* (2nd ed., CC BY 4.0; chapter PDF located, page-level locators to be added when the unit is prepared), with Section 3.1 (The Principle of Relativity) and the MIT 8.20 Week 1 videos (Lecture 2.1 Events, Lecture 2.2 Galilean Transformation) as supplements.

**Scope.** The operational definition of an inertial frame as a *free-float* frame in which a released test particle stays at rest or moves uniformly (Sections 2.1–2.2); the *local* character of such frames — a frame is inertial only over a region of space and an interval of time small enough that tidal relative accelerations are below the resolution of the experiment (Section 2.3), with the book's numerical criteria; test particles (2.5); locating events with a latticework of rods and synchronised clocks and the Einstein synchronisation procedure by light flashes (2.6); the observer as the whole lattice rather than a person with eyes (2.7); measuring a particle's speed from lattice records (2.8); the rocket frame as a second lattice (2.9). The tidal discussion is the one place in units 1–9 where the limits of special relativity are mentioned; it is phrased as a limitation of the idealisation, and curvature is not introduced.

**What preparation will add.** A full lesson, original exercises (a synchronisation procedure checked for consistency, a tidal-acceleration estimate fixing the size of an acceptable free-float region, an explain-back on why "observer" means a lattice), instructor reference reasoning, an exit check and the unit's common misconceptions. Until then the reading list is accurate and the tutor can discuss the material, but there is no assessed work for this unit.
