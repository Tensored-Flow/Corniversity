# One titration, one exact balance, four useful approximations — reference reasoning

Initial acid amount is $0.0300(0.0800)=0.00240$ mol; equivalence volume is $0.00240/0.120=0.0200$ L. Writing $V$ in litres, $C_T=0.00240/(0.0300+V)$ and $C_N=0.120V/(0.0300+V)$. Charge balance is $h+C_N=[A^-]+K_w/h$, so the full ideal equilibrium is
$$h+C_N-\frac{C_TK_a}{K_a+h}-\frac{K_w}{h}=0.$$
At zero addition the quadratic gives $h=0.00125495$ M (about $1.255\times10^{-3}$), pH about $2.9014$; dissociation is about $1.57\%$, with water negligible. At 10.0 mL, half the original acid is converted to base, so pH $\approx\mathrm pK_a=4.69897$; both buffer species are approximately 0.0300 M, much larger than $h$. At equivalence, $C_T=0.0480$ M of conjugate base, $K_b=5.00\times10^{-10}$, and $[OH^-]\approx4.89898\times10^{-6}$ M, giving pH $\approx8.6901$. At 25.0 mL there is $0.000600$ mol excess OH$^-$ in 0.0550 L: $[OH^-]\approx0.0109091$ M and pH $\approx12.0378$. Hydrolysis is negligible against this excess. The regimes are weak acid, buffer, conjugate-base hydrolysis, and excess strong base. Equivalence is stoichiometric; A$^-$ remains a base. Both $K_a(T)$ and $K_w(T)$ must be updated at another temperature, and neutral pH need not be seven. Substitute each estimated positive $h$ into the full residual, compare residual magnitude with the total ionic concentrations, and/or solve the monotone balance numerically to a declared concentration tolerance. Also reconstruct mass and charge sums explicitly; a small unscaled residual alone can hide a large relative error at low concentrations.

## Assessment note

Accept any correct alternative derivation with equivalent stated conventions. A numerical check supports a calculation; it does not prove a general claim. Diagnose the first substantive gap, and do not equate help-assisted work with independent demonstration.
