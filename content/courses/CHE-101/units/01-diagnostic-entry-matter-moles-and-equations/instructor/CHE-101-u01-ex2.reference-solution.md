## Reference reasoning (instructor only)

**Check of the equation.** Fe: 2 = 2. O: 3 + 3 = 6 on the left, $3\times2 = 6$ on the right. C: 3 = 3. Balanced.

**1. Limiting reactant.** $M(\ce{Fe2O3}) = 2(55.85) + 3(16.00) = 159.70\ \text{g mol}^{-1}$; $n(\ce{Fe2O3}) = 25.0/159.70 = 0.15654\ \text{mol}$. $M(\ce{CO}) = 28.01\ \text{g mol}^{-1}$; $n(\ce{CO}) = 15.0/28.01 = 0.53552\ \text{mol}$. Consuming all the $\ce{Fe2O3}$ needs $3\times0.15654 = 0.46963\ \text{mol}$ of CO, which is less than the $0.53552\ \text{mol}$ available. So $\ce{Fe2O3}$ is limiting and CO is in excess. (Equivalently: $0.53552/3 = 0.17851\ \text{mol}$ of $\ce{Fe2O3}$ could be consumed by the CO, more than is present.)

**2. Theoretical yield.** $n(\ce{Fe}) = 2\times0.15654 = 0.31309\ \text{mol}$; $m(\ce{Fe}) = 0.31309\times55.85 = 17.49\ \text{g}$ ($17.5\ \text{g}$ at three significant figures).

**3. Percent yield.** $15.2/17.49\times100\% = 86.9\%$.

**4. Excess.** CO remaining: $0.53552 - 0.46963 = 0.06589\ \text{mol}$, i.e. $0.06589\times28.01 = 1.85\ \text{g}$.

**5. Assumptions and shortfall.** The theoretical yield assumes pure $\ce{Fe2O3}$ and CO, reaction to completion exactly as written (no intermediate oxides such as $\ce{Fe3O4}$ or FeO left over, no side reactions), and complete recovery of the iron. A real yield falls short for any of those physical reasons; none is a calculation error.

**Alternative approaches.** Either direction of the ratio comparison is fine. Accept 17.5 g, 86.9% to 87%, 1.85 g. A learner who reports excess CO as moles (0.0659 mol) rather than grams has answered a slightly different question; ask for grams but do not mark the chemistry wrong.

**Common gaps.** Comparing 15.0 g with 25.0 g; computing iron from the CO amount after correctly naming $\ce{Fe2O3}$ as limiting (inconsistent working); forgetting the factor 2 for iron; reporting yield above 100% after a slip without noticing that this is impossible.

**Actual versus theoretical leftover.** The approximately 1.85 g CO remainder assumes complete consumption of the limiting Fe₂O₃ in the stated reaction. Recovering 15.2 g Fe could reflect incomplete conversion, product recovery loss or other departures. The recovered iron mass alone therefore does not determine actual CO consumption or CO remaining.
