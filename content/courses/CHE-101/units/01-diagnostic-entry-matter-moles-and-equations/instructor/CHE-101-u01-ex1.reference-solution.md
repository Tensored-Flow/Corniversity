## Reference reasoning (instructor only)

**1. Balancing.** $\ce{C2H5OH(l) + 3 O2(g) -> 2 CO2(g) + 3 H2O(l)}$. Carbon: 2 on each side. Hydrogen: $5 + 1 = 6$ on the left, $3\times2 = 6$ on the right. Oxygen: $1 + 3\times2 = 7$ on the left, $2\times2 + 3\times1 = 7$ on the right. Charge: zero on both sides.

**2. Moles and mass of CO2.** $M(\ce{C2H5OH}) = 2(12.01) + 6(1.008) + 16.00 = 46.07\ \text{g mol}^{-1}$. $n(\ce{C2H5OH}) = 11.5/46.07 = 0.2496\ \text{mol}$. The mole ratio $\ce{CO2}:\ce{C2H5OH}$ is $2:1$, so $n(\ce{CO2}) = 0.4993\ \text{mol}$ and $m(\ce{CO2}) = 0.4993\times44.01 = 21.97\ \text{g}$, which is $22.0\ \text{g}$ at the three significant figures of the data.

**3. Mass balance.** $n(\ce{O2}) = 3\times0.2496 = 0.7489\ \text{mol}$, $m(\ce{O2}) = 0.7489\times32.00 = 23.96\ \text{g}$. $n(\ce{H2O}) = 0.7489\ \text{mol}$, $m(\ce{H2O}) = 0.7489\times18.02 = 13.49\ \text{g}$. Reactants: $11.5 + 23.96 = 35.46\ \text{g}$. Products: $21.97 + 13.49 = 35.46\ \text{g}$. Agreement to the rounding is the conservation of atoms expressed in grams.

**4. Assumptions.** The ethanol is pure and fully combusted to $\ce{CO2}$ and liquid water (no CO or soot from incomplete combustion); the phases are as written (the energy bookkeeping in unit 4 depends on that, the stoichiometry does not); atomic masses as given.

**Alternative valid approaches.** Working in moles of oxygen atoms, or computing the water mass by difference after establishing conservation, is acceptable provided the conservation claim is not circular (the learner must compute at least three of the four masses independently). Accept $22.0\ \text{g}$ or $21.97\ \text{g}$; accept totals of $35.4$–$35.5\ \text{g}$.

**Common gaps.** Balancing by altering a subscript (e.g. writing $\ce{O3}$); using the ethanol coefficient instead of the ratio; dividing grams of ethanol by 44.01; omitting units; asserting mass balance without numbers.
