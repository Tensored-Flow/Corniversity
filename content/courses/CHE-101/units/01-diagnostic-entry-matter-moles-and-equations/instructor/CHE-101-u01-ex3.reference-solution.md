## Reference reasoning (instructor only)

**1. The ion $\ce{^{37}_{17}Cl^-}$.** $Z = 17$ protons. Neutrons $= A - Z = 37 - 17 = 20$. Electrons $= Z - q = 17 - (-1) = 18$.

**2. Average atomic mass.** $0.7576\times34.969 + 0.2424\times36.966 = 26.493 + 8.961 = 35.45\ \text{u}$. It is an abundance-weighted mean of two isotopic masses; every chlorine atom has a mass close to either 34.969 u or 36.966 u, and none has 35.45 u. (The textbook periodic-table value agrees to this precision.)

**3. Molarity and dilution.** $n(\ce{NaCl}) = 5.85/58.44 = 0.1001\ \text{mol}$. $c = 0.1001\ \text{mol}/0.2500\ \text{L} = 0.400\ \text{M}$. Dilution: $c_2 = c_1V_1/V_2 = 0.400\times50.0/200.0 = 0.100\ \text{M}$. Each formula unit of NaCl dissociates to one $\ce{Na+}$ and one $\ce{Cl-}$, so $[\ce{Cl-}] = 0.100\ \text{M}$. The dilution calculation assumes the amount of solute is conserved (nothing precipitates or evaporates) and that the final solution volume is the stated $200.0\ \text{mL}$ (it is a made-up-to volume, so additivity of volumes is not needed; if the learner instead assumes additive volumes, note that this is the weaker assumption and only approximately true in general).

**Alternative approaches.** Computing moles in the 50.0 mL aliquot ($0.0200\ \text{mol}$) and dividing by 0.2000 L is equivalent and preferred by some learners; accept.

**Common gaps.** Reading 37 as the neutron count; giving the anion 17 or 16 electrons; an unweighted mean (35.97 u); using 250.0 mL as solvent volume language without consequence (accept, but correct the wording); forgetting to convert mL to L (0.0004 M or 400 M); chloride given as 0.200 M.
