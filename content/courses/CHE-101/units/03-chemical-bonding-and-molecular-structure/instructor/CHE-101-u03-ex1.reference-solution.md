## Reference reasoning (instructor only)

**1. Lewis structure.** Valence electrons: N 5, O 6 each, plus 1 for the charge: $5 + 12 + 1 = 18$. Full shells: $3 \times 8 = 24$; bonding electrons $24 - 18 = 6$, three bonds: one N=O double and one N–O single. Remaining $18 - 6 = 12$ electrons as lone pairs: one on N (2), two on the double-bonded O (4), three on the single-bonded O (6). Formal charges: N: $5 - 2 - \tfrac12(6) = 0$. Double-bonded O: $6 - 4 - \tfrac12(4) = 0$. Single-bonded O: $6 - 6 - \tfrac12(2) = -1$. Sum $= -1$, equal to the ion's charge.

**2. Resonance.** The second structure has the double bond on the other oxygen. Hybrid: three bonding pairs over two N–O bonds, bond order $1.5$; both bonds equal in length, between single and double. (Experiment agrees: the two N–O distances in nitrite are equal.)

**3. The double-double structure.** Two N=O double bonds (8 electrons) plus a lone pair on N (2) gives nitrogen 10 electrons. Nitrogen is in period 2 and has only the $2s$ and $2p$ orbitals available; it cannot exceed an octet. The electron count (8 + 2 + 2×4 = 18) and the formal-charge sum (N: $5 - 2 - 4 = -1$; each O: $6 - 4 - 2 = 0$; total $-1$) are both fine, which is exactly why this check matters: count and sum are necessary, not sufficient.

**4. Shape.** Around N: two bonded atoms and one lone pair, $\ce{AX2E}$, steric number 3: trigonal planar electron-pair geometry, bent molecular shape. The lone pair repels the bonding pairs more than they repel each other, so the O–N–O angle is compressed below $120^\circ$ (measured about $115^\circ$). $\ce{CO2}$: carbon has two double bonds and no lone pairs, $\ce{AX2}$, steric number 2: linear.

**Alternative valid approaches.** A learner may draw the structure directly and verify octets without the counting steps; accept if the formal charges and sum are shown. Some write the hybrid with dashed partial bonds and $-\tfrac12$ on each oxygen; that is a fine description of the hybrid's charge distribution, provided the individual resonance structures carry integer formal charges.

**Common gaps.** Forgetting the extra electron for the charge (17 electrons); formal charges computed with bonding electrons counted fully rather than halved; accepting the double-double structure because "formal charges are all zero on oxygen"; calling nitrite linear by analogy with $\ce{CO2}$ without counting the lone pair.
