## Lesson notes (instructor only)

**Source mapping (all opened 2026-10-30).** Lecture 9 page: electronegativity, ionic, covalent and polar covalent bonds (OpenStax 7.1–7.2 replace the Atkins and Jones sections 2.1–2.3, 2.14–2.16). Lecture 10 notes: octet rule and bonding versus lone-pair electrons (PDF page 1), the eight-step procedure with HCN and CN⁻ (page 2), FC = V − L − ½B and the sum rule with the thiocyanate example (page 3), choosing by formal charge and electronegativity, ozone resonance (page 4), resonance hybrid (page 5). Lecture 11 page: radicals, octet-deficient and expanded-valence molecules. Lecture 12 notes: VSEPR principles and AXE nomenclature (page 1), AX2–AX6 table (page 2), repulsion ordering and lone-pair examples (page 3), see-saw, T-shape and square planar rationalisation with NH3/CH4/PH3 angles (page 4), lone-pair examples and the limits of VSEPR (page 7). Lecture 13 page: MO theory for s and p homonuclear and heteronuclear diatomics (OpenStax 8.4). Lecture 14 page: σ and π bonds, sp³, sp², sp hybridisation (OpenStax 8.1–8.2).

**Teaching intent.** Three skills: a counting procedure done every time (not from memory of common molecules), shape from regions, and MO bond order as the place where a better model earns its keep. The ozone and thiocyanate examples belong to the notes; the lesson uses carbonate, and the exercises use nitrite, so none of the three are copies.

**Tutor behaviour.** Insist on the formal-charge sum check; ask what the molecule's shape would be if a lone pair were a bonded atom instead; separate the model (VSEPR, hybridisation as a description) from measurements (bond lengths, dipole moments, paramagnetism). Hybridisation is assigned from geometry, never claimed to cause it.

**Honesty points.** Electronegativity thresholds for ionic versus covalent are conventions. The MO ordering switch between N2 and O2 is stated as the standard result, not derived here. No laboratory instructions: the paramagnetism of liquid oxygen is reported as a known observation.
