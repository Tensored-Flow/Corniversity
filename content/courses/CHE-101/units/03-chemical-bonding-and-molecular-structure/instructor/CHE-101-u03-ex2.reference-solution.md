## Reference reasoning (instructor only)

| Molecule | Valence e⁻ | Class | Electron-pair geometry | Shape | Angles | Dipole |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| $\ce{SF4}$ | $6 + 4(7) = 34$ | $\ce{AX4E}$ | trigonal bipyramidal | see-saw (lone pair equatorial) | equatorial F–S–F a little below $120^\circ$; axial F–S–F below $180^\circ$; axial–equatorial near $90^\circ$ | polar |
| $\ce{XeF4}$ | $8 + 4(7) = 36$ | $\ce{AX4E2}$ | octahedral | square planar (lone pairs trans) | $90^\circ$ | nonpolar |
| $\ce{NH3}$ | $5 + 3 = 8$ | $\ce{AX3E}$ | tetrahedral | trigonal pyramidal | below $109.5^\circ$ (about $107^\circ$) | polar |
| $\ce{SO3}$ | $6 + 3(6) = 24$ | $\ce{AX3}$ | trigonal planar | trigonal planar | $120^\circ$ | nonpolar |

**Angle compression.** Lone pair–bond pair repulsion exceeds bond pair–bond pair repulsion (lecture 12, page 3), so bonded angles shrink wherever a lone pair is present: $\ce{SF4}$ and $\ce{NH3}$. In $\ce{XeF4}$ the two lone pairs sit opposite each other, so the four bonds stay in a plane at $90^\circ$; in $\ce{SO3}$ there is no lone pair.

**Polarity.** $\ce{SF4}$: the see-saw has no symmetry that cancels the four S–F dipoles; polar. $\ce{XeF4}$: four equivalent dipoles in a square cancel pairwise; nonpolar. $\ce{NH3}$: three N–H dipoles and the lone pair give a net moment along the axis; polar. $\ce{SO3}$: three equivalent dipoles at $120^\circ$ sum to zero; nonpolar.

**3. $\ce{SF4}$ versus $\ce{OF4}$.** Sulfur is in period 3 and can be hypervalent (more than eight electrons around it, as in $\ce{SF6}$); oxygen is in period 2 and is limited to an octet, so a central oxygen with four bonds and a lone pair (10 electrons) is not available. The proposed AX₄E Lewis structure actually gives O formal charge zero: 6−2−4=0. Electronegativity does not forbid positive formal charge as a general rule; reject that proposed alternative argument. The issue here is the second-period octet constraint in the stipulated Lewis model.

**4. $\ce{SO3}$ and resonance.** Whether drawn with one S=O and two S–O⁻ (formal charges) or with three S=O (expanded octet on S), every structure has three electron regions around S and no lone pairs; VSEPR depends on the number of regions only, so the shape is trigonal planar in all cases (lecture 12, page 2).

**Common gaps.** Treating $\ce{SF4}$ as tetrahedral because it has four fluorines (lone pair ignored); putting $\ce{XeF4}$'s lone pairs cis; calling $\ce{SO3}$ pyramidal by analogy with $\ce{NH3}$ (no lone pair on S); declaring every molecule with polar bonds polar.
