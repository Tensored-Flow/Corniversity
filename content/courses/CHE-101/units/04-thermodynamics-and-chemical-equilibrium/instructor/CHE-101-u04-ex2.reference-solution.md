## Reference reasoning (instructor only)

**1. $\Delta H^\circ$ and $\Delta S^\circ$.** $\Delta H^\circ_r = [(-634.9) + (-393.5)] - (-1207.6) = +179.2$ kJ/mol: endothermic. One mole of gas forms from solids, so $\Delta S^\circ$ should be large and positive: $\Delta S^\circ_r = 38.1 + 213.8 - 91.7 = +160.2$ J K$^{-1}$ mol$^{-1}$.

**2. $\Delta G^\circ$ at 298.15 K.** $\Delta G^\circ = 179.2 - 298.15\times0.1602 = 179.2 - 47.8 = +131.4$ kJ/mol. Positive: not spontaneous under standard conditions at room temperature.

**3. Crossover.** $\Delta G^\circ = 0$ at $T^* = \Delta H^\circ/\Delta S^\circ = 179200/160.2 = 1118.6$ K, about $1119$ K ($846$ °C). Assumption: $\Delta H^\circ$ and $\Delta S^\circ$ taken as independent of temperature over 298–1119 K; they are not exactly constant (heat capacities differ), so this is an estimate.

**4. $K$.** Both solids have activity 1, so $K = P(\ce{CO2})/P^\circ$. $\ln K = -\Delta G^\circ/RT = -131400/(8.314\times298.15) = -53.0$, $K = 9.4\times10^{-24}$: the equilibrium pressure of $\ce{CO2}$ over the two solids at 298 K is about $10^{-23}$ bar, effectively nothing. At the crossover temperature $\Delta G^\circ = 0$ and $K = 1$, so $P(\ce{CO2}) = 1$ bar, which is the thermodynamic meaning of "decomposes above about 1100 K at atmospheric pressure".

**5. Correction.** Endothermic reactions are spontaneous whenever $T\Delta S^\circ$ exceeds $\Delta H^\circ$; for this reaction that happens above about 1119 K. Even below it the reaction does occur, to a tiny equilibrium extent set by $K$; "cannot happen" confuses a small $K$ with no reaction.

**Alternative valid approaches.** Using $T = 298$ K rather than 298.15 K changes $\Delta G^\circ$ by 0.02 kJ; accept. Reporting $K$ as $10^{-23}$ in order of magnitude is fine. A learner who computes the $\ce{CO2}$ pressure at an intermediate temperature with the constant-$\Delta H$, $\Delta S$ assumption has gone beyond the question; check the arithmetic and approve.

**Common gaps.** Forgetting to convert J to kJ in $T\Delta S$ (gives $\Delta G^\circ \approx -47600$ kJ/mol, an absurd number that should trigger a sanity check); including the solids in $K$; omitting the constancy assumption; concluding from $\Delta G^\circ > 0$ that nothing happens.
