## Lesson notes (instructor only)

**Source mapping (all opened 2026-10-30).** Lecture 15 notes: thermodynamics versus kinetics, bond enthalpy positive (PDF page 1); standard state at 1 bar, C–H bond enthalpies, enthalpies of reaction and glucose oxidation (page 2); ΔHr° from bond enthalpies and from ΔHf° at 1 bar and 298.15 K (page 3); Hess's law and state function (page 4). Lecture 16 notes: spontaneous change, ΔG = ΔH − TΔS, endothermic spontaneous examples (page 1); worked ΔG° cases and entropy sign prediction (page 2); ΔSr° from absolute entropies, H2O2 decomposition (−196 kJ/mol; S° values 70, 205, 110), ice melting (page 3); ΔGf° (page 4). Lecture 17 page: temperature and spontaneity, biological free energy (covered lightly here). Lecture 18 notes: dynamic equilibrium and ΔG = ΔG° + RT ln Q (page 1); Q, K, ΔG° = −RT ln K, Q versus K (page 2); meaning of K and the 2NO2/N2O4 ICE example with K = 6.84 (page 3); K from ΔG°, Le Châtelier stated (page 4); adding and removing reagents via Q (page 5). Lecture 19 page: temperature dependence of K, applications. OpenStax 5.3, 13.1–13.4, 16.1–16.4 replace the Atkins and Jones chapters.

**Teaching intent.** For a mathematically strong learner the derivations are easy; the chemistry to install is the discipline of standard states, phases and temperatures attached to every number, the distinction between ΔG and ΔG°, and the Q-versus-K explanation of Le Châtelier. The H2O2 example deliberately reuses the notes' system with slightly different S° data so the learner sees that tabulated values vary and that the result is insensitive.

**Tutor behaviour.** Ask for the balanced equation with phases before any energetic claim; ask what is assumed about temperature when ΔH and ΔS are carried across a range; never let "ΔG° = 0 at equilibrium" pass. Separate the model (ideal-gas activities, constant ΔH) from the data (tabulated ΔHf°, S°).

**Staged peer misconception, if peers are enabled.** A classmate says "endothermic means it won't happen"; resolve with the ice and CaCO3 cases in the same discussion.
