## Reference reasoning (instructor only)

**1. Formation route.** $\Delta H^\circ_r = [\Delta H^\circ_f(\ce{CO2}, g) + 2\Delta H^\circ_f(\ce{H2O}, l)] - [\Delta H^\circ_f(\ce{CH4}, g) + 2\Delta H^\circ_f(\ce{O2}, g)] = [-393.5 + 2(-285.8)] - [-74.6 + 0] = -965.1 + 74.6 = -890.5$ kJ/mol.

**2. Bond-enthalpy estimate.** Broken: $4$ C–H $= 4(413) = 1652$; $2$ O=O $= 2(498) = 996$; total $2648$ kJ. Formed: $2$ C=O $= 2(799) = 1598$; $4$ O–H $= 4(463) = 1852$; total $3450$ kJ. Estimate $= 2648 - 3450 = -802$ kJ/mol.

**3. The discrepancy.** Mean bond enthalpies refer to gaseous molecules, so the estimate is for $\ce{2 H2O(g)}$. The reaction as written produces liquid water, and condensing two moles releases $2\times44.0 = 88.0$ kJ. Corrected estimate: $-802 - 88.0 = -890$ kJ/mol, within $0.5$ kJ of the formation value; the residual is the averaging error of mean bond enthalpies (the C–H bonds in methane are not exactly the average C–H bond).

**4. Gaseous water.** $\Delta H^\circ_r(\ce{H2O}, g) = -890.5 + 2(44.0) = -802.5$ kJ/mol, which is what the bond-enthalpy estimate was approximating. Equivalently, $\Delta H^\circ_f(\ce{H2O}, g) = -285.8 + 44.0 = -241.8$ kJ/mol and $-393.5 + 2(-241.8) + 74.6 = -802.5$.

**5. Assumptions.** All values at 298.15 K; every species in its standard state (1 bar); the phases as written, which fix which enthalpy of formation of water applies; mean bond enthalpies are averages over many compounds and hold for gas-phase bonds only; the reaction goes completely as written.

**Alternative valid approaches.** Writing a Hess cycle through the elements; or correcting the bond-enthalpy estimate by converting $\ce{H2O(g)}$ to $\ce{H2O(l)}$ explicitly as a separate step. Accept $-890$ to $-891$, $-802$, and the explicit condensation argument in either direction.

**Common gaps.** Reversing the broken-minus-formed convention (giving $+802$); counting one O=O bond instead of two; treating the 90 kJ gap as "just approximation" without naming the phase; forgetting that $\Delta H^\circ_f(\ce{O2}, g) = 0$.
