# Electricity from a concentration difference — reference reasoning

The higher-activity Cu²⁺ side has higher reduction potential because Ered=E°+(RT/2F)ln a(Cu²⁺). It is the cathode. Net transfer: Cu²⁺(high)→Cu²⁺(low), with copper dissolving at low side and depositing at high. E=(RT/2F)ln(0.200/0.00200)=0.059159 V. Dividing both activities by ten preserves their ratio in the stated ideal/junction-free model, so E is unchanged; ratio one gives E=0. Drawing charge consumes the maintained activity difference. Restoring it needs work, and real junction/transport effects can alter measured voltages.
