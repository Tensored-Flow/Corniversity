## Grading notes (instructor only)

This is the course's laboratory report (assessment a3). Read it as a model study, not as a chemistry fact sheet.

- **Automatic checks** look for the five reference numbers (0.250, 3, 2.50, 1.73, 0.143), flag "the reaction has stopped" language, and require that a first-order model is accompanied by assumption language. All are advisory. The value 3 can appear coincidentally (as a part number), so do not treat that check as evidence on its own.
- **Non-negotiables for acceptance:** the conservation law derived from the equations; a solved ODE (not a guessed exponential); $K = k_f/k_r$ identified; the two parameter cases distinguished as ratio versus sum; at least two distinguishing observations each with the model's prediction and the no-reaction prediction; the assumptions listed. The simulator comparison must report an actual number read from the model and say whether it agreed.
- **Honesty requirements:** the report must call its outputs model outputs. Any sentence of the form "this shows that real reactions..." needs the assumption it depends on stated in the same sentence; otherwise quote it as the first substantive gap. The activity is virtual; if the learner describes anything as a bench procedure, correct it explicitly.
- **First-gap localisation:** most commonly $\tau = 1/k_f$ (forgetting the reverse rate contributes to relaxation) or the half-life of $A$ in place of the half-life of the deviation. Quote the line.
- **Alternate approaches:** matrix eigenvalue treatment is likely from this learner and is fully acceptable; the zero eigenvalue is the conservation law and deserves a comment. Dimensionless time $k_f t$ is fine if the final numbers are converted back to minutes.
- **Confidence:** high when all six parts are present with the numbers matching; medium when the derivation is right but the distinguishing observations are thin; route to `needs_review` if the learner proposes a distinguishing observation the model cannot express (for example a spectroscopic measurement) and argues from real chemistry, since that may be correct but is outside the model's evidence.
- Keep feedback structured by the six parts; under 250 words.
