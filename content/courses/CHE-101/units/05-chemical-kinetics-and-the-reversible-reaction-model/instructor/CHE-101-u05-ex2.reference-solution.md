## Reference reasoning (instructor only)

Rate defined as $r = \tfrac12\,d[\ce{NOCl}]/dt$.

**1. Bookkeeping.** Step 1 + step 2: $\ce{NO + Cl2 + NOCl2 + NO -> NOCl2 + 2 NOCl}$; cancelling the intermediate $\ce{NOCl2}$ gives $\ce{2 NO + Cl2 -> 2 NOCl}$. Since each step-2 event forms two $\ce{NOCl}$, $d[\ce{NOCl}]/dt = 2k_2[\ce{NOCl2}][\ce{NO}]$ and $r = k_2[\ce{NOCl2}][\ce{NO}]$.

**2. Pre-equilibrium (step 1 fast and reversible, step 2 slow).** $k_1[\ce{NO}][\ce{Cl2}] = k_{-1}[\ce{NOCl2}]$, so $[\ce{NOCl2}] = (k_1/k_{-1})[\ce{NO}][\ce{Cl2}] = K_1[\ce{NO}][\ce{Cl2}]$. Then $r = k_2K_1[\ce{NO}]^2[\ce{Cl2}]$: second order in NO, first in $\ce{Cl2}$, matching exercise 1 with $k_{\text{obs}} = k_1k_2/k_{-1}$. (If the learner defines the rate as $d[\ce{NOCl}]/dt$ instead, $k_{\text{obs}} = 2k_1k_2/k_{-1}$; either is correct if the definition is stated. The lecture 32 notes make the same point for $\ce{NO + O2}$.)

**3. Slow first step.** The first step is rate determining and effectively irreversible: $r = k_1[\ce{NO}][\ce{Cl2}]$ (each step-1 event leads to two $\ce{NOCl}$ through the fast step 2, so $d[\ce{NOCl}]/dt = 2k_1[\ce{NO}][\ce{Cl2}]$ and $r = k_1[\ce{NO}][\ce{Cl2}]$). First order in NO: inconsistent with the observed second order. Refuted.

**4. Steady state.** $d[\ce{NOCl2}]/dt = k_1[\ce{NO}][\ce{Cl2}] - k_{-1}[\ce{NOCl2}] - k_2[\ce{NOCl2}][\ce{NO}] = 0$ gives
$$[\ce{NOCl2}] = \frac{k_1[\ce{NO}][\ce{Cl2}]}{k_{-1} + k_2[\ce{NO}]}, \qquad r = \frac{k_1k_2[\ce{NO}]^2[\ce{Cl2}]}{k_{-1} + k_2[\ce{NO}]}.$$
Limit $k_{-1} \gg k_2[\ce{NO}]$ (the intermediate falls apart far faster than it reacts on): $r \to (k_1k_2/k_{-1})[\ce{NO}]^2[\ce{Cl2}]$, part 2. Limit $k_{-1} \ll k_2[\ce{NO}]$ (almost every intermediate reacts on): $r \to k_1[\ce{NO}][\ce{Cl2}]$, part 3.

**5. Distinguishing experiment on the model.** Vary $[\ce{NO}]$ over a wide range at fixed $[\ce{Cl2}]$ and measure the apparent order in NO (slope of $\ln r$ against $\ln[\ce{NO}]$). The steady-state law predicts order 2 when $k_2[\ce{NO}] \ll k_{-1}$, falling towards 1 as $[\ce{NO}]$ grows past $k_{-1}/k_2$; a strict pre-equilibrium predicts order 2 throughout; a strict slow first step predicts order 1 throughout. Observing the crossover, and the concentration at which it occurs, measures $k_{-1}/k_2$.

**Alternative valid approaches.** Writing the rate in terms of $\ce{Cl2}$ consumption ($-d[\ce{Cl2}]/dt = k_1[\ce{NO}][\ce{Cl2}] - k_{-1}[\ce{NOCl2}]$) and using steady state to simplify; the result is the same. Accept either rate convention if stated.

**Common gaps.** Losing or doubling the factor 2; writing the pre-equilibrium with $k_1/k_2$; forgetting the $k_2[\ce{NO}]$ term in the steady-state balance; stating the limits as "fast" and "slow" without an inequality between rate terms; proposing to "measure the intermediate" without saying what would be seen.
