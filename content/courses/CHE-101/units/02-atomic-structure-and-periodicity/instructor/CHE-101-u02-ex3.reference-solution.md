# Periodic trends — complete reference reasoning

1. Radius, largest first: K > Na > Mg > Cl. K has a larger occupied shell than the period-3 atoms; across that period increasing effective nuclear attraction generally contracts the electron cloud. First ionisation energy, lowest first: K < Na < Mg < Cl, reflecting the more weakly bound outer electron down the group and stronger binding across the period, with configuration-dependent exceptions.
2. B loses a 2p electron, which is less tightly bound than Be's filled 2s electron. O has a paired electron in its 2p configuration, whereas N has three singly occupied 2p orbitals; electron-repulsion and configuration effects make removal from O easier than the simple across-period trend suggests. These are qualitative many-electron explanations, not exact independent-orbital total-energy calculations.
3. Photoelectron spectroscopy compares a known photon energy with emitted-electron kinetic energies to determine binding-energy differences under the stated gas-phase model. At the same photon energy, an outermost Mg electron with greater kinetic energy than Na's would imply a lower Mg ionisation energy, contradicting the proposed Na < Mg ordering. State the species and comparable transitions; real resolved spectra can have additional structure.
4. With EA = −ΔE for X(g)+e⁻→X⁻(g), chlorine releases energy on attachment into its nearly complete valence shell, giving positive EA. Neon has a closed shell and adding another electron is unfavourable in the elementary trend model; it does not form the analogous stable ground-state monatomic anion. Sign conventions differ among sources, so quote this one explicitly.

Accept equivalent configuration-based explanations and explicit limitations. The pair of exceptions is evidence against treating periodic trends as exceptionless rules.
