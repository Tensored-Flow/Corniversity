## Reference reasoning (instructor only)

**1. Configurations.** S ($Z = 16$): $[\text{Ne}]\,3s^2\,3p^4$. Fe ($Z = 26$): $[\text{Ar}]\,3d^6\,4s^2$. $\ce{Fe^3+}$: remove the two $4s$ electrons and one $3d$ electron: $[\text{Ar}]\,3d^5$. For the isolated gas-phase ground-state ion, five $d$ electrons in five orbitals, one each with parallel spins (Hund's rule): five unpaired electrons. (A learner who writes $[\text{Ar}]\,3d^3\,4s^2$ has removed $3d$ electrons first; this is the targeted misconception.)

**2. The four-peak spectrum.** Under the stipulated unresolved-subshell model, one peak per occupied subshell; actual resolved spectra can have additional structure. In filling order the subshells are $1s, 2s, 2p, 3s, 3p, \dots$; exactly four occupied means $1s, 2s, 2p, 3s$ with nothing in $3p$. Elements with electrons in $3s$ but none in $3p$: sodium ($[\text{Ne}]\,3s^1$) and magnesium ($[\text{Ne}]\,3s^2$). (The lecture 8 notes pose the analogous question for five peaks; this one is set for four so the answer cannot be copied.) Note the spectrum does not reveal the number of electrons in a subshell from peak count alone; peak intensities would be needed to separate Na from Mg.

**3. $4s$ versus $3d$.** Orbital energies in a multi-electron atom depend on $Z$ and on the occupancy of the other orbitals. At K and Ca the empty $3d$ orbitals lie above $4s$, so $4s$ fills first. As $3d$ fills across the transition series the $3d$ electrons, which penetrate less but are closer in on average, drop below $4s$ in energy; in neutral Fe the $4s$ electrons are the least tightly bound and are removed first, which is why $\ce{Fe^2+}$ is $[\text{Ar}]\,3d^6$ and $\ce{Fe^3+}$ is $[\text{Ar}]\,3d^5$. Both statements are true because "filling order" and "energy order in the finished atom" are different orderings.

**Alternative valid approaches.** Full configurations without noble-gas cores are fine. Accept $3d^5$ written with a superscript, caret or plain digit. In part 2, a learner who adds that peak intensities (electron counts) would be needed to tell Na from Mg has gone beyond the question; note it approvingly.

**Common gaps.** Fe written as $[\text{Ar}]\,4s^2\,3d^6$ is fine (ordering of notation is a convention); $\ce{Fe^3+}$ as $3d^3\,4s^2$ is wrong; "four peaks means four electrons" (beryllium) confuses electrons with subshells; naming only one of Na and Mg.
