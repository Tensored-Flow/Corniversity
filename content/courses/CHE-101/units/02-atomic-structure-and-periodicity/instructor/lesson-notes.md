## Lesson notes (instructor only)

**Source mapping (all opened 2026-10-30).** Lecture 2 notes: Thomson (PDF page 1), Rutherford interpretation and nuclear charge (page 4), Coulomb force law (page 5), classical collapse and the need for quantum mechanics (page 6). Lectures 3–7 pages: topics and Atkins and Jones reading sections (replaced here by OpenStax 6.1–6.4). Lecture 8 notes: IE definitions (page 1), trends and the Be/B and N/O glitches (page 2), photoelectron spectroscopy with the five-peak example (page 3), electron affinity with Cl and N values and the EA = −ΔE convention (page 4). OpenStax 6.2 gives the Bohr constant as k = 2.179 × 10⁻¹⁸ J; the lesson calls it R_H and says so.

**Teaching intent.** The learner's mathematics makes the Schrödinger equation unthreatening; the chemistry to install is (a) the measurement-model pairing (photoelectric effect ↔ photon; line spectra ↔ levels; PES ↔ shielded orbitals), (b) configurations as a bookkeeping skill with the 4s/3d trap, and (c) trends argued from n and effective nuclear charge rather than memorised. Do not spend tutorial time on solving the hydrogen Schrödinger equation unless asked; if asked, point to OpenStax 6.3 and say the course uses the results.

**Tutor behaviour.** Always ask what is assumed (one-electron formula applies only to H-like species; work functions are given values, not looked up here). Separate the model of the atom from the spectroscopic data that supports it.

**Staged peer misconception, if peers are enabled.** A classmate claims brighter light makes faster photoelectrons; resolve through KE_max = hν − φ and the intensity-flux distinction in the same discussion.
