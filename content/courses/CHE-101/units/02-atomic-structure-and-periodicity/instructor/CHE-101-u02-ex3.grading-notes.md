## Grading notes (instructor only)

This is an explanation exercise; grade the chemistry of the reasons, not the prose.

**Expected content.**
- Radius: K > Na > Mg > Cl. K is in period 4 (larger $n$); across period 3, Na → Mg → Cl, $Z$ grows at fixed $n$ and the outer electrons are pulled in.
- First IE: K < Na < Mg < Cl, the same two effects in reverse; the automatic check `trend-direction` flags the reversed group trend.
- Glitches: B ($[\text{He}]\,2s^2\,2p^1$) has its outermost electron in $2p$, higher in energy and better shielded than Be's $2s$, so IE(B) < IE(Be). O ($2p^4$) must pair its fourth $2p$ electron; the pairing repulsion makes it easier to remove than N's unpaired $2p^3$ electron, so IE(O) < IE(N). Lecture 8 notes page 2 states both.
- Measurement: photoelectron spectroscopy, $h\nu = \text{IE} + \text{KE}$ at fixed photon energy (notes page 3). A contradicting observation: the outermost peak of Mg at higher kinetic energy than that of Na, which would mean IE(Mg) < IE(Na). Any concrete observation of that type is acceptable.
- EA with the notes' convention $\text{EA} = -\Delta E$: Cl ($+349$ kJ/mol) because the added electron completes $3p$ and is bound by a large effective nuclear charge; Ne unfavourable because the added electron would occupy $3s$, a new shell with little net attraction. If the learner uses the opposite sign convention consistently (and says so), accept and note the convention difference; penalise only inconsistency.

**Feedback.** Keep under 150 words. The most valuable comment is usually on part 3: many learners name "the periodic table" or "experiments" rather than a specific measurement; ask for the observable.

**Confidence.** High when all four parts are present and the group trend is correct; route to `needs_review` if the learner argues an alternative ranking from tabulated values they cite (for example quoting a real EA for Ne), since that may be correct data beyond the unit's model.
