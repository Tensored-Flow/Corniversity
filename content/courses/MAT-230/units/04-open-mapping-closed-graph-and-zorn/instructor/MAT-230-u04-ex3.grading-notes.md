## Grading notes (instructor only)

Expected argument: $E=\{S\subseteq V: S\text{ linearly independent}\}$ ordered by inclusion; nonempty since $\{v\}$ for $v\ne0$ is independent. For a chain $\mathcal C\subseteq E$, $U=\bigcup\mathcal C$ is independent: a finite dependence $\sum a_iu_i=0$ with $u_i\in S_i\in\mathcal C$ has all $u_i$ in the largest $S_i$ (chains are totally ordered), contradicting its independence; $U$ is an upper bound. Zorn gives a maximal $H$. If $v\notin\operatorname{span}H$ then $H\cup\{v\}$ is independent and strictly larger, contradicting maximality; so $H$ spans and is a Hamel basis.

Synthesis: by unit 3 exercise 2, in an infinite-dimensional Banach space any Hamel basis is uncountable and its existence is non-constructive, so coordinates with respect to it are useless analytically. The replacement is a basis with respect to which vectors are *infinite* convergent sums: orthonormal bases in Hilbert spaces (unit 9), where $v=\sum\langle v,e_n\rangle e_n$ converges in norm.

Accept "Schauder basis" as the replacement in general Banach spaces. Keep feedback brief.
