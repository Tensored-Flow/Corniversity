## Lesson notes (instructor only)

Source mapping, lecture 1 notes (Spring 2021, notes by Andrew Lin, following Melrose): Definition 3 (p. 1), Definition 5 and Example 6 (p. 2), Definition 7 (p. 2), Proposition 8 and Example 9 (p. 3), Definition 10, Proposition 11, Definition 12 (p. 4), Example 13, Definition 14, Example 15, Theorem 16 (p. 5). Page numbers here are PDF pages (index + 1); the file has no printed labels.

Teaching intent: the unit exists to install one habit, asking which hypothesis does the work. Keep the tutorial on exercise 1 until the learner has written the failing inequality in the correct direction. If they immediately know the answer (an Imperial mathematics background makes this likely), move quickly to exercise 2 and to the question in the reference notes about the reverse inequality.

Staged misconception for a peer discussion, if the learner enables peers: a classmate may float "all norms are equivalent, so this is fine"; the tutor must resolve it in the same discussion via the counterexample and the finite-dimensional hypothesis.
