## Grading notes (instructor only)

This is an explanation exercise; grade the ideas, not the prose.

- Must contain the idea that approximation or iteration produces Cauchy sequences, and that completeness guarantees the limit exists in the same space, so the "solution" is an element of the space one started in. Equating completeness with "closed" or "bounded" is a conceptual error worth a precise comment (closedness is relative to an ambient space; completeness is intrinsic).
- Acceptable examples: rationals converging to $\sqrt2$ in $\mathbb{Q}$; the ramp sequence of exercise 2 in $(C([0,1]),\|\cdot\|_1)$; polynomials converging uniformly to a non-polynomial (Cauchy in the sup norm in the space of polynomials, whose limit is outside that space). Any correct example counts.
- Keep feedback under 120 words; this is an explain-back, not a proof.
