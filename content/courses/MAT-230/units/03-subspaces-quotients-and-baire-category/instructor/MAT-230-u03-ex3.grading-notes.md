## Grading notes (instructor only)

Expected: (1) for fixed $x\in c_{00}$ there is $N$ with $x_n=0$ for $n>N$, so $|T_n(x)|\le\max_{n\le N}n|x_n|<\infty$. (2) $|T_n(x)|=n|x_n|\le n\|x\|_\infty$ gives $\|T_n\|\le n$; $T_n(e_n)=n$ with $\|e_n\|_\infty=1$ gives $\|T_n\|\ge n$; so $\|T_n\|=n\to\infty$. (3) No contradiction: $c_{00}$ with the sup norm is not complete (its closure is $c_0$), so the principle's hypothesis fails. In the proof, completeness is used when Baire's theorem is applied to $B=\bigcup_kC_k$ with $C_k=\{b:\|T_nb\|\le k\ \forall n\}$ closed; without completeness no $C_k$ need contain a ball, and indeed here none does.

Accept the alternative $S_n(x)=\sum_{k\le n}x_k$ on $c_{00}$ with the sup norm. For each fixed finitely supported $x$, these partial sums eventually become constant and hence are bounded. The triangle inequality gives $\|S_n\|\le n$, and the vector with its first $n$ coordinates equal to one and the rest zero gives equality. Therefore $\|S_n\|=n$, so this is another valid pointwise-bounded but not uniformly operator-bounded family. Give enough feedback to identify the mathematical issue; do not reject a valid alternative or impose an arbitrary word limit.
