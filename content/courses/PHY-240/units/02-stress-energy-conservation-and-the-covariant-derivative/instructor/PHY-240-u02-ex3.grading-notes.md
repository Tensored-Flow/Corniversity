## Grading notes (instructor only)

Explanation exercise at graduate level; grade the ideas, keep feedback under 120 words.

- Must contain: on the flat plane $\Gamma = 0$ in Cartesian coordinates and $\Gamma^\theta{}_{r\theta} = 1/r$, $\Gamma^r{}_{\theta\theta} = -r$ in polar coordinates; a tensor that vanishes in one chart vanishes in every chart; therefore $\Gamma$ is not a tensor. The structural reason: its transformation law contains an inhomogeneous term built from second derivatives of the coordinate map.
- Why $\nabla$ is a tensor: $\partial_\beta V^\mu$ carries the same kind of inhomogeneous term (with the opposite sign), so the sum $\partial_\beta V^\mu + \Gamma^\mu{}_{\alpha\beta}V^\alpha$ transforms homogeneously; geometrically, it is the component form of $\partial_\beta\mathbf V$ with the variation of the basis included.
- Curved versus flat: in a curved space there is no coordinate system in which all $\Gamma$ vanish on an open neighbourhood — only at a chosen point, to first order (local flatness, unit 3) — and the failure to do so at second order is curvature (unit 5). Unchanged: $\Gamma$ is still given by the same metric formula, $\nabla$ is defined the same way, $\nabla g = 0$ still holds.
- Conceptual error to correct precisely: "$\Gamma\neq0$ means the space is curved". Reply with the polar-coordinate example.
- Accept the language of connections/affine connections if the learner uses it correctly.
