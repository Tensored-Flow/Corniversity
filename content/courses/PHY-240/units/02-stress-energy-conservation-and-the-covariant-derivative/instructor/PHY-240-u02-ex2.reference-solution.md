## Reference reasoning (instructor only)

Signature $(-,+,+,+)$, $c = 1$ except where restored.

**1. Dust in the laboratory.** $u^\mu = \gamma(1,v,0,0)$, so $T^{\mu\nu} = \rho\gamma^2\begin{pmatrix}1 & v\\ v & v^2\end{pmatrix}$ in the $(t,x)$ block and zero otherwise. $T^{00} = \gamma^2\rho$: energy density, one $\gamma$ because each particle has energy $\gamma m$, one because the laboratory number density is $\gamma n$ (Lorentz contraction of the volume). $T^{0x} = \gamma^2\rho v$: energy flux (energy density times velocity) and equally the $x$-momentum density ($\gamma m v$ per particle times $\gamma n$). $T^{xx} = \gamma^2\rho v^2$: flux of $x$-momentum in the $x$ direction (momentum density times velocity) — the "ram pressure" of a stream. No transverse components: dust exerts no stress across surfaces it does not cross.

**2. Perfect fluid.** Rest frame $u^\mu = (1,\mathbf 0)$, $\eta^{\mu\nu} = \mathrm{diag}(-1,1,1,1)$: $T^{00} = (\rho+P)(1) + P(-1) = \rho$; $T^{ij} = 0 + P\delta^{ij}$; $T^{0i} = 0$. Uniqueness: the only symmetric $(2,0)$ tensors constructible from $u^\mu$ and $\eta^{\mu\nu}$ are $Au^\mu u^\nu + B\eta^{\mu\nu}$ with scalar $A,B$ (there is no other index structure available); matching the rest frame gives $B = P$ and $A - B = \rho$, so $A = \rho + P$. Since the expression is covariant, matching in one frame fixes it in all.

**3. Newtonian limit.** $\nu = 0$: $\partial_t[(\rho+P)\gamma^2 - P] + \partial_i[(\rho+P)\gamma^2v^i] = 0$. $\nu = j$: $\partial_t[(\rho+P)\gamma^2v^j] + \partial_i[(\rho+P)\gamma^2v^iv^j] + \partial_jP = 0$ (using $\partial_i(P\eta^{ij}) = \partial_jP$, $\partial_t(P\eta^{0j}) = 0$). Approximation (i): $v\ll c$, so $\gamma^2 = 1 + O(v^2/c^2)\to 1$; this discards the relativistic increase of energy and momentum density of moving matter. Approximation (ii): $P\ll\rho c^2$ (with $\rho$ an energy density; equivalently $P\ll\rho_{\rm mass}c^2$), so $\rho + P\to\rho$; this discards pressure as a source of inertia. With both: $\nu = 0$ gives $\partial_t\rho + \partial_i(\rho v^i) = 0$ (continuity; the $-\partial_tP$ term is also dropped, being $O(P)$). $\nu = j$: $\partial_t(\rho v^j) + \partial_i(\rho v^iv^j) + \partial_jP = 0$; expand $= v^j[\partial_t\rho + \partial_i(\rho v^i)] + \rho[\partial_tv^j + v^i\partial_iv^j] + \partial_jP$, and the bracket vanishes by continuity, leaving
$$\rho\,(\partial_t\mathbf v + \mathbf v\cdot\nabla\mathbf v) = -\nabla P .$$
Euler's equation. (Gravity is absent because this is special relativity; in Newtonian hydrodynamics $-\rho\nabla\Phi$ would be added, and in GR it arrives through $\nabla_\mu$ instead — unit 4.) Where the approximations fail: in a neutron star $P$ is a sizeable fraction of $\rho c^2$ and (ii) fails; in a relativistic jet (i) fails.

**4. Dust.** $\partial_\mu(\rho u^\mu u^\nu) = u^\nu\partial_\mu(\rho u^\mu) + \rho u^\mu\partial_\mu u^\nu = 0$. Contract with $u_\nu$: $(u_\nu u^\nu)\partial_\mu(\rho u^\mu) + \rho u^\mu u_\nu\partial_\mu u^\nu = -\partial_\mu(\rho u^\mu) + \rho u^\mu\cdot\tfrac12\partial_\mu(u\cdot u) = -\partial_\mu(\rho u^\mu) = 0$. So $\partial_\mu(\rho u^\mu) = 0$ (conservation of rest energy, equivalent to $\partial_\mu N^\mu = 0$ with $\rho = mn$), and then the original equation reduces to $\rho u^\mu\partial_\mu u^\nu = 0$, i.e. $du^\nu/d\tau = 0$ along each flow line: dust particles are unaccelerated and move on straight lines, as non-interacting free particles must. In curved spacetime the same manipulation with $\nabla_\mu$ gives $u^\mu\nabla_\mu u^\nu = 0$, the geodesic equation — the first hint that the equation of motion of matter is contained in the conservation law for $T$, which the field equation will enforce (unit 6).

**Alternative valid approaches.** Deriving the perfect-fluid Newtonian limit by first projecting $\partial_\mu T^{\mu\nu} = 0$ parallel and perpendicular to $u$ (giving $u^\mu\partial_\mu\rho + (\rho+P)\partial_\mu u^\mu = 0$ and $(\rho+P)u^\mu\partial_\mu u^\nu = -(\eta^{\mu\nu} + u^\mu u^\nu)\partial_\mu P$) is cleaner and fully acceptable; the Lecture Guide quotes the second form in Lecture 20. Then the limit is read off directly.

**Common gaps.** Forgetting the second $\gamma$ in $T^{00}$; writing $\eta^{\mu\nu}$ with the wrong sign so that the rest frame gives $T^{00} = \rho + 2P$; stating "Newtonian limit" without the two approximations (flagged by the automatic check); in part 4, not using $u\cdot\partial u = 0$ and so failing to separate the two equations.
