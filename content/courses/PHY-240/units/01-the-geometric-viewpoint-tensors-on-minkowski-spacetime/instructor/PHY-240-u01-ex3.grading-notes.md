## Grading notes (instructor only)

Explanation exercise at graduate level; grade the ideas, keep feedback under 120 words.

- Must contain: a tensor is one geometric object; components are its values on a basis; because the transformation law is built in, an equation between tensors that holds in one frame holds in every frame — which is what "the laws of physics are the same for all inertial observers" means operationally.
- Example: $N^\mu = nu^\mu$ with $n$ the rest-frame density (a scalar) and $u^\mu$ the 4-velocity. One observer reads $N^0$ as number density and $N^i$ as flux; $\partial_\mu N^\mu = 0$ is a single scalar statement, read by every observer as their own continuity equation $\partial_t(\gamma n) + \nabla\cdot(\gamma n\mathbf v) = 0$. Credit any correct alternative (e.g. $p^\mu$ conservation in a collision).
- Component insight, any one of: four numbers do not make a vector (transformation law is the content); upper versus lower index placement records which slot of the multilinear map is meant and makes contraction legitimate; the basis-dependence of components is exactly what lets us compute in a convenient frame.
- Do not penalise the learner for preferring the component definition in practice; the point is that both views agree and that the geometric one explains why the component rules are what they are.
