## Outline: The Einstein field equation

This unit is **outlined, not yet prepared**. It will be written from Lectures 12 and 13 of MIT 8.962 (Spring 2020; CC BY-NC-SA 4.0), using the Lecture Guide (PDF page 3) and the recorded lectures with transcripts, with Chapter 4 (The Einstein Equations) of Tong's *General Relativity* notes and Chapter 4 (Gravitation) of Carroll's 1997 notes as reference-only supplements.

**Scope.** The contracted Bianchi identity $\nabla^\mu G_{\mu\nu} = 0$ for the Einstein tensor $G_{\mu\nu} = R_{\mu\nu} - \tfrac12g_{\mu\nu}R$, and its trace $G^\mu{}_\mu = -R$; the argument for the field equation — the stress-energy tensor is the natural source, the left-hand side must be a symmetric, divergence-free two-index curvature tensor, hence $G_{\mu\nu} = \kappa T_{\mu\nu}$ — and the fixing of $\kappa = 8\pi G$ (or $8\pi G/c^4$) by demanding the Newtonian limit, Poisson's equation for $\Phi$, from the weak-field metric of units 5 and 7 (Lecture 12); the cosmological constant as the one further divergence-free term, $\Lambda g_{\mu\nu}$, and its reading as a perfect fluid with $\rho = -P = \Lambda/8\pi G$; the variational route (Lecture 13): the Einstein–Hilbert Lagrangian density $\sqrt{-g}R$ as the simplest curvature scalar, its variation yielding the field equation, the subtle boundary term (referred by the Guide to Wald's Appendix E), the Palatini variation in which metric and connection are varied independently, and why this makes general relativity "the simplest possible relativistic theory of gravity" while pointing at the higher-curvature alternatives. Signature $(-,+,+,+)$; units $G = c = 1$ with the SI constant $8\pi G/c^4 = 2.08\times10^{-43}\ \mathrm{s^2\,kg^{-1}\,m^{-1}}$ restored for the comparison with Poisson's equation.

**What preparation will add.** A full lesson, original exercises (the trace and divergence properties of $G_{\mu\nu}$ proved from the Bianchi identity, the Newtonian normalisation carried out, the cosmological constant as a fluid, an explain-back on why $R_{\mu\nu} = \kappa T_{\mu\nu}$ fails), instructor reference reasoning, an exit check and the unit's common misconceptions. The mid-course synthesis (assessment a2) follows this unit. Until then the reading list is accurate and the tutor can discuss the material, but there is no assessed work for this unit.
