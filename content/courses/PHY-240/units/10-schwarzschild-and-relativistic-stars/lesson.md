## Outline: Spherical compact sources — Schwarzschild spacetime and relativistic stars

This unit is **outlined, not yet prepared**. It will be written from Lectures 20 and 21 of MIT 8.962 (Spring 2020; CC BY-NC-SA 4.0), using the Lecture Guide (PDF pages 8–10) and the recorded lectures with transcripts, with Section 1.3 (A First Look at the Schwarzschild Metric) of Tong's *General Relativity* notes and Chapter 7 (The Schwarzschild Solution and Black Holes) of Carroll's 1997 notes as reference-only supplements. The Guide notes that the handwritten notes for these lectures carry labels shifted by one; the video titles are used here.

**Scope.** A compact source with $T_{\mu\nu}\neq0$ for $r\le R_S$ and vacuum outside, asymptotically flat; the general static spherically symmetric line element $ds^2 = -e^{2\Phi(r)}dt^2 + e^{2\Lambda(r)}dr^2 + r^2d\Omega^2$ with $r$ the areal radius; the exterior vacuum equations $\Phi = -\Lambda$ and $\partial_r(re^{2\Phi}) = 1$, the integration constant fixed by non-relativistic radial infall in the weak field to give the Schwarzschild metric $ds^2 = -(1-2GM/r)dt^2 + (1-2GM/r)^{-1}dr^2 + r^2d\Omega^2$, and Birkhoff's theorem (Lecture 20); the interior with a perfect fluid, the mass function $m(r)$ with $dm/dr = 4\pi r^2\rho$, the Tolman–Oppenheimer–Volkoff equations for $\Phi$ and $P$, and the need for an equation of state; constant-density stars, the central pressure as a function of $R_S$, its divergence at $GM/R_S = 4/9$ and Buchdahl's theorem $R_S > 9GM/4$ (stated, proof beyond the course); polytropes and the distinction between rest-mass density and energy density (Lecture 21); the classic tests from Tong's Section 1.3 — perihelion precession and light bending — as the observational anchor. Signature $(-,+,+,+)$; $G = c = 1$ with $2GM_\odot/c^2 = 2.95\ \mathrm{km}$ quoted for scale.

**What preparation will add.** A full lesson, original exercises (the exterior vacuum equations solved from the field equation for the static spherical ansatz, the TOV equations applied to a constant-density star with the Buchdahl bound recovered, perihelion precession from the effective potential, an explain-back on Birkhoff's theorem), instructor reference reasoning, an exit check and the unit's common misconceptions. Until then the reading list is accurate and the tutor can discuss the material, but there is no assessed work for this unit.
