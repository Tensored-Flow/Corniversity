## Outline: Cosmology — Robertson–Walker spacetimes and the Friedmann equations

This unit is **outlined, not yet prepared**. It will be written from Lectures 18 and 19 of MIT 8.962 (Spring 2020; CC BY-NC-SA 4.0), using the Lecture Guide (PDF pages 5–8) and the recorded lectures with transcripts, with Chapter 8 (Cosmology) of Carroll's 1997 notes as a reference-only supplement (read as a 1997 text: it predates the accelerating-universe observations).

**Scope.** Solving the field equation by imposing symmetry: maximally symmetric spaces with $n(n+1)/2$ Killing vectors and Riemann tensor $R_{\alpha\mu\beta\nu} = R(g_{\alpha\beta}g_{\mu\nu} - g_{\alpha\nu}g_{\beta\mu})/[n(n-1)]$; spatial homogeneity and isotropy without temporal isotropy, comoving coordinates and the Robertson–Walker metric $ds^2 = -dt^2 + a^2(t)[dr^2/(1-\kappa r^2) + r^2d\Omega^2]$, with $k = -1,0,+1$ as hyperbolic, flat and spherical spatial sections ($S_k(\chi) = \sinh\chi,\chi,\sin\chi$) and the Guide's caution that "flat" here refers to the slices while spacetime is curved; the perfect-fluid source and $\nabla_\mu T^{\mu0} = 0$ as the first law $d(\rho a^3) = -P\,d(a^3)$; the Friedmann equations $(\dot a/a)^2 = 8\pi G\rho/3 - \kappa/a^2$ and $\ddot a/a = -4\pi G(\rho + 3P)/3$; the Hubble parameter, critical density and $\Omega - 1 = \kappa/(H^2a^2)$; equations of state $P = w\rho$ with $\rho\propto a^{-3(1+w)}$ for matter, radiation and a cosmological constant (Lecture 18); the inverse problem — measuring $a(t)$ — through the Killing tensor $K_{\mu\nu} = a^2(g_{\mu\nu} + u_\mu u_\nu)$ and the redshift $a_{\rm emit} = 1/(1+z)$; luminosity, angular-diameter and proper-motion distances with $D_L = (1+z)D_M = (1+z)^2D_A$; the flatness and horizon problems and inflation as their proposed resolution (Lecture 19). Signature $(-,+,+,+)$; $G = c = 1$ with $H_0$ quoted in $\mathrm{km\,s^{-1}\,Mpc^{-1}}$. Observational statements are reported as the Guide reports them, dated to 2020.

**What preparation will add.** A full lesson, original exercises (the Friedmann equations derived from the field equation for the RW metric, the $a(t)$ solutions for single-component flat universes with their limiting behaviour, the redshift from the Killing tensor, an explain-back on what "flat universe" does and does not mean), instructor reference reasoning, an exit check and the unit's common misconceptions. Until then the reading list is accurate and the tutor can discuss the material, but there is no assessed work for this unit.
