## Outline: Curvature — the Riemann tensor, Bianchi identity and geodesic deviation

This unit is **outlined, not yet prepared**. It will be written from Lectures 10 and 11 of MIT 8.962 (Spring 2020; CC BY-NC-SA 4.0), using the Lecture Guide (PDF pages 2–3) and the recorded lectures with transcripts, with Chapter 3 (Introducing Riemannian Geometry) of Tong's *General Relativity* notes and Chapter 3 (Curvature) of Carroll's 1997 notes as reference-only supplements.

**Scope.** Geodesics in the weak-field spacetime $ds^2 = -(1+2\Phi)dt^2 + (1-2\Phi)(dx^2+dy^2+dz^2)$ and the recovery of Newton's equation of motion in the slow-motion limit, with $\Phi$ the gravitational potential (Lecture 10; the spacetime itself is justified in unit 7); the Riemann tensor from parallel transport of a vector around a small parallelogram, $\delta V^\mu = R^\mu{}_{\nu\alpha\beta}V^\nu\delta x^\alpha\delta y^\beta$, equivalently from the commutator of covariant derivatives $[\nabla_\mu,\nabla_\nu]p^\alpha = R^\alpha{}_{\beta\mu\nu}p^\beta$; the symmetries of Riemann and the count of twenty independent components in four dimensions, matching the twenty constraints that local flatness could not remove (unit 3); the Ricci tensor $R_{\mu\nu} = R^\alpha{}_{\mu\alpha\nu}$ and scalar $R$ (Lecture 11); geodesic deviation $u^\beta\nabla_\beta(u^\alpha\nabla_\alpha X^\mu) = R^\mu{}_{\gamma\delta\nu}u^\gamma u^\delta X^\nu$ as the precise statement that curvature is tides, compared with the Newtonian tidal tensor $\partial_i\partial_j\Phi$; the Bianchi identity $\nabla_\alpha R_{\beta\gamma\mu\nu} + \nabla_\beta R_{\gamma\alpha\mu\nu} + \nabla_\gamma R_{\alpha\beta\mu\nu} = 0$. Signature $(-,+,+,+)$; sign conventions for Riemann are stated explicitly, since sources differ.

**What preparation will add.** A full lesson, original exercises (a complete Riemann computation for a two-dimensional metric such as the sphere with the Gauss curvature recovered, the component count from the symmetries, geodesic deviation in the weak-field metric compared with Newtonian tides, an explain-back), instructor reference reasoning, an exit check and the unit's common misconceptions.
