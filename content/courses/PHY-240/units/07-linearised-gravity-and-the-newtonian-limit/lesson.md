## Outline: Linearised gravity and the Newtonian limit

This unit is **outlined, not yet prepared**. It will be written from Lectures 14 and 15 of MIT 8.962 (Spring 2020; CC BY-NC-SA 4.0), using the Lecture Guide (PDF page 4) and the recorded lectures with transcripts, with Chapter 5 (When Gravity is Weak) of Tong's *General Relativity* notes and Chapter 6 (Weak Fields and Gravitational Radiation) of Carroll's 1997 notes as reference-only supplements.

**Scope.** The two strategies for solving ten coupled non-linear partial differential equations — linearise about flat spacetime, or impose a symmetry — with the third (numerical relativity) named and set aside as the Guide does; the expansion $g_{\mu\nu} = \eta_{\mu\nu} + h_{\mu\nu}$ with $O(h^2)$ dropped; infinitesimal coordinate transformations $x^\alpha\to x^\alpha + \xi^\alpha$ as gauge transformations $h_{\mu\nu}\to h_{\mu\nu} - \partial_\mu\xi_\nu - \partial_\nu\xi_\mu$ that leave the linearised curvature unchanged, in exact analogy with electrodynamics; the trace-reversed perturbation $\bar h_{\mu\nu} = h_{\mu\nu} - \tfrac12\eta_{\mu\nu}h$ and the Lorenz gauge in which the field equation becomes the wave equation $\Box\bar h_{\mu\nu} = -16\pi G\,T_{\mu\nu}$; the static limit, Poisson's equation and the Newtonian metric $ds^2 = -(1+2\Phi)dt^2 + (1-2\Phi)(dx^2+dy^2+dz^2)$ that units 5 and 6 used (Lecture 14); the radiative Green's function for non-static sources and the apparent radiativity of all ten components; the gauge-invariant content of $h_{\mu\nu}$ — six physical degrees of freedom, four obeying Poisson-type equations and two radiative, in the transverse-traceless spatial components — presented, as the Guide says, "on the advanced side" (Lecture 15). Signature $(-,+,+,+)$; the regime of validity $|h|\ll 1$ stated and checked for the solar system ($GM_\odot/(c^2R_\odot)\approx2\times10^{-6}$).

**What preparation will add.** A full lesson, original exercises (the gauge transformation of $h$ and the invariance of the linearised Riemann tensor, the Newtonian limit from $\Box\bar h = -16\pi GT$ with the two factors of $2\Phi$ explained, an estimate of $h$ for the Sun and for Earth's surface, an explain-back on gauge), instructor reference reasoning, an exit check and the unit's common misconceptions. Until then the reading list is accurate and the tutor can discuss the material, but there is no assessed work for this unit.
