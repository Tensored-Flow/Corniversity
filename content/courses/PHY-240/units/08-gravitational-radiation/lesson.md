## Outline: Gravitational radiation

This unit is **outlined, not yet prepared**. It will be written from Lectures 16 and 17 of MIT 8.962 (Spring 2020; CC BY-NC-SA 4.0), using the Lecture Guide (PDF pages 4–5) and the recorded lectures with transcripts, with Chapter 5 (When Gravity is Weak) of Tong's *General Relativity* notes and Chapter 6 (Weak Fields and Gravitational Radiation) of Carroll's 1997 notes as reference-only supplements.

**Scope.** A metric perturbation propagating along $z$ at the speed of light, made transverse (no $t$ or $z$ components) and traceless, leaving the two polarisations $h_{xx} = -h_{yy} = h_+$ and $h_{xy} = h_{yx} = h_\times$; why the geodesic equation shows test bodies at rest staying at rest in these coordinates, and why the physical content is instead the proper separation of two bodies or their geodesic deviation, which stretches and squeezes according to $h_+$, $h_\times$ and the geometry; the solution of the linearised field equation for a slowly varying source and the leading-order dependence of radiation on the second time derivative of the source's mass quadrupole moment, with the projection tensors that enforce transversality and tracelessness, in analogy with the dipole radiation of electrodynamics (Lecture 16); the more advanced material of Lecture 17, presented schematically as the Guide does: separating radiation from a slowly varying background by a separation of scales, the necessary non-locality of gravitational-wave energy (no wave at a single point in a freely falling frame), averaging over several wavelengths, second-order perturbation theory and the effective stress-energy tensor of the waves, and the power carried away as the square of the third time derivative of the quadrupole moment. Signature $(-,+,+,+)$; SI estimates of $h$ for a binary at a stated distance are included so that the detector sensitivities quoted in the literature can be interpreted.

**What preparation will add.** A full lesson, original exercises (the transverse-traceless reduction of $h_{\mu\nu}$, geodesic deviation of a ring of test masses under $h_+$ and $h_\times$, an order-of-magnitude strain and power estimate for a binary with every approximation named, an explain-back on why gravitational-wave energy is non-local), instructor reference reasoning, an exit check and the unit's common misconceptions. Until then the reading list is accurate and the tutor can discuss the material, but there is no assessed work for this unit.
