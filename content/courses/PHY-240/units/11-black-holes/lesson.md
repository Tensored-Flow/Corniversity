## Outline: Black holes

This unit is **outlined, not yet prepared**. It will be written from the end of Lecture 21 and Lectures 22 and 23 of MIT 8.962 (Spring 2020; CC BY-NC-SA 4.0), using the Lecture Guide (PDF pages 10–12) and the recorded lectures with transcripts, with Chapter 6 (Black Holes) of Tong's *General Relativity* notes and Chapter 7 (The Schwarzschild Solution and Black Holes) of Carroll's 1997 notes as reference-only supplements. Lectures 24 and 25 (unrecorded advanced topics) are outside the syllabus.

**Scope.** The Schwarzschild metric taken as valid for all $r$: a vacuum with mass, compared with the Coulomb point charge and the limits of the analogy; the two suspicious radii $r = 0$ and $r = 2GM$, the Kretschmann scalar $R_{\alpha\beta\gamma\delta}R^{\alpha\beta\gamma\delta} = 48G^2M^2/r^6$ showing a genuine singularity only at $r = 0$, and the coordinate singularity at $r = 2GM$; radial infall in proper time versus coordinate time, reaching $r = 0$ in finite $\tau$ while $t\to\infty$ as $r\to2GM$ (end of Lecture 21); what the coordinate $t$ means — time on the clocks of distant stationary observers, connected inward by light signals — and how the behaviour of light near $2GM$ explains the divergence; the horizon as a one-way surface, gravitational redshift from near the horizon, and coordinates adapted to infalling light (Lecture 22); testing black-hole spacetimes through the motion of light and matter: effective potentials, the innermost stable circular orbit, the photon sphere, and the observables that follow (Lecture 23). Signature $(-,+,+,+)$; $G = c = 1$ with the solar Schwarzschild radius quoted for scale. The connection to the Rindler horizon of unit 1's hyperbolic observer is drawn explicitly.

**What preparation will add.** A full lesson, original exercises (the Kretschmann scalar for Schwarzschild, radial infall in proper and coordinate time with the limits taken, the effective potential and the innermost stable circular orbit at $r = 6GM$, an explain-back on the difference between a coordinate and a curvature singularity), instructor reference reasoning, an exit check and the unit's common misconceptions. The final synthesis project (assessment a3) follows this unit. Until then the reading list is accurate and the tutor can discuss the material, but there is no assessed work for this unit.
