## Outline: Parallel transport, geodesics, Lie derivatives and Killing vectors

This unit is **outlined, not yet prepared**. It will be written from Lectures 7 (second half), 8 and 9 of MIT 8.962 (Spring 2020; CC BY-NC-SA 4.0), using the Lecture Guide (PDF page 2) and the recorded lectures with transcripts, with Tong's *General Relativity* Sections 1.1.1 (The Geodesic Equation) and 1.2.6 (Geodesics in Spacetime) and Chapter 2, and Carroll's Chapters 2 (Manifolds) and 3 (Curvature, first half) as reference-only supplements.

**Scope.** Why comparing tensors at different points needs a transport law (basis objects live in the tangent space at each point); transport method 1 — connection coefficients that, if the metric is to have zero derivative under transport, are the Christoffel symbols of unit 2, and the covariant derivative that results; parallel transport as holding components fixed in the freely falling frame, extended to tensors of any rank (Lecture 7); transport method 2 — Lie transport and the Lie derivative, its appearance in fluid flow, and the Killing vectors $\xi$ with $\mathcal L_\xi g = 0$, Killing's equation $\nabla_{(\alpha}\xi_{\beta)} = 0$; tensor densities, $\sqrt{|g|}$ and the properly tensorial Levi-Civita tensor $\epsilon_{\alpha\beta\gamma\delta} = \sqrt{|g|}\,\tilde\epsilon_{\alpha\beta\gamma\delta}$, and the "party tricks" with $\det g$ including $\nabla_\alpha V^\alpha = |g|^{-1/2}\partial_\alpha(|g|^{1/2}V^\alpha)$ promised in unit 2 (Lecture 8); the kinematics of a test mass: inertial motion in the freely falling frame, the frame-independent statement $u^\alpha\nabla_\alpha u^\beta = 0$, the geodesic as the extremal (maximal) proper-time curve, the null version with $p^\alpha$, and conserved quantities $p_a$ for cyclic coordinates and $p_\alpha\xi^\alpha$ for Killing vectors (Lecture 9). Signature $(-,+,+,+)$.

**What preparation will add.** A full lesson, original exercises (the geodesic equation from the proper-time action compared with the parallel-transport form, Killing vectors of a given metric and the resulting conserved quantities, an explain-back on the two transport laws), instructor reference reasoning, an exit check and the unit's common misconceptions. Until then the reading list is accurate and the tutor can discuss the material, but there is no assessed work for this unit.
