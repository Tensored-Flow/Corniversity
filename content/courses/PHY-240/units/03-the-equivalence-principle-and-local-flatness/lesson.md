## Outline: The equivalence principle and local flatness

This unit is **outlined, not yet prepared**. It will be written from Lectures 6 and 7 of MIT 8.962 (Spring 2020; CC BY-NC-SA 4.0), using the Lecture Guide's synopses (PDF pages 1–2) and the recorded lectures with transcripts, with Section 1.2.4 (The Equivalence Principle) and 1.2.5 (Gravitational Time Dilation) of Tong's *General Relativity* notes and Chapter 4 (Gravitation) of Carroll's 1997 notes as reference-only supplements.

**Here curvature enters.** Units 1–2 were flat spacetime. From this unit on, the metric $g_{\mu\nu}$ is a field to be determined, and the question is what replaces the inertial frames of special relativity.

**Scope.** Freely falling frames as the generalisation of inertial frames, because gravity couples to the same mass that appears in $\mathbf F = m\mathbf a$ (Lectures 6–7); the weak equivalence principle (free fall is locally indistinguishable from uniform acceleration over regions small enough that tides can be neglected) and the Einstein equivalence principle (in a freely falling frame the local laws of physics are those of special relativity), with the strong form mentioned and set aside as the Guide does; gravitational redshift and time dilation as the first consequence, derived from the equivalence principle and checked against the Pound–Rebka magnitude $gh/c^2$; local flatness by counting (Lecture 7): a general coordinate transformation has exactly enough freedom to set $g_{\mu\nu} = \eta_{\mu\nu}$ and $\partial g = 0$ at a point, with six leftover degrees of freedom (three boosts, three rotations) at zeroth order and a deficit of twenty at second order — the twenty components of the curvature tensor that unit 5 will exhibit. Signature $(-,+,+,+)$; $c$ restored for the redshift comparison.

**What preparation will add.** A full lesson, original exercises (the local-flatness count carried out explicitly, a gravitational-redshift derivation with the regime of validity, an explain-back distinguishing the weak and Einstein forms), instructor reference reasoning, an exit check and the unit's common misconceptions. Until then the reading list is accurate and the tutor can discuss the material, but there is no assessed work for this unit.
