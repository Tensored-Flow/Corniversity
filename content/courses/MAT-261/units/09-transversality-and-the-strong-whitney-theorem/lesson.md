## Outline: transversality and the strong Whitney embedding theorem

This unit is **outlined, not yet prepared**. It will be written from lectures 20–22 of the MIT 18.965 Fall 2004 notes (CC BY-NC-SA 4.0), keeping the notes' statement numbering (Definition 18.1, Theorems 18.3, 18.5, 18.8 and 19.1).

**Scope.** A smooth map $f:M\to N$ is transversal to a submanifold $Z\subseteq N$ when the image of its differential and the tangent space of $Z$ span $TN$ along $f^{-1}(Z)$; then $f^{-1}(Z)$ is a submanifold of the expected codimension. The parametric transversality theorems show that for a family $F:M\times S\to N$ which is a submersion, almost every member $F(\cdot,s)$ is transversal to $Z$, by applying Sard's theorem to a projection. These tools give the strong Whitney embedding theorem of 1944: every compact $n$-manifold embeds in $\mathbb{R}^{2n}$, with the Whitney trick removing pairs of intersection points of an immersion.
