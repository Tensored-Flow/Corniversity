## Grading notes (instructor only)

This is an explanation exercise; grade the ideas, not the prose.

- Hausdorff: the witness is the line with two origins (lecture 2, §3; exercise 1). Acceptable consequences: uniqueness of limits; compact subsets are closed; the diagonal is closed; later, that a sequence or a flow line has a well-defined limit. Equating Hausdorff with "metrisable" is an overstatement (metrisability also needs second countability, by Urysohn) and deserves a precise comment, not a penalty.
- Second countability: the witness is the long line (lecture 2, pages 1–2). Acceptable consequences: existence of smooth partitions of unity (lecture 8, §9; Hitchin §6.2 and §10.4); embedding of compact manifolds in $\mathbb{R}^N$ (lecture 9); a countable atlas; paracompactness. Saying "so that the manifold is small enough to embed" is fine if the connection to countability is stated.
- Must state explicitly that the atlas alone gives neither axiom (the notes' sentence in §3). A learner who says "Hausdorff follows because charts are homeomorphisms onto Hausdorff sets" has the standard misconception: locally Hausdorff is not Hausdorff. Correct it with the two origins.
- Give enough feedback to identify the first substantive gap; do not impose an arbitrary length cap.
