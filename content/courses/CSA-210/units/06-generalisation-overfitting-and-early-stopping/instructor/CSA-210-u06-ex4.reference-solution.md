# Evidence audit: a synthetic wearable pipeline — reference reasoning

A complete audit identifies mechanisms, not just labels. Random windows can put the same volunteer in multiple splits, so new-patient performance is not estimated. Group by volunteer and respect prediction-time ordering; ensure any feature was available before the target event. Fit normalisation only on the training partition. Remove or redefine the post-event note feature because it directly accesses unavailable future information. The repeatedly inspected named test split is validation after architecture/threshold selection; a fresh independent test/confirmation set is needed for a final estimate. All seed outcomes or a predeclared seed-aggregation procedure should be reported; choosing the best seed adds selection optimism. Compare with 90% majority baseline and informative minority metrics such as sensitivity/specificity/precision at a validation-selected threshold; report prevalence and calibration if probabilities are claimed. Count independent volunteers, not 2000 windows, for uncertainty appropriate to the estimand; use group-level resampling or a stated hierarchical analysis. Predeclare early-stopping metric/patience/ties, save and restore copied validation-best states and freeze preprocessing/model/threshold before test. Later-clinic performance requires a suitably sampled temporal/external evaluation and monitoring of relevant shifts; the original 94% cannot support the deployment claim. Existing code and training diagnostics may be useful for debugging, and contaminated results should be retained as exploratory observations with their limits, not relabelled as clean test evidence. A falsifiable prediction could state in advance that the frozen procedure exceeds the majority baseline by a chosen clinically or practically relevant margin on independent later-clinic patients, while achieving a specified minority-class sensitivity; define metric, sample and uncertainty criterion before gathering outcomes. This exercise is a synthetic method audit, not medical advice or validation of a health product.

Accept any correct alternative derivation with equivalent stated conventions. A numerical check supports a calculation; it does not prove a general claim. Diagnose the first substantive gap, and do not equate help-assisted work with independent demonstration.
