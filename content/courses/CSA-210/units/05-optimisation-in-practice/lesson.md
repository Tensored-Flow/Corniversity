## Outline: Optimisation in practice: conditioning, momentum, mini-batches and adaptive methods

This unit is **outlined, not yet prepared**. It will be written from W1D4 Tutorial 1 (Optimization techniques) of the Neuromatch Deep Learning ebook (content CC BY 4.0), cited by tutorial page and section.

**Scope.** The unit picks up where unit 2's quadratic analysis stopped. Poor conditioning: why the condition number sets the speed of plain gradient descent and how momentum (heavy ball) changes the recursion into a second-order one whose contraction rate depends on $\sqrt\kappa$ rather than $\kappa$; the tutorial's "Poor conditioning" and "Momentum vs. GD" demonstrations. Non-convexity: why overparameterisation makes the landscape easier in practice, and what can and cannot be proved. Mini-batches: the stochastic gradient as an unbiased but noisy estimate, the cost–variance trade-off in batch size, and the learning rate's interaction with noise. Adaptive methods: RMSprop and Adam as per-coordinate step sizes, with their known failure modes. The unit ends with the department convention that an optimiser's hyperparameters, schedule and seed policy are part of the reported result.

**What preparation will add.** A full lesson with the momentum recursion analysed on a quadratic (the learner should derive the $\sqrt\kappa$ rate), original exercises (the heavy-ball eigen-analysis; the variance of a mini-batch gradient as a function of batch size; a comparison protocol that does not confuse optimiser differences with seed noise), instructor reference reasoning, an exit check and the unit's common misconceptions ("Adam always beats SGD"). Until then the reading above is accurate and the tutor can discuss the material, but there is no assessed work for this unit.
