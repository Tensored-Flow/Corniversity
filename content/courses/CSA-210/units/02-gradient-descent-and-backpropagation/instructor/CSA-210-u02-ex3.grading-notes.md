## Grading notes (instructor only)

This is an explain-back; grade the ideas, not the prose, and keep feedback under 120 words.

- Must contain: the adjoint of a node is assembled from its children by the chain rule (this is where the chain rule enters, once per edge); the sweep runs from the single scalar loss in reverse topological order and touches each edge once, so its cost is a constant multiple of the forward pass and it yields every parameter's derivative together.
- Forward mode: one seeded direction per sweep; for a scalar loss in $N$ parameters, $N$ sweeps. A learner who says "forward mode is slower" without the per-direction reason is partially met.
- Acceptable cases for forward mode: few inputs and many outputs (tall Jacobians), directional derivatives or Jacobian–vector products, Hessian–vector products via forward-over-reverse. Any one with its reason counts.
- Conceptual errors worth a precise comment: describing backprop as symbolic differentiation of the whole expression; claiming it needs the loss to be convex or everywhere differentiable; saying autograd "stores the gradient of every function" rather than the local derivative of each elementary operation.
