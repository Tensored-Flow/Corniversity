## Reference reasoning (instructor only)

**1. Threshold.** $\nabla L(w)=Aw$, so $w^{(1)}_{t+1}=(1-\eta)w^{(1)}_t$ and $w^{(2)}_{t+1}=(1-10\eta)w^{(2)}_t$; hence $w^{(1)}_t=(1-\eta)^tw^{(1)}_0$ and $w^{(2)}_t=(1-10\eta)^tw^{(2)}_0$.

*If.* For $0<\eta<0.2$: $|1-\eta|<1$ (since $0<\eta<2$) and $|1-10\eta|<1$ (since $0<\eta<0.2$), so both coordinates tend to $0$ for every $w_0$.

*Only if.* If $\eta\le0$, $|1-\eta|\ge1$ and $w_0=(1,0)$ does not converge to $0$ (it is constant or grows). If $\eta\ge0.2$, $|1-10\eta|\ge1$: at $\eta=0.2$, $w_0=(0,1)$ gives $w_t=(0,(-1)^t)$, which oscillates; for $\eta>0.2$ it diverges. So convergence for every $w_0$ holds exactly when $0<\eta<0.2=2/\lambda_{\max}$.

**2. Rates.** Worst case over $w_0$ gives $\rho(\eta)=\max(|1-\eta|,|1-10\eta|)$. $\rho(0.1)=\max(0.9,0)=0.9$; $\rho(0.18)=\max(0.82,0.8)=0.82$. The two moduli are $1-\eta$ (decreasing) and $10\eta-1$ (increasing for $\eta>0.1$); their maximum is minimised where they cross: $1-\eta=10\eta-1\Rightarrow\eta^*=2/11\approx0.1818$, with $\rho^*=9/11\approx0.818=(\kappa-1)/(\kappa+1)$ for $\kappa=10$. Steps for a factor $10^3$: $\lceil\ln10^3/\ln(1/0.9)\rceil=\lceil6.908/0.1054\rceil=66$ at $\eta=0.1$; $\lceil6.908/\ln(11/9)\rceil=\lceil6.908/0.2007\rceil=35$ at $\eta^*$.

**3. Descent lemma.** For $\beta$-Lipschitz $\nabla L$ and any $w,v$:
$$L(v)-L(w)=\int_0^1\nabla L(w+s(v-w))^\top(v-w)\,ds=\nabla L(w)^\top(v-w)+\int_0^1\big(\nabla L(w+s(v-w))-\nabla L(w)\big)^\top(v-w)\,ds,$$
and the last integrand is at most $\beta s\|v-w\|^2$ by Cauchy–Schwarz and the Lipschitz bound, so $L(v)\le L(w)+\nabla L(w)^\top(v-w)+\tfrac\beta2\|v-w\|^2$. With $v=w-\eta\nabla L(w)$: $\nabla L(w)^\top(v-w)=-\eta\|\nabla L(w)\|^2$ and $\tfrac\beta2\|v-w\|^2=\tfrac{\beta\eta^2}2\|\nabla L(w)\|^2$, giving $L(v)\le L(w)-\eta(1-\beta\eta/2)\|\nabla L(w)\|^2$. For $L=\tfrac12w^\top Aw$, $\nabla L(w)-\nabla L(v)=A(w-v)$ and $\|A(w-v)\|\le\|A\|_{\mathrm{op}}\|w-v\|=10\|w-v\|$ with equality along $e_2$, so $\beta=10$. The lemma's safe step is $\eta\le1/\beta=0.1$, which guarantees descent but is half the exact threshold $0.2$; the lemma trades sharpness for needing only a Lipschitz constant rather than the spectrum.

**4. Conditioning.** The largest eigenvalue caps the step ($\eta<2/\lambda_{\max}$) while the smallest sets how slowly the flattest direction shrinks ($1-\eta\lambda_{\min}$); their ratio $\kappa$ therefore dictates the number of steps. A better method would move with a direction-dependent step (preconditioning, adaptive methods) or accumulate velocity along consistently signed gradients while damping oscillation (momentum), both treated in unit 5.

**Alternative valid approaches.** Spectral-radius argument with $\|(I-\eta A)^t\|$ in the operator norm; proving the lemma via Taylor's theorem with the integral remainder; using $\eta^*$ from the general formula $2/(\lambda_1+\lambda_d)$ without re-deriving the crossing (acceptable if cited from the lesson and checked).

**Common gaps.** Proving only the "if" direction; asserting divergence at $\eta=0.2$ (it oscillates without decaying, which is still non-convergence); taking $\beta=1$ or $\beta=11$; forgetting the factor $\tfrac12$ in the quadratic bound; confusing the number of steps with $1/\rho$.
