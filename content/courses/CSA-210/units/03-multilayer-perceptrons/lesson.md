## Outline: Multilayer perceptrons: approximation, depth and width

This unit is **outlined, not yet prepared**. It will be written from W1D3 of the Neuromatch Deep Learning ebook (Tutorial 1: Biological vs. Artificial Neural Networks; Tutorial 2: Deep MLPs; content CC BY 4.0), cited by tutorial page and section.

**Scope.** An MLP as a composition of affine maps and pointwise nonlinearities; the universal approximation theorem stated precisely, with a constructive sketch of how ReLU units build a piecewise-linear approximant of a continuous function on a compact set, and the two things the theorem does not say (how many units, and whether gradient descent finds them); the cross-entropy loss derived as negative log-likelihood of a softmax model and why it pairs with the logits rather than with probabilities; the tutorial's wide-versus-deep comparison at a fixed parameter count and the expressivity argument behind it; the role of `.train()` and `.eval()`; initialisation (Xavier scaling and its dependence on the transfer function) from the bonus section.

**What preparation will add.** A full lesson with definitions and the approximation statement proved in one dimension; original exercises (a parameter count and shape calculation; a derivation of the gradient of cross-entropy with softmax, $\partial L/\partial z=p-y$; a wide-versus-deep argument on a piecewise-linear target), instructor reference reasoning, an exit check and the unit's common misconceptions ("universal approximation means the network will learn any function"). Until then the reading above is accurate and the tutor can discuss the material, but there is no assessed work for this unit.
