# Synthetic forecast audit — complete reference reasoning

The prediction occurs at the start of record week t. Sensor summaries were stipulated available at that cutoff; the retrospectively completed ticket flag was not. That timing makes the ticket feature inadmissible. The supplied story does not establish that it is a near-copy of the next-week label or that a ticket-only predictor would achieve any particular accuracy.

Random row splitting mixes machines and time periods. That does not match the claimed future/new-machine task and can exploit persistent entity information or temporal dependence. For a nonfinal weekly record, the following record has roughly an 80% chance of entering training under the stated random split, conditional details aside. Its presence is not proof that the fitted model can retrieve its label, that sensors identify the failure perfectly, or that adjacent rows are literal duplicates. Those stronger claims need actual features, joint outcomes and an access mechanism.

Fitting imputation/scaling on all rows uses evaluation inputs. The 30-way test-based choice also makes the named test set part of selection. Neither effect has a known numerical inflation from the supplied information: candidate-error dependence, class-conditional errors, ticket reliability, distribution shift and dataset structure matter. A maximum of many noisy scores may be optimistic, but the exact bias cannot be obtained by assuming independent equal-accuracy candidates without evidence.

The majority baseline is 1 − 0.06 = 94%. The selected 97.4% is 3.4 percentage points higher on this contaminated selection sample, not a clean estimate of future new-machine risk. It still describes the stated selected run. Confusion matrices, sensitivity, precision and operating-threshold choices are needed to understand the 6% positive class. No unique ordering of leak sizes is justified. Accept conditional reasoning and a proposal to rerun controlled ablations with eligible features and independent evaluation; do not penalise a supported ranking merely for placing preprocessing above another risk.

One corrected protocol is:

1. Define prediction time as the start of week t and target as failure in week t+1.
2. Use machines 1–150, record weeks 1–10, for training; their labels finish by the end of week 11.
3. Use machines 151–200, weeks 13–15, for validation; outcomes finish by the end of week 16.
4. Use machines 201–250, weeks 18–20, for final evaluation after their outcome windows finish. This tests later periods on unseen machines; other predeclared designs can answer different questions.
5. Admit only features available at the cutoff; remove the retrospective ticket flag or replace it with a genuinely earlier snapshot.
6. Fit transforms and model weights only on training data; choose architecture, threshold and checkpoint on validation.
7. Freeze the whole procedure before final evaluation; do not respond to final outcomes with unreported retuning.
8. Report all predeclared seeds, majority and simple-model baselines, class-specific outcomes and uncertainty at an appropriate independent machine/time level.

Information contract: observed inputs are cutoff-available sensor histories; the target is following-week failure; training uses only earlier completed outcome windows on designated training machines; inference uses the allowed history of the queried machine; the entity is a machine and final entities are unseen; the task is the same forecast label; later record/outcome periods are explicitly evaluated; retrospective ticket values and future outcomes are unavailable inputs; the simplest baseline predicts no failure; the design does not establish other-site performance, arbitrary longer horizons or causal maintenance effects.

Accept an alternative split that explicitly states its estimand, temporal availability and entity independence. A time-only split may legitimately test future performance on known machines if that narrower claim is stated. Automatic mention/number checks remain advisory; grade the mechanism and limits of each claim.
