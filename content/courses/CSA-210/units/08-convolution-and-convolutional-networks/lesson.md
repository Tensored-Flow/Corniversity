## Outline: Convolution as a linear operator; convolutional networks

This unit is **outlined, not yet prepared**. It will be written from W2D2 Tutorial 1 (Introduction to CNNs) of the Neuromatch Deep Learning ebook (content CC BY 4.0), cited by tutorial page and section.

**Scope.** Convolution treated as what it is: a linear operator on signals that commutes with translation. The unit defines discrete convolution and cross-correlation (and notes that deep learning libraries implement the latter under the former's name, as the tutorial's "Definitional Note" flags), proves linearity and translation equivariance, derives the output-size formula from kernel size, padding and stride, and shows a convolutional layer as a sparse, weight-shared matrix, which gives the parameter-count comparison with a fully connected layer in the tutorial's Section 4.1. Multiple filters, ReLU after convolution, pooling as a translation-tolerant subsampling, and the tutorial's edge-detection kernels connect the operator to what the network computes. Transfer learning from a pretrained ResNet closes the unit with an evidence question: what does fine-tuning performance show about the pretrained features?

**What preparation will add.** A full lesson, original exercises (output-size and parameter-count calculations; a proof of equivariance and a counterexample showing pooling breaks exact equivariance; a hand convolution with an edge-detecting kernel), instructor reference reasoning, an exit check and the unit's common misconceptions ("pooling gives translation invariance", which is only approximate).
